# Adyen × F1: pitch script for Peter

How to use this: the words under each slide are written to be spoken. Say them in your own rhythm; you do not need to read them word for word. Lines in *italics* are cues, not things to say. Every number in the script is on the slide behind you, so you can point to it.

The thread to hold all the way through: **F1 is not a transaction-volume play. It is a complete marketing and commercial platform. Today's ask is buy-in to F1. Max and Alpine are the first doors in, and the rest of the grid follows.**

**Timing.** The full script runs about 35 minutes. For a 20-minute slot, use the ★ slides and pass through the others in one line, or skip them.

## 6 · Why Formula 1

## 7 · F1 is growing in audience and commercial value ★

Two numbers tell you where F1 is heading.

The audience: 625.3 million followers in 2026, up 30.8% since 2022. That's 147 million more fans in four years.

And brands are voting with their money. The value of new sponsorship deals rose 165% between 2020 and 2025. The average new deal went from $2.21 million to $5.78 million a year. Companies are paying more to be in F1 because it's working for them.

## 8 · Half of the world's most valuable brands are already in F1 ★

This is the slide that matters most for an enterprise business like Adyen.

50% of the world's most valuable brands are involved in Formula 1. The public brands in the F1 ecosystem have combined revenue of $10 trillion. That's bigger than the GDP of Germany, Japan, India, the UK or France.

On the right is who's in the room: Visa, American Express, Salesforce, SAP, Oracle, Cisco, PepsiCo, Nestlé, LVMH, and five of the Magnificent Seven. These are the companies Adyen wants as clients and partners, and F1 is a place where they spend time together.

Kantar's Strong Brands Portfolio grew 504% between 2006 and 2026, against 451% for the S&P 500 and 234% for the MSCI World. The green boxes on the left are the brands in Kantar's Top 100 that are involved in F1.

## 10 · F1 is the strongest sport for every audience Adyen sells to ★

Now the audience.

We compared five sports on five audiences that matter to Adyen. F1 comes first on all five. The two standouts: early tech adopters at 237.9, which is 31% above the next-best sport, and senior decision-makers at 166.6.

## Questions Peter might ask, and how to answer

**"What's the transaction volume? Is it worth it on payments alone?"**
Be honest. Payment volume through a team or Verstappen Travel is real, but it isn't the headline. The value is the whole platform: brand, audience, influence, hospitality, network and proof. Go back to slide 4, then slide 28.

**"Why not Mercedes or McLaren?"**
Mercedes has Stripe, and McLaren has Airwallex. Both are direct competitors (slide 22).

**"What else is out there?" / "What does the grid look like?"**
Use the back-pocket answer and show slide 40 (Ferrari).

**"Max or Alpine?"**
They do different jobs. Max brings reach and influence plus a business ecosystem Adyen can power. His rights are non-exclusive and his fee isn't quoted yet. Alpine is the full team platform: exclusive category, ecommerce integration, 56 Paddock Club tickets a year, and a 42-partner network, at £10 million in 2027. The plan allows for either or both. The decision today is F1 itself.

**"Does Max's deal include F1 hospitality?"**
His package includes a garage tour, a private track day and paddock meet-and-greets. It excludes Red Bull assets and hospitality. We've proposed adding Paddock Club hospitality, and that's still to be confirmed.

**"How do we measure it?"**
Pipeline sourced, deals accelerated, priority accounts retained and grown, payment volume processed, and case studies published (slide 28).

**"Are these numbers forecasts?"**
No. They're proof points from named sources: partners already in F1 (HPE, Komatsu, Businessolver), the rights holders' proposals, and Adyen's own case study.
