# Adyen × F1: pitch script for Peter (Chairman deck 8)

How to use this: the words under each slide are written to be spoken. Say them in your own rhythm; you do not need to read them word for word. Lines in *italics* are cues, not things to say. Every number in the script is on a slide or in the speaker notes, so you can point to it.

The thread to hold all the way through: **brands aren't in F1 for exposure. They're there to show their technology working under pressure, to be in the room with the right people, and to hit several goals with one partnership. Our role is to find Adyen the right fit. Today's ask is buy-in to F1 and to one centralised programme; the marketing rights in the proposals are the basis for a conversation, not the decision.**

**Timing.** The full script runs about 30 minutes. For a 15-minute slot, use the ★ slides and pass through the others in one line. Slides 14 and 24 are hidden: skip them.

## 1 · Opening ★

Peter, thank you for the time.

Let me start with why we're here. As an agency, our role is to support you: to understand what Adyen needs and to find the right fit in Formula 1. We're not here to sell you a package.

So what are brands actually in F1 for? It isn't exposure. The logo on the car is the least interesting part. The best partners are there for two things.

First, to show their technology working in real time, under pressure. F1 is one of the most demanding operating environments in sport, and every race weekend is a live test in front of the world.

Second, to rub shoulders with the right people. The decision-makers they want as clients are in the paddock, and the audience they want to reach is watching.

And different brands are in it for different reasons. Some use it to build talent. Some to grow the brand. Some to build pipeline. The point of F1 is that one partnership can do all of that at once. Many birds, one stone.

*Pause.*

Now, commercial terms matter to you, and the rights holders have built their proposals around them. But I'd ask you not to focus on the marketing rights today. They're the basis for a conversation, not the decision.

What matters more is how you'd use F1. You've told us Adyen already does hospitality at races, market by market, independently. Bring that into one centralised programme and you get economies of scale, one standard for how Adyen hosts, and one organisation acting together instead of every country doing its own thing.

And timing matters, because F1 is scarce. There are 11 teams, 22 drivers and 24 races. Partners don't overlap: each one owns its category, so the brands around you in the paddock aren't your rivals. They're people you can do business with, and they're there to engage with each other as much as with the fans.

That's also why teams are commercialising so hard. Teams run as businesses now. Under the budget cap they can't simply outspend each other on the car, so commercial growth is where they build value, and partnerships are the engine. McLaren is the clearest example: 57 partners, more than any other team, built on the back of winning on track.

*Cue: the budget cap is a public F1 regulation, in place since 2021; it isn't from our data pack, so keep it qualitative.*

So that's the frame. Let me take you into the specifics.

The playbook works. Adyen's version goes further: it runs the fan-facing commerce, live at the races, and uses F1 to host and win enterprise clients.

## Questions Peter might ask, and how to answer

**"What's the transaction volume? Is it worth it on payments alone?"**
Be honest. Payment volume through a team or Verstappen Travel is real, but it isn't the headline. Brands are in F1 to show their technology under pressure, meet the right people and hit several goals with one partnership. Go back to slide 4, then slide 12.

**"Aren't we just following Stripe and Airwallex?"**
No. They're in different seats: Stripe partners Mercedes, Airwallex runs McLaren's supplier payments behind the scenes. Adyen's seat is the fan-facing commerce, live at races (slides 3 and 15).

**"Why not Mercedes or McLaren?"**
Mercedes has Stripe and McLaren has Airwallex, both direct competitors (slide 3).

**"What exactly do we get for the money?"**
The rights in the proposals are on slides 22 and 31, and they're the basis for a conversation, not the decision. The first question is whether F1 is the right platform; then we shape the rights around how Adyen wants to use it.

**"We already do hospitality at races. Why change?"**
Because today it's market by market. One centralised programme gives you economies of scale, one standard for how Adyen hosts, and it's what your own brief asks for: one global programme across an initial eight to ten races (slide 2).

**"What does the rest of the grid look like?"**
Three routes are in front of you: Max, Alpine and Ferrari (slide 19). Mercedes and McLaren already have Adyen competitors. Partner data for all eleven teams is on slide 13. Once Adyen commits to F1, we expand from a proven start.

**"Max, Alpine or Ferrari?"**
They do different jobs. Max brings reach and influence plus a business ecosystem Adyen can power, and hospitality can be bundled in. His rights are non-exclusive, at €5 to 8 million a year depending on the rights. Alpine is the full team platform: exclusive category, ecommerce integration, 56 Paddock Club tickets a year and 49 team partners, at £10 million in 2027. Ferrari has 42 partners and the most fans in financial services, but no proposal yet. The decision today is F1 itself.

**"Does Max's deal include F1 hospitality?"**
His package includes a garage tour, a private track day and paddock meet-and-greets. It excludes Red Bull assets and F1 hospitality. That's why we'd bundle hospitality with the Max route (slides 23 and 25 to 28), built bespoke from your feedback.

**"What would hospitality cost?"**
It depends on the races and the format. At Miami (slide 25), packages start at $5,250 a person for the Red Bull Energy Station on race day and go up to $250,000 for a private villa for 20 guests. We'd price a programme once we know the races and guest numbers.

**"Why are teams so keen to sign partners?"**
Teams run as businesses now. Under the budget cap they can't simply outspend each other on the car, so commercial growth is where they build value. McLaren has the most partners on the grid: 57.

**"How do we measure it?"**
Pipeline sourced, deals accelerated, priority accounts retained and grown, payment volume processed, and case studies published.

**"Are these numbers forecasts?"**
No. They're proof points from named sources: partners already in F1 (HPE, Komatsu, Businessolver, Airwallex), the rights holders' proposals, and SPORTFIVE's partner data.
