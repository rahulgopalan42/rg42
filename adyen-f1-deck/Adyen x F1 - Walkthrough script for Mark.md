# Adyen × F1: Monday walkthrough with Mark (Chairman deck 9)

**Who it's for.** You, walking Mark through the deck before he takes it to Peter. Bjorn is in the room for questions, the Airwallex story and Ferrari. Tom (hospitality) isn't. If it's confirmed, a 30-minute virtual call with Ferrari follows.

**The goal.** Send Mark into Peter's office with an objective business case, not a passion play. Eleri's line: Peter doesn't need persuading to love F1. He needs a business case he can defend.

**The spine.** Everything below comes back to three sentences:
1. F1 isn't a payments-volume play. If volume were the goal, football is cheaper and easier to buy.
2. F1 is white space and always on: 2 of the 32 sports deals Adyen's rivals hold, and 10 racing months a year across five continents.
3. One partnership does several jobs: a live case study, a global hospitality programme that pays for itself, and a partner network full of prospects.

**Time plan (45 minutes).** About 30 minutes of us, 15 of Mark. If time runs short, protect parts 1, 3 and 5.

**How to read it.** **Land it** is the headline the slide must leave behind. **Say** is how you'd put it. **Insight** is the point that adds value. **Ask Mark** is where you stop talking. Lines in *italics* are cues.

## Part 1 · Why F1, as a business case (12 min)

## 2 · Adyen's brief ★

**Land it:** F1 hits five of Adyen's priorities with one partnership, and the last one is already in your brief.

**Say:** This is your team's brief, in five boxes. Payment integration first: Adyen running inside a team's business, as a working product. Then a global brand, client relationships, and commercial growth.

Look at the fifth box. One global programme, uniting local hosting across eight to ten races. You already host at races, country by country. That box is the economic case for doing it centrally: one budget, one standard, more leverage.

**Insight:** The headline on this slide still says "financial efficiencies". Eleri's point is that's not the story Peter needs. The story is the B2B one: every touchpoint where Adyen meets a client, a prospect or a partner.

**Ask Mark:** Of these five, which one does Peter care about most personally? Which one would make him lean in?

## 3 · Competitive landscape ★

**Land it:** Your rivals crowd football and the US leagues. F1 is still open.

**Say:** Each dot is one sports deal held by a payments company. Seven companies, 32 deals. Football has 11, US leagues 12. F1 has two: Stripe with Mercedes and Airwallex with McLaren.

**Insight:** Two things make F1 different. First, white space: almost nobody in payments is there yet. Second, it's always on. Football and US leagues run on matchdays and seasons, so concession and merchandise volume comes in bursts. F1 runs for ten months a year, March to December, across five continents. That's a story Adyen can tell every week, not every other weekend.

*Cue: Eleri noted Adyen itself is active only in football. If Mark confirms it, ask the next question.*

**Ask Mark:** Adyen's own sports work is in football. What has it delivered for you, and what hasn't it? That tells us what F1 needs to do differently.

## 4 · Why F1, at a glance ★

**Land it:** One platform puts Adyen in front of its buyers, in its markets, all year.

**Say:** Six facts. F1 is the number one sport, of the five we compared, for all five audiences Adyen sells to. 625 million followers, up 31% in four years. Half the world's most valuable brands are involved. 457 million fans in Adyen's own office markets. 24 race weekends. And 371 partners across the eleven teams.

**Insight:** The number to remember for Peter isn't the audience, it's the network. 125 new F1 sponsorship deals were signed in 2025, up 29%. More brands are joining every year, and each new one is a potential client sitting in the same paddock.

## 5 · Executive summary: three routes

**Land it:** Two proposals on the table, one option in reserve. Today's decision is F1, not a contract.

**Say:** Max: reach, influence and a business Adyen can power. Alpine: the full team platform, exclusive category, with Adyen running the team's online store. Ferrari: the biggest pool of finance-sector fans, no proposal yet. We'll come back to each one. For now, the point for Peter is that there's more than one way in.

**Ask Mark:** If you had to walk into Peter's office with one route, which would it be today, and why?

*Don't debate it yet. Note the answer and come back to it in part 4.*

## 6 · The audience ★

**Insight:** That's both sides of Adyen's business in one audience: the enterprise buyer and the early adopter. Few sports give you both.

**Ask Mark:** Who is Adyen's hardest buyer to reach today: the CFO, the CTO, or the head of ecommerce?

## Questions to have ready

**"What's the payment volume?"**
Honest answer: modest, and it isn't the headline. Max's ticketing volumes are on request. The value is the case study, the hosting and the network.

**"Why not just do more football?"**
For volume, football is cheaper and more accessible. But it's crowded with Adyen's rivals, seasonal, and doesn't put Adyen in a room with this calibre of buyer every weekend for ten months.

**"Aren't we following Stripe and Airwallex?"**
No: they're behind the scenes or on a logo. Adyen's seat is the commerce fans see, live at the races.

**"What does it cost?"**
Max: €5–8M a year depending on rights (as drafted, about €8M, roughly €9M with hospitality; about €5M without GT3 branding). Alpine: £10M in 2027, rising 5% a year, with 56 Paddock Club tickets included.

**"What do we get back?"**
Pipeline from hosting (proof points: Komatsu, HPE, Businessolver; keep them verbal), a case study sales can use, introductions to the team's partners, and brand lift in the markets Adyen is growing in.

**"What if Peter likes F1 but not these routes?"**
Then we look at others, Red Bull among them. The first decision is F1.

**"Can you answer X?"**
If it's a rights-holder question, we'll get the answer to you before you see Peter.
