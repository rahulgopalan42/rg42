Mandatory Multi-Source Research and Independent Company Discovery
You currently have access to two company research connectors:

* Clay
* Crunchbase

I want you to maximise the use of both connectors while simultaneously conducting extensive independent research using your own web research capabilities.
Do not limit company discovery to the results available through Clay and Crunchbase. These databases should complement your independent research, not replace it.

1. Conduct three parallel discovery exercises

Research Stream A – Clay
Use all available company discovery, filtering and enrichment capabilities to identify potentially qualifying non-public companies.
Run independent searches based on revenue, employee count, industry, geographic presence, ownership structure and other available indicators.

Research Stream B – Crunchbase
Use Crunchbase to identify venture-backed companies, growth-stage businesses, privately held technology companies, recently funded businesses and companies with significant valuations.
Apply the agreed OR-based financial qualification criteria.
If usage allowances or technical restrictions temporarily prevent access to Crunchbase, continue with the other research streams rather than stopping the overall exercise.

Research Stream C – Independent Web Research
Conduct comprehensive independent research beyond Clay and Crunchbase.
Actively identify companies that may not be listed in either database, particularly established private businesses, family-owned companies, bootstrapped businesses, industrial manufacturers, subsidiaries and state-backed organisations.
Use every relevant, credible and accessible research source, including:

* Official company websites and corporate press releases.
* National company registries and statutory financial filings.
* Largest private company rankings by country.
* Industry-specific company rankings.
* Global family-owned business rankings.
* Trade associations and membership directories.
* Industry exhibitions and conference exhibitor lists.
* Government export and investment promotion directories.
* Private equity portfolio company directories.
* Sovereign wealth fund portfolio directories.
* Corporate annual reports and parent-company disclosures.
* Industry publications and specialist trade media.
* Financial and business news publications.
* Manufacturer, supplier and distributor directories.
* Regional business publications.
* LinkedIn company pages and corporate recruitment websites.
* Acquisition and merger announcements.
* Industry market research reports.
* Sports sponsorship announcements.
* Competitor and industry ecosystem research.

Search in relevant local languages where appropriate, particularly when researching companies outside English-speaking markets.
Do not rely exclusively on English-language sources, internationally recognised brands or companies with extensive media coverage.

2. Combine and deduplicate all research streams

Merge the companies discovered through Clay, Crunchbase and independent web research into one central discovery register.
Before conducting detailed company-level research, deduplicate every candidate against:

* The master spreadsheet I previously shared, including all its tabs.
* All previously delivered prospect lists.
* All previous research batches.
* Companies discovered through the other research streams.

I do not want any repetition of brands already present in my master database.
Deduplicate using company names, website domains, trading names, historical names and ownership relationships.

3. Research beyond the obvious companies

Do not stop after identifying the largest or most visible companies in each industry.
Investigate the entire relevant industry ecosystem, including manufacturers, suppliers, service providers, technology providers, distributors and commercially independent subsidiaries.
For every sector, systematically research its relevant subsectors and geographic markets.
Use independent web research to identify blind spots in the Clay and Crunchbase datasets.
Do not treat a company being absent from either database as a reason for exclusion.

4. Maximise research depth and coverage

Research one sector at a time, in depth, rather than conducting a superficial search across all sectors simultaneously.
Use multiple independent discovery sources for each subsector and geographical market.
Where possible, extract company names from entire accessible rankings, directories and industry lists rather than researching only the first few results.
Do not impose arbitrary company quotas or stop after reaching a predetermined number of brands.
Continue until additional independent sources consistently generate relatively few genuinely new qualifying companies.
Record any sources, subsectors or geographic markets that remain unresearched.

5. Track the contribution of each research source

For every newly identified company, record whether it was discovered through:

* Clay.
* Crunchbase.
* Independent web research.
* Multiple sources.

Provide a research coverage summary showing:

* Total companies discovered through each research stream.
* Unique companies contributed by each source.
* Companies discovered exclusively through independent web research.
* Duplicates removed.
* Companies already present in the master spreadsheet.
* Genuinely new financially qualified prospects.
* Companies requiring further financial verification.
* Remaining research gaps.

This will help establish which research sources are contributing the most valuable additional prospects.

6. Preserve all previously agreed requirements

Continue applying the latest agreed methodology, including:

* Non-public companies only.
* OR-based financial qualification criteria.
* Alternative qualification routes for companies with undisclosed financials.
* Sector-specific financial assessment.
* Buying signals as prioritisation criteria, not mandatory eligibility requirements.
* Mandatory parent-company identification for subsidiaries.
* Existing Formula 1 sponsorship and geographic exclusions.
* Financial confidence classifications.
* Estimated partnership investment potential.
* Weighted outreach scoring.
* Rankings from highest to lowest outreach priority within every sector.
* Consolidated cross-sector rankings.
* Exactly the same spreadsheet format historically provided.
* Strict exclusion of brands already included in the master spreadsheet or previously delivered prospect lists.

Do not loosen the financial parameters simply to increase the number of results.

7. Execution expectations

Complete the assessment of the 569 outstanding companies already identified, then continue with the seven sectors that have not yet produced results and the remaining under-researched sectors.
Work in structured batches, preserving all research progress and maintaining a central discovery register.
If a connector reaches its usage allowance, continue researching through other available sources.
If execution limits prevent completion within a single session, save the completed work and establish a precise continuation checkpoint.
Do not claim to have accessed sources that are unavailable or describe partially researched sectors as exhaustive.
The objective is to build the most comprehensive possible universe of genuinely new, commercially relevant non-public Formula 1 sponsorship prospects by combining the capabilities of Clay, Crunchbase and extensive independent research.
I want maximum credible market coverage, not simply the maximum number of companies that can be retrieved from two databases.
