Crunchbase stream: not run. The allowance on your account was used up before this batch (500,000 of 500,000 data tokens) and resets on 1 Dec 2026. Venture-backed and recently funded companies are therefore the thinnest part of this batch.
Clay stream: five search sets were run (industry by revenue bracket for 68 industries; headquarters country for 30 countries; headcount and ownership type), 188 result pages in all. Almost every search still had more results when its page limit was reached, so Clay was sampled, not exhausted. Only the search was used; no paid enrichment was run.
Clay data quality: Clay marks many listed companies and local arms of multinationals as privately held (for example Danone, BNP Paribas, Inditex), and its revenue is a modelled bracket. A Clay name is ranked only after a web check; in the follow-up research roughly half to two thirds of the largest Clay and ranking names turned out to be master aliases, listed companies or local entities and were left out.
The 569 companies carried over from batch 1: 527 are now assessed and ranked, 5 were left out (in administration, absorbed, or the parent is on your master), and 37 could not be assessed because their sites block automated requests; those 37 are named in the Research Record.
Research queue: the Discovery Register lists every new name that nobody has researched yet ("New: not yet researched"). 21 follow-up files of 80 names were researched in this batch; the rest is the starting point for batch 3, already ordered and balanced across sectors.
Sector depth was partial everywhere. Each sector run researched 15 to 45 companies and left 75 to 360 discovered names unresearched. Sources that would not open included Automotive News, Waste360, PRovoke, Ad Age, Horizont, Lebensmittel Zeitung, the Forbes private-company list, HOTELS 225, Nilson and The Banker; several Wikipedia pages were rate-limited.
Regions reached least: the Gulf beyond the largest groups, Turkey, Japan, South Korea, China, India, South-East Asia, Africa and Latin America outside Brazil, Mexico and Argentina. Local-language rankings were opened for Switzerland, Spain, Poland, the Netherlands, Germany, Austria, Italy, Mexico and Australia only; France, Portugal, Belgium, the Nordics, Czechia, Hungary, Romania, Greece, Turkey, Ireland and the UK were not opened.
Sectors not given a depth run in this batch (batch 1 coverage only, plus what the follow-up files happened to include): software and AI, industrial machinery, investment firms, aerospace and defence, mining and metals, chemicals, professional services, insurance, utilities, packaging, staffing and real estate.
Listed-company check: the register does not know every listed company, so researchers checked listing on each company's own pages; a few names were left out as listed on the researcher's knowledge without a page, and say so in the Excluded tab.
Names suggested by researchers from sector knowledge, with no list page behind them, are in the Discovery Register with an empty "Where It Was Found"; none of them is ranked unless a page was opened.
Older figures: many qualifying figures are more than three years old (for example the 2022 Australian private-company ranking); each is flagged in its row and counts as Indicative, not Confirmed.
Current F1 deals: every name was checked against the roster, including Grand Prix-level partners added in batch 1, and the top-ranked names were searched individually.
