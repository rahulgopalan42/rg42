# MASTER PROMPT – Global Formula 1 Prospect Identification and Outreach Prioritisation: Non-Public Companies

## 1. Objective

I would like you to revise and enhance the existing methodology for identifying, qualifying and prioritising non-public companies that could potentially invest in Formula 1 partnerships.

The previous methodology may disproportionately favour venture-backed technology companies and businesses with publicly available financial information, while overlooking financially capable private companies across other industries.

The objective is to identify the most comprehensive, financially credible and commercially relevant universe of potential Formula 1 sponsors globally, focusing on companies that have not already been identified in the master document I have shared with you.

The guiding principle should be:

**Be broad in discovery, rigorous in financial qualification and selective in outreach prioritisation.**

## 2. MANDATORY: No duplication of existing brands

Before beginning any new company discovery, review the latest master spreadsheet and master document I have shared with you.

Treat these as the definitive exclusion database for all subsequent prospect identification.

I do not want to receive brands that are already included in the master spreadsheet.

This is a strict requirement, regardless of whether an existing company:

- Qualifies under the revised financial parameters.
- Has received additional funding.
- Has experienced significant revenue growth.
- Has demonstrated new buying signals.
- Has changed its priority classification.
- Has entered a new sector classification.
- Has changed its trading name.
- Has previously been classified as To Verify, High, Medium or Low priority.

If the company already exists in the master spreadsheet, do not include it in any newly delivered prospect list.

Check against all relevant tabs, including domestic companies, watchlists and previously identified companies.

Perform deduplication using:

- Company names.
- Alternative trading names.
- Website domains.
- Legal entity names.
- Historical company names.
- Brand aliases.
- Acquisitions and rebranding.
- Parent-company and subsidiary relationships.

Do not treat minor spelling variations, abbreviations or differences in legal entity suffixes as separate companies.

A genuinely independent subsidiary or operating brand may still be eligible if that specific brand is not already present in the master spreadsheet and has its own commercial identity and a credible independent partnership proposition.

However, do not use subsidiaries, regional entities or alternative trading names as a way of artificially duplicating existing prospects.

Also deduplicate all newly identified companies against every previous research batch.

Maintain an ongoing exclusion register so that a company identified in one sector is not repeated in another sector. Assign it to its most relevant primary sector and record secondary sector classifications where appropriate.

Previously rejected companies that were never included in the master spreadsheet may be reconsidered under the revised methodology.

You may analyse existing companies internally for methodology validation, benchmarking and backtesting, but do not reproduce them in the new prospect deliverables.

If you cannot access the master spreadsheet, explicitly flag this limitation. Do not claim that a list has been fully deduplicated without checking it against the master document.

**Every new company list must contain genuinely new prospects only.**

Present all newly identified brands in exactly the same spreadsheet format that you have historically provided me with.

Use the latest existing spreadsheet as the reference template.

Maintain the existing:

Do not unnecessarily redesign the spreadsheet or introduce an entirely different format.

Where additional research fields, scoring calculations or supporting evidence are required, capture them in a separate supporting research tab.

Use existing columns wherever possible.

If dedicated Parent Company, Outreach Rank or Overall Score columns do not exist, introduce them while otherwise preserving the existing format.

The primary spreadsheet should remain practical, familiar and immediately usable for my day-to-day Formula 1 prospecting and outreach activities.

## 4. Mandatory sector-level outreach ranking

For every sector and subsector researched, rank all newly identified companies from the highest to the lowest outreach priority.

The first company in each sector should represent the strongest overall new outreach opportunity within that industry.

Do not simply provide an alphabetical list or rank companies according to revenue, valuation, funding or employee count.

The ranking should take into consideration:

- Financial capacity.
- Strategic Formula 1 relevance.
- Commercial growth ambitions.
- Buying signals and timing.
- Accessibility of senior decision-makers.
- Potential category conflicts.

For each company, provide:

- Numerical outreach rank within the sector.
- Overall prospect score out of 100.
- Priority classification.
- Concise, company-specific explanation of why it should be prioritised.
- Financial confidence classification.
- Parent company, where applicable.

In addition to individual sector rankings, provide a consolidated cross-sector ranking of all newly identified, qualified prospects.

Apply the same scoring methodology consistently across industries and research batches.

If additional companies are identified in subsequent batches, update the sector and consolidated rankings accordingly.

The biggest company should not automatically be considered the strongest prospect.

A fast-growing, profitable and relatively lean organisation with international ambitions and accessible senior leadership could represent a stronger outreach opportunity than a much larger multinational with a complex decision-making structure.

The objective is to identify which companies I should realistically approach first.

## 5. Scope of eligible companies

Include all commercially relevant non-public operating companies globally, regardless of industry, geography or ownership structure.

This includes:

- Venture-backed companies.
- Bootstrapped companies.
- Founder-owned businesses.
- Family-owned businesses.
- Private equity-backed companies.
- Privately held multinational companies.
- State-owned companies.
- Sovereign wealth fund-backed companies.
- Subsidiaries of publicly listed and privately held companies.
- Operating asset management businesses.
- Wealth management companies.
- Private equity and alternative investment management firms.
- Privately owned financial institutions.
- Joint ventures operating as independent commercial businesses.

A subsidiary of a publicly listed company remains eligible provided its own shares are not publicly traded.

Do not disproportionately focus on technology, AI, software, fintech or venture-backed companies.

## 6. Mandatory parent-company identification

For every subsidiary, explicitly identify:

- The operating company or brand being considered as the prospect.
- Its immediate parent company.
- Its ultimate parent company, where applicable.
- Whether the parent company is publicly listed or privately held.
- Relevant private equity or sovereign wealth fund ownership.
- Whether the subsidiary has independent commercial and marketing functions.
- Whether there is credible evidence of parent-company financial backing.

The subsidiary or individual operating brand must remain the primary prospect in the spreadsheet.

Use the existing ownership or parent-company column wherever possible. If no such column exists, introduce a dedicated Parent Company column.

Do not confuse minority investors with controlling parent companies.

Do not automatically consolidate commercially independent brands because they share a parent.

Do not assume that a subsidiary automatically has access to its parent's financial resources.

However, a subsidiary may qualify through documented parent-company support, a dedicated marketing budget or a credible commercial expansion mandate rather than exclusively through its standalone financial performance.

Where ownership cannot be verified, mark it as requiring verification.

## 7. Exclusion criteria

Exclude:

- Companies already included in the master spreadsheet or previous prospect lists.
- Companies whose own shares are publicly traded.
- Companies that have ceased operations or are undergoing liquidation.
- Passive investment funds without an independent operating business.
- Single-project special-purpose vehicles without an independent commercial or marketing function.
- Brands currently holding active Formula 1 championship, team or Grand Prix sponsorship agreements.
- Russian companies, in accordance with the existing geographic exclusion.
- Companies subject to applicable sanctions, regulatory restrictions or established material compliance concerns.

Do not automatically exclude an entire corporate group, conglomerate or private equity portfolio because one of its operating businesses sponsors Formula 1.

Assess individual operating brands based on their commercial autonomy, marketing budgets, ownership relationships and potential contractual category conflicts.

Operating asset managers, private equity firms and investment management businesses should remain eligible.

Where a potential sponsorship conflict cannot be confirmed, flag it for verification rather than treating it as an established conflict.

Do not automatically exclude companies because they:

- Do not disclose financial information.
- Have never raised venture capital.
- Have no recent funding activity.
- Have no confirmed buying signals.
- Operate predominantly domestically.
- Have limited media coverage.
- Are subsidiaries with limited standalone financial disclosures.

## 8. Separate discovery, qualification and prioritisation

Apply a three-stage methodology.

### Stage 1 – Discovery

Identify the broadest reasonable universe of potentially relevant companies using financial metrics, operational scale, ownership structures, financial backing, commercial activity and industry positioning.

### Stage 2 – Financial and commercial qualification

Assess whether the company has credible financial capacity, a relevant Formula 1 commercial proposition and a realistic potential partnership investment level.

### Stage 3 – Outreach prioritisation

Rank companies based on financial capacity, strategic Formula 1 fit, commercial motivation, buying signals, timing and decision-maker accessibility.

Do not confuse discovery eligibility with confirmed sponsorship affordability or willingness to invest.

## 9. Critical qualification logic: OR, not AND

The financial qualification criteria must operate on an OR basis, not an AND basis.

A company is not required to meet all financial thresholds simultaneously.

Meeting any one qualifying financial criterion is sufficient for initial discovery and further assessment, provided the mandatory eligibility and exclusion requirements are satisfied.

There should be three principal discovery routes:

**Route A – Standard financial qualification**

The company meets at least one of the standard financial thresholds.

**Route B – Exceptional financial qualification**

The company falls below a standard threshold but meets an exceptional threshold and demonstrates additional credible financial or commercial strength.

**Route C – Alternative financial qualification**

The company does not disclose sufficient financial information but demonstrates at least two independent and credible indicators of substantial commercial or operational scale.

Meeting any one of these three routes is sufficient for initial discovery.

Companies with compelling strategic relevance but insufficient evidence to meet any route should remain on a separate research watchlist.

Buying signals are not mandatory for inclusion. They should influence outreach priority.

## 10. Revised financial discovery thresholds

Apply the following provisional financial parameters:

| Financial indicator | Standard discovery threshold |
|---|---|
| Total equity funding | USD 100M+ |
| Annual revenue | USD 150M+ |
| Annual recurring revenue (ARR) | USD 50M+ |
| Annual EBITDA | USD 20M+ |
| Company valuation | USD 1B+ |
| Acquisition or transaction valuation | USD 1B+ |
| Assets under management (AUM) | USD 5B+ |
| Existing substantial sponsorship expenditure | Credible supporting evidence |
| Parent-company or state financial backing | Documented financial support and commercial mandate |

Only one of these criteria needs to be met for standard discovery.

These thresholds are indicators of potential capacity, not confirmation of available sponsorship budgets.

### Exceptional financial qualification

Consider companies below the standard thresholds where additional credible evidence supports inclusion:

| Financial indicator | Exceptional threshold |
|---|---|
| Total equity funding | USD 75M to below USD 100M |
| Annual revenue | USD 75M to below USD 150M |
| Company valuation | USD 500M to below USD 1B |
| Acquisition valuation | Below USD 1B where operating performance supports affordability |

Additional supporting evidence may include strong profitability, high margins, operating cash flow, recurring revenue, rapid commercial growth, established marketing expenditure, financial backing or significant sports sponsorships.

Do not exclude an attractive company simply because it marginally falls below a numerical threshold.

All exceptions must be documented.

Adapt financial assessment to the relevant company type rather than applying a single financial metric universally.

| Company type | Relevant financial indicators |
|---|---|
| Venture-backed | Equity funding, ARR, revenue, valuation, liquidity and cash runway |
| Bootstrapped | Revenue, ARR, EBITDA, profitability and cash flow |
| Family-owned | Revenue, profitability, operating scale and international presence |
| Private equity-backed | Revenue, EBITDA, acquisition value, growth strategy and financial backing |
| State-owned | Operating budget, financial backing and commercial mandate |

| Manufacturing | Revenue, profitability, production capacity and order books |

| Real estate | Operating income, portfolio scale, management fees and available capital |
| Hospitality | Operating revenue, property portfolio and profitability |
| Marketplaces | Net revenue, take rate, gross profit and liquidity |

Do not treat revenue, valuation, assets under management, gross merchandise value, transaction volume and available cash as interchangeable.

The methodology must not systematically disadvantage established private companies that have never raised venture capital.

## 12. Companies with undisclosed financial information

The absence of publicly available financial information must not automatically result in exclusion.

Where reliable financial information is unavailable, assess alternative indicators such as:

- Credible third-party revenue estimates.
- Employee count and workforce growth.
- International offices and operating markets.
- Manufacturing facilities and production capacity.
- Distributor and reseller networks.
- Retail footprint.
- Enterprise customers.
- Major commercial contracts.
- Order books and contracted revenues.
- Operating assets and infrastructure.
- Fleet size.
- International export activity.
- Industry rankings and market leadership.
- Existing sports sponsorships.
- Major advertising campaigns.
- Parent-company or institutional financial backing.

Use the following indicative benchmarks where relevant:

| Alternative indicator | Indicative benchmark |
|---|---|
| Estimated annual revenue | USD 150M+ |
| Employee count | 500+, with sector-specific exceptions |
| International operating presence | Three or more countries |
| Retail footprint | 100+ stores |
| Assets under management | USD 5B+ |
| Marketing expenditure | Evidence of multi-million-dollar annual investment |
| Sponsorship activity | Evidence of substantial paid commercial partnerships |

Require at least two independent, credible indicators of meaningful commercial scale when relying on this alternative discovery route.

Do not require every alternative indicator to be satisfied.

Do not count overlapping indicators as independent evidence when they describe essentially the same underlying fact.

Interpret these indicators within the context of the relevant industry.

A profitable enterprise software company with 250 employees could have greater sponsorship capacity than a low-margin industrial business with 3,000 employees.

Operational scale alone must not establish confirmed affordability.

## 13. Financial confidence classifications

Assign each company one of four classifications:

**Confirmed capacity:** Reliable, sufficiently recent financial evidence supports credible capacity for an identifiable partnership investment level. This does not mean an approved sponsorship budget exists.

**Indicative capacity:** Multiple credible indicators suggest potential financial capacity, but further verification is required.

**Unverified capacity:** Financial information is insufficient, although the company remains strategically relevant.

**Insufficient capacity:** Available evidence indicates that the company is unlikely to afford the relevant Formula 1 opportunity at present.

Do not confuse missing financial information with negative financial evidence.

Retain commercially attractive companies requiring additional verification in a dedicated research watchlist rather than permanently excluding them.

## 14. Correct treatment of venture funding

The previous methodology specified equity funding, while Crunchbase total funding figures may have included debt financing.

Where available, use Total Equity Funding Amount rather than Total Funding Amount.

Distinguish between:

- Total equity funding.
- Debt financing.
- Project financing.
- Credit facilities.
- Secondary share transactions.
- Acquisition consideration.
- Latest primary equity round amount and date.
- Valuation.
- Revenue and ARR.
- Profitability.
- Liquidity and cash runway.
- Purpose of the capital raise.

Do not treat debt, project financing or acquisition consideration as unrestricted capital available for marketing.

Assess whether capital was raised for customer acquisition, commercial expansion, international growth, infrastructure, manufacturing capacity, research and development or other capital-intensive activities.

Pre-revenue companies may remain eligible for discovery but require additional evidence of commercial readiness and realistic financial capacity before being prioritised.

Consider signs of financial distress, significant layoffs, restructuring and liquidity constraints.

## 15. Financial data reliability

Prioritise financial information from the most recent three financial years.

Older information may remain useful for discovery but should be flagged for verification.

Do not use significantly outdated figures as the sole basis for confirming current financial capacity.

Record:

- Financial reporting period.
- Publication date.
- Original reported figure and currency.
- USD equivalent and exchange-rate methodology.
- Source.
- Whether the figure is reported, estimated or inferred.
- Confidence level.

Where sources conflict, prioritise authoritative and recent evidence and document the discrepancy.

Do not fabricate financial figures or use zero values to represent unavailable information.

## 16. Expanded buying signals

Buying signals should influence outreach prioritisation, not determine basic eligibility.

A financially capable company with no confirmed buying signals should remain eligible.

Assess the following categories:

### Capital and corporate developments

- Equity funding within the past 24 months, with greater weight for the past 12 months.
- Growth capital and private equity investment.
- Credible IPO preparation.
- Acquisitions and mergers.
- Ownership changes associated with growth.
- Spin-offs and corporate transformation.

### Geographic expansion

- Entry into new markets.
- New international offices or regional headquarters.
- New manufacturing facilities.
- Expansion of distribution networks.
- Recruitment targeting new markets.
- Announced international growth strategies.

### Marketing and brand activity

- Appointment of a new CMO or senior marketing executive.
- Senior brand, marketing or partnership recruitment.
- Marketing team expansion.
- Marketing recruitment in new countries.
- Rebranding.
- Major advertising campaigns.
- Brand ambassador appointments.
- New sports and entertainment sponsorships.
- Increased marketing expenditure.
- Brand repositioning.

Do not require a minimum of five open marketing roles in every instance.

A single senior global marketing appointment could be more meaningful than several junior vacancies.

### Commercial expansion

- Entry into new customer segments.
- Major customer wins.
- Enterprise sales expansion.
- Product launches.
- New commercial divisions.
- Distribution agreements.
- Strategic technology partnerships.
- New B2B or B2C growth strategies.

### Strategic and competitive developments

- Direct competitors entering Formula 1.
- Competitors increasing sports sponsorship investment.
- Global brand-building ambitions.
- Documented leadership interest in motorsport.
- Customer hospitality initiatives.
- Transition between B2B and B2C business models.
- Premium market positioning.
- Increased focus on international brand awareness or enterprise customer acquisition.

Support confirmed buying signals with credible sources and dates.

Distinguish confirmed developments from inferred opportunities.

Do not count multiple articles reporting the same corporate event as separate buying signals.

A strong commercial development should carry more weight than several minor activities.

## 17. Reinstate sponsorship activity as a buying signal

Meaningful sports and entertainment sponsorship activity should be included within the revised methodology.

Distinguish between:

- Existing sponsorship as evidence of financial capacity.
- Recent sponsorship as evidence of marketing intent.
- Sponsorship expiry or renewal as a potential timing opportunity.

Recognise that a recently announced sponsorship may also mean the company's marketing budget is already committed elsewhere.

Avoid disproportionately inflating the overall score through double-counting.

Record the rights holder, sport, partnership designation, announcement date, duration and disclosed financial value where available.

Do not assume undisclosed sponsorship fees.

## 18. Strategic Formula 1 relevance

Assess every company's commercial relevance to Formula 1 against the following dimensions:

**Customer ecosystem alignment:** Are its target customers, distributors, resellers or commercial partners already present within the F1 ecosystem?

**B2B opportunities:** Could the company benefit from access to enterprise customers, senior decision-makers, multinational companies or strategic commercial partners?

**Technical integration:** Could its products or services have a genuine application within a Formula 1 team, its operations or wider partner ecosystem?

**Hospitality:** Could race hospitality, factory visits and partner networking support customer acquisition, retention and sales pipeline development?

**Geographic alignment:** Does the Formula 1 calendar align with its current markets and international expansion priorities?

**Brand positioning:** Could Formula 1 strengthen awareness, credibility, differentiation or consideration among relevant audiences?

**Consumer activation:** Could the business leverage content, licensing, retail promotions, consumer experiences or product campaigns?

**Category availability:** Are there realistic partnership opportunities across the grid, taking existing sponsors and category exclusivity into consideration?

Provide a concise, company-specific Formula 1 rationale.

Avoid generic explanations such as “Formula 1 provides global exposure.”

Identify actual potential customer overlap where possible.

Do not assume technical integration or contractual category availability without evidence.

## 19. Estimated annual partnership investment potential

Recognise that different companies will have different levels of realistic partnership affordability.

Use the following indicative annual rights-fee bands:

| Investment tier | Estimated annual potential |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 | USD 20M+ |
| Tier 2 | USD 8M to below USD 20M |
| Tier 3 | USD 3M to below USD 8M |
| Tier 4 | USD 1M to below USD 3M |
| Tier 5 | Below USD 1M |
| Unverified | Insufficient evidence |

These are prospecting estimates, not confirmed sponsorship budgets or rights-holder prices.

Consider revenue, profitability, liquidity, funding, cash runway, capital expenditure requirements, existing marketing commitments, ownership structure and financial backing.

Do not apply a universal fixed percentage of revenue or funding to estimate affordability.

Distinguish rights fees from activation expenditure and technical or in-kind contributions.

Where insufficient evidence exists, classify the investment potential as unverified.

## 20. Weighted outreach prioritisation

Score each company out of 100 using the following methodology:

| Assessment dimension | Maximum score |
|---|---:|
| Financial capacity | 35 |
| Strategic Formula 1 fit | 30 |
| Buying intent and timing | 25 |
| Decision-maker accessibility | 10 |
| Total | 100 |

### Financial capacity – 35 points

Assess financial strength, profitability, available liquidity, financial backing, existing marketing expenditure, reliability of financial evidence and realistic partnership affordability.

### Strategic Formula 1 fit – 30 points

Assess B2B opportunities, customer ecosystem alignment, technical integration, hospitality, geographic alignment, brand positioning, consumer activation and potential category opportunities.

### Buying intent and timing – 25 points

Assess the strength, relevance and recency of commercial growth, international expansion, marketing investment, funding, leadership changes and other confirmed strategic developments.

### Decision-maker accessibility – 10 points

Assess organisational structure, identifiable senior leadership, founder or CEO involvement, corporate complexity, marketing autonomy and accessibility of the relevant budget owner.

Give appropriate consideration to lean organisations where access to senior decision-makers may be easier.

Do not automatically favour small companies or penalise large organisations.

### Priority classification

| Overall score | Priority |
|---|---|
| 75–100 | High |
| 55–74 | Medium |
| 35–54 | Watchlist |
| Below 35 | Low |

Develop a reproducible scoring rubric and apply it consistently across industries and research batches.

Apply the following safeguards:

- Companies do not need to score positively across every dimension to remain eligible.
- Companies without confirmed buying signals remain eligible.
- Companies with unverified or merely indicative financial capacity should be clearly distinguished from financially qualified immediate-outreach prospects.
- Companies with confirmed insufficient capacity must not be prioritised for opportunities they cannot afford.
- Unresolved category conflicts must be flagged.
- Missing information must be distinguished from negative evidence.
- Numerical scores must not override material financial, compliance or commercial concerns.

## 21. Ranking logic

Rank every newly identified company within its individual sector from the highest to the lowest outreach priority.

Where companies receive similar scores, prioritise based on:

1. Stronger and more reliable financial capacity.
2. More compelling company-specific Formula 1 relevance.
3. Greater commercial growth ambitions.
4. Stronger and more timely buying signals.
5. Better accessibility of senior decision-makers.
6. Fewer potential contractual or category conflicts.

Provide individual numerical rankings rather than relying exclusively on High, Medium and Low classifications.

Produce separate sector rankings and a consolidated cross-sector ranking.

Companies requiring substantial financial verification should be presented in a separate ranked watchlist.

The ranking must reflect realistic commercial opportunities for SPORTFIVE, rather than simply company size.

## 27. Backtest the revised methodology

Before treating the revised numerical thresholds and scoring weights as permanent, backtest the original and revised methodologies against a representative sample of historical and current Formula 1 sponsors that were privately owned when they entered the sport.

Include different industries, geographies, ownership structures, business models and partnership types.

Use information that was publicly available before the relevant sponsorship announcement.

Do not use the sponsorship being tested as pre-existing evidence of capacity or buying intent.

Assess:

- Which sponsors the original methodology would have identified.
- Which sponsors it would have excluded and why.
- Which sponsors the revised methodology would capture.
- Which alternative indicators are most useful.
- Whether certain industries remain underrepresented.
- Whether broader discovery creates excessive numbers of weak prospects.
- Whether the revised thresholds are commercially realistic.

Where possible, assess a comparison group of similar non-sponsoring companies, while recognising that non-sponsorship does not necessarily indicate commercial unsuitability.

Do not invent historical sponsorship fees.

Use the findings to recommend evidence-based refinements.

Historical and current sponsors used for backtesting must not be added to the new prospect lists if they are otherwise excluded.

## 30. Final guiding principles

A company does not need to meet every financial threshold.

A company does not need to have raised venture capital.

A company does not need to disclose its financial information.

A company does not need to demonstrate recent buying signals.

A company does not need to operate internationally.

A subsidiary does not necessarily need to qualify through standalone financial performance if credible financial backing can be established.

The absence of publicly available financial information is not evidence that a company cannot afford a Formula 1 partnership.

Equally, substantial funding, high revenue or a billion-dollar valuation is not proof that a company has an available sponsorship budget.

The largest company is not necessarily the strongest outreach opportunity.

The objective is to identify companies with a credible combination of financial capacity, strategic relevance, commercial motivation, appropriate timing and realistic access to decision-makers.
