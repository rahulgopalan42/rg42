**Subject:** Microsoft x Mercedes F1: logo placement and what's behind the deal

Hi Alex,

Here's a quick rundown on Microsoft's deal with Mercedes and where the logo sits today.

**The deal**

Microsoft signed a multi-year partnership with Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS, announced on 22 January and starting this season. It moved over from Alpine, a team it first sponsored in 2012 when it raced as Lotus. That deal ended after 2025.

**Logo placement**

Nothing has moved since launch. Here's where it sits as of the Mexico City GP on 1 November:

- Both sides of the airbox, the intake above the driver's head
- The outer face of both front wing endplates, under the IWC logo
- The front of the race suits, below the PETRONAS lettering, with the same spot on the pit crew overalls

It's the standard Microsoft logo throughout, with no Azure or Copilot branding. It also stayed on for this year's one-off liveries, including the blue one in Austin.

**What Microsoft brings**

Microsoft is a technology partner as well as a sponsor. From the January announcement and a September case study:

- Azure and Microsoft AI add to the team's existing computing and data setup, at the factory and trackside. The uses named are simulation, performance analysis, race strategy and cross-team analytics.
- The team runs up to 10 million strategy simulations per race weekend, about 75% of them live. It scales computing up for race windows and back down afterwards to control costs.
- It has piloted "virtual sensors" on Azure. These are models that work out a sensor reading from data the car already produces, so no extra hardware is needed.
- Microsoft 365 and GitHub were already in use across Brackley, Brixworth and trackside, and the team is expanding both. More than 150 of its engineers use GitHub Copilot daily.
- It tracks its cost-cap spending in Power BI.

Side note: Fortune reported on 12 October that Peter Lee, who is leaving his role as president of Microsoft Science, plans to join Mercedes in a newly created AI advisor role. Mercedes didn't comment, and the article doesn't link the move to the sponsorship.
