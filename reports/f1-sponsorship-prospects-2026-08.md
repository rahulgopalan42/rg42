# F1 Sponsorship Prospects — USD 3.5M p.a. tier (August 2026)

**Prepared:** 6 August 2026 · **Season context:** 2026 grid of 11 teams (Cadillac debut; Audi has taken over Sauber, title-partnered by Revolut; Toyota Gazoo Racing title partner at Haas) ([F1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/cadillac-receive-final-approval-to-join-formula-1-grid-in-2026-as-11th-team.1saxQLtLPc7k9TFl4eNrQN), [RTR Sports](https://rtrsports.com/en/blog/every-teams-title-sponsor-for-the-2026-formula-1-season/), [F1.com/Audi](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/audi-f1-team-announce-title-partner-for-debut-2026-season.l7hjsMWxNbpPJKrAe9yGE))

**Method note:** Clay MCP tools were available and were used to enrich companies and decision-maker contacts for the top-10 prospects (company firmographics plus named marketing/partnership contacts at Databricks, Ramp, Robinhood, Klarna, Oura and Chime). Remaining buyer names were verified by web search. All revenue/funding figures below are cited; figures older than two fiscal years are flagged as stale.

## 1. Executive summary

The best money at the USD 3.5M/yr tier right now sits in three pools. First, US-facing fintech and AI/data companies that closed large raises or IPOs in the last 12 months — Databricks, Ramp, Chime, Klarna, Netskope, Oura, Mistral — have both the cash and an explicit brand-awareness motive, and F1's fintech/crypto precedent (Revolut titling Audi, Kraken at Williams, Coinbase/Public/Pepperstone at Aston Martin) creates acute category pressure on the ones still outside the sport. Second, Gulf aviation and real estate (Riyadh Air, Sobha Realty) are proven sports spenders at 5–10x this ticket who have not yet bought F1 despite Saudi/Abu Dhabi races on the calendar. Third, the two new works entries — Cadillac (all-American, still building its commercial roster) and Audi (debut season) — plus perennially accessible Haas, Williams, Racing Bulls and Alpine give this budget genuine car-visible inventory for the first time in years. The single strongest signal found: Chime already pays ~$11M/yr for an NBA jersey patch, Riyadh Air pays ~€40M/yr to Atlético Madrid, and Stake's ~$100M/yr Sauber title budget was freed when Audi took over — all are buyers who demonstrably clear the affordability gate.

## 3. Top-10 deep dives

### 1. Databricks — score 90 (Budget 38 / Fit 30 / Timing 22)
- **Affordability:** Raised $4B+ Series L at $134B valuation (Dec 2025, completed Feb 2026 at $5B with debt); $5.4B annualized revenue growing 65% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/16/databricks-funding-valuation.html), [Databricks PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/databricks-grows-55-yoy-surpasses-4-8b-revenue-run-rate-and-is-raising-4b-series-l-at-134b-valuation-302643445.html)). A $3.5M property is <0.1% of revenue.
- **Strategic rationale:** Hospitality-led enterprise selling to Fortune 500 CDOs/CIOs; direct rival Oracle renews Red Bull title at ~$110M/yr and Snowflake lurks — category pressure without a Databricks response. Data/AI story maps perfectly onto a data-driven sport.
- **Property to pitch:** Cadillac F1 Team official data & AI partner (US brand, debut-season inventory, TWG AI is affiliated with ownership, not a conflict) or Haas (US-owned, data category empty). Include Paddock Club hospitality at US races (Miami, Austin, Vegas).
- **Likely buyer:** CMO **Rick Schultz** (identified via Clay contact enrichment); regional heads of marketing exist in every F1 market.
- **Outreach hook:** "Oracle pays $110M a year to put its name on a title contender. For 3% of that, Databricks can own the data story at America's new F1 team — and host every Fortune 500 data leader at Vegas."

### 2. Riyadh Air — score 88 (Budget 34 / Fit 32 / Timing 22)
- **Affordability:** Wholly funded by Saudi Arabia's PIF; already pays ~€40M/season to Atlético Madrid plus a ~$274–329M stadium naming deal ([SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/news/atletico-madrid-riyadh-air-saudi-new-shirt-sponsor/), [Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/sponsorship/riyadh-air-replaces-civitas-as-atletico-madrid-stadium-naming-sponsor/)). $3.5M is rounding error.
- **Strategic rationale:** A launch-phase global airline needs premium international reach; F1's affluent, travel-heavy audience and the Saudi Arabian GP are an obvious platform. Airline category is open at team level almost everywhere (Qatar Airways is at Alpine and race-level; Etihad joins McLaren in 2026 per [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/mclaren-racing-names-etihad-airways-official-partner-from-2026-season-1.500456182)).
- **Property to pitch:** Aston Martin (Saudi nexus via Aramco/Maaden already on the car) or Williams official airline partner; alternatively trackside packages at Jeddah + European rounds.
- **Likely buyer:** **Osamah Alnuaiser**, SVP Marketing & Corporate Communications; **Michael Caip**, Head of Marketing & Brand ([leadership page](https://www.riyadhair.com/en/about-us/our-story/riyadh-air-leadership), RocketReach listings).
- **Outreach hook:** "You bought Atlético's shirt before your first flight. F1 gives you the same statement to a global premium audience — with a home race in Jeddah — at a tenth of the price."

### 3. Ramp — score 86 (Budget 34 / Fit 28 / Timing 24)
- **Affordability:** $750M raised at $44B (June 2026); $1B+ annualized revenue ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/04/ramp-raises-750m-at-44b-valuation-as-investors-hunger-for-fintechs-with-an-ai-story/), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ramp-reaches-32-billion-valuation-doubling-revenue-and-customers-in-past-year-302616510.html)).
- **Strategic rationale:** Sells corporate cards/expense/travel to CFOs — exactly the buyer F1 Paddock Club hospitality converts. Corporate-spend category is empty at team level (Mastercard/Visa/Cash App are consumer payments). Ramp's "save time and money" message rhymes with cost-cap-era F1 efficiency.
- **Property to pitch:** Williams official corporate spend partner (sits naturally beside Atlassian/Claude tech stack) or Cadillac (US enterprise base); anchor on Miami/Austin/Vegas hospitality.
- **Likely buyer:** Strategic events/brand team surfaced via Clay — **Amber Endicott** (Senior Strategic Events Manager), **Sejal Agrawal** (Senior Events Marketing Manager); route upward to head of brand/CMO office.
- **Outreach hook:** "Every CFO you want to meet is already in the Paddock Club. Put Ramp's name on the garage they're standing in."

### 4. Robinhood — score 86 (Budget 36 / Fit 30 / Timing 20)
- **Affordability:** Record FY2025: $4.5B revenue (+52%), $1.9B net income, $2.5B adjusted EBITDA ([Robinhood IR](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)).

- **Likely buyer:** CMO **Christina Smedley** ([Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/christina-smedley-joins-robinhood-as-chief-marketing-officer/)).

### 5. Chime — score 85 (Budget 31 / Fit 30 / Timing 24)
- **Affordability:** $2.19B FY2025 revenue (+31%), IPO June 2025 ([Chime IR](https://investors.chime.com/news-releases/news-release-details/chime-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial)); **already pays ~$11M/yr for the Dallas Mavericks jersey patch and renewed for ~$33M over three more years** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/13/dallas-mavericks-paid-33-million-over-3-years-by-chime-for-patch.html)) — direct evidence of comparable sponsorship spend well above the $3.5M gate.
- **Strategic rationale:** Post-IPO consumer awareness motive; everyday-Americans brand matches F1's exploding US fanbase across three US races.
- **Property to pitch:** Haas team partner (the US-owned team, most affordable car real estate on the grid) or Cadillac member-experience activation ("Chime Lane" translated to US GPs).
- **Likely buyer:** **Ryan McDaid**, Head of Brand Strategy, and **Jen Pao**, Head of Brand & Creative Operations (both via Clay); CMO office above them.
