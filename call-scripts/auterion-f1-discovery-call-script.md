# Auterion x SPORTFIVE: F1 Discovery Call Script

**Call type:** First call / discovery, listening-led. Goal: open warm and short, say why Auterion caught our eye, make the goal plain (understand if it makes sense, not sell), let them talk, fill the brief with sharp questions, and show how this sport could fit an engineering-first autonomy company. Be straight that there's a fit check on our side before anything moves. Agree next steps only if the conversation earns them.

**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you'd say it out loud, contractions and all. Don't read it word for word; glance at the bold anchors and talk. This audience is engineers first: the company grew out of an open-source project at ETH Zurich, and they'll spot vague claims and marketing talk instantly. Most of their business today is with governments, much of it defense. Keep every spoken block on the civilian story: autonomy, software that lets many machines work together, real-time control, engineering at the limit, and the people who build it. Don't raise the defense side yourself beyond the fit check in Part 6; if they raise it, go to Q8 and answer it straight. Let them talk more than you.

## Part 3: The smart questions (12 to 15 minutes)

*[Conversation starters, not a form. Ask one, stay quiet, follow what they give you. Their answers become the brief.]*

**Evaluation and success**
- "If you ever did something like this, on what basis would it be judged internally, and by whom?"

**Customers and partners**
- "Who buys from you, and who builds on you? I'm guessing those are different groups, and they'd need very different things from a program like this."

**Markets**
- "Which markets matter most for the brand: the US, Germany, Switzerland, wider Europe? The calendar runs five continents, with three races in the US and plenty within reach of Munich and Zurich."

## Part 4: How Formula 1 works, and where it could fit (8 to 10 minutes)

*[Calibrate to their answer from Part 2. Engineers: keep it precise, no fluff, every claim checkable. Stay on the civilian story. Invite questions: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Quick picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains a brand's options.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company: the calendar, the broadcasts, the races. The FIA writes the rules. And then eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac, each an independent business with its own partners and its own rules on who it works with.
>
> So a brand can enter at three levels: the sport, a team, or a driver personally. They come in very different sizes, and for Auterion I'd be straight from the start: the interesting doors are the right-sized ones."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Five parts, quickly.
>
> **Branding:** your name on the car, the suits, the garage, around 24 weekends a year, 1.8 billion total audience last season.
>
> **The official title:** Official Something Partner of the team, usable in all your own marketing and every partner conversation.
>
> **Hospitality:** paddock access and the Paddock Club, where 66% of guests are senior executives. One detail people miss: passes work all three days, so seven passes can host twenty different guests in a weekend.
>
> **Content:** access to the team's channels and drivers. This is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **People:** driver appearances, filming days, hot laps for your own people and partners.
>
> And around it all, the business platform: 25-plus partners per team, 350-plus across the grid. That room is a market in itself."

### 4.3 The audience

*[Three groups for Auterion: the engineers they want to hire, the industry they build with, and the wider public when they want it. Lead with talent.]*

> "Who you reach. For Auterion I'd split it in three.
>
> **The engineers.** This is the most engineering-obsessed sport on earth, and its audience skews young: 43% under 35. The robotics, software and controls engineers you're competing to hire grew up watching these cars. Being visible where they look is a hiring tool, not just a brand one.
>
> **The industry.** Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport, index 160 against 100 for the general population, and two thirds of the Paddock Club are senior executives. More than 350 companies sit around the grid. I won't pretend that's where governments buy; it isn't. But it's where industry meets: manufacturers, engineering firms, technology partners.
>
> **The wider world, when you want it.** 827 million fans. You don't need mass reach for what you sell, but if you ever want people to see the engineering side of Auterion, the stage is there.
>
> And one small thing I enjoyed finding: drones are part of how the sport is filmed now. F1 has flown drone shots in its live race coverage this year. Your world is already in the picture, quite literally."

*[Never imply Auterion's software flies those drones. We don't know whose it is. If asked: "No idea whose kit it is; I'd never guess."]*

### 4.4 Why this could fit Auterion

> "Why we thought this was worth a call. Three reasons, and you tell me if they hold.
>
> **One, the car is your kind of machine.** A Formula 1 car is covered in sensors, run by software, making decisions in real time at the very limit, with the driver in charge and the team watching every signal live. From this year the cars carry a much bigger electric share, and how that energy gets used, lap by lap, is a software problem. That's the most public showcase anywhere for the thing you build: software that lets a machine think fast while a person stays in control.
>
> **Two, talent.** You're hiring hard, in Zurich, Munich and the US. The engineers you want already love this sport, because it's their own discipline at full speed. Few platforms let a company that's not a household name stand in front of exactly those people.
>
> **Three, the open-source story.** Auterion grew out of an open project that a whole community of engineers built on together. This sport runs on the same idea at team level: a whole team of people, the car and the data working as one system, under hard rules, against a clock. That's a story about collaboration and engineering, and it's yours to tell.
>
> One honest note. Two things decide whether any of this happens. One is fit: some teams and partners have their own rules on who they work with, so we'd check that first and tell you plainly what we find. The other is size: these seats are scarce and priced that way, so we'd start with the small doors, and if the honest answer is 'not yet', we'll say exactly that."

### 4.5 Three early sketches (ideas, not proposals)

Low single-digit millions, not headline money. And if that's too big or the fit isn't there, a driver or content program sits well below it."
