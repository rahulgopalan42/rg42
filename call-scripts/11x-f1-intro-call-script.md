## Boilerplate: what 11x does

- **About 11x:** 11x builds AI agents that do sales and go-to-market work for revenue teams: finding and contacting prospects, picking up and qualifying inbound leads by voice, chat and email, and calling existing customers to grow accounts. Its two named agents are Alice, which runs outbound prospecting, and Julian, which handles inbound leads, and it sells them as "digital workers" that do the work rather than another tool a team has to run. Hasan Sukkar founded it in London in 2022; it moved its headquarters to San Francisco in 2024, keeps an office in London, and since May 2025 has been led by CEO Prabhav Jain, a former Brex executive, with Sukkar as non-executive chairman. It has raised more than $70 million, including a $24 million Series A led by Benchmark and a $50 million Series B led by Andreessen Horowitz in 2024, which TechCrunch's sources put at about a $350 million valuation. In October 2026 it set out a broader pitch, "agents that drive revenue" across inbound, outbound and account growth, and in May 2025 IBM announced it would offer 11x's agents to its enterprise customers through watsonx Orchestrate.
- **In one line, if someone asks on the call:** "They make AI agents that do the sales team's legwork. Finding the right people, writing to them, and picking up the phone the minute a lead comes in, so the humans can spend their time on the real conversations."

## 4. Questions to ask them

*[A conversation, not a form. Ask one, then stop and listen.]*

**1. Priorities**

> "You've just put out the new positioning, inbound, outbound and growing accounts. What's the one thing that has to go right in the next year?"

- *Follow-up:* "Is that more about new customers, or going deeper with the ones you've got?"
- *If they say bigger companies:* "That's interesting, because that's exactly the kind of buyer a paddock room is built for."

**2. Markets**

> "Is it mainly a US story for you, or is London and Europe a big part of it too?"

- *Follow-up:* "Is there anywhere you feel less known than you'd like to be?"
- *If they say Europe matters:* "Good to know. A big chunk of the calendar's in Europe, and so are a lot of the teams."

**3. Buyers and decision makers**

> "Who signs for 11x at a customer? Is it the CRO, the head of sales, RevOps, marketing?"

- *Follow-up:* "If I gave you a paddock weekend tomorrow, who would you bring?"
- *If they name CROs and sales leaders:* "That's a list a hosting program can be built around."

**4. Marketing today**

> "From the outside, you put a lot into content and events. What's working best for you right now, and what's the job for the brand next year?"

- *Follow-up:* "Have you done anything in sport before? And is there anything you'd never do?"
- *If they say they're rebuilding the brand:* "Then the timing's interesting. F1 works best as part of a plan, not as the whole plan."

**5. Brand versus pipeline**

> "It's B2B all the way, I'm guessing. But how much of the job is being known as a name in the category, versus straight pipeline?"

- *Follow-up:* "And is there anyone else you need to reach? Engineers you're hiring, partners like IBM?"

**6. Measuring success**

> "If you did something like this, how would it get judged internally, and by whom? I'm guessing you'd measure it the way you measure everything, in pipeline."

- *Follow-up:* "And what would make it a failure in your eyes? That usually tells me more."

**7. F1 familiarity**

> "How much do you follow Formula 1, if at all? Anyone on the team who's into it?"

- *If they barely know it:* "Quick version: there's the series itself, eleven teams that each run as their own business, and the drivers. You can come in at any of those levels, and they're very different sizes."

**8. Decision, budget and timing**

> "The unromantic one. Is there a rough budget range in mind, and who else would be part of a decision like this? I ask early because it decides which doors are worth your time."

- *Follow-up:* "And is this a this-year conversation or a next-year one? Both are fine."
- *If they won't give a number:* "That's fine. We'd come back with options at different levels, including the smallest sensible ones."
