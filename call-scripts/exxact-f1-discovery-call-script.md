# Exxact Corporation x SPORTFIVE: F1 Discovery Call Script

**Call type:** First call / discovery, brand-led. Goal: open with Exxact's own story to show real homework, keep the SPORTFIVE intro to three clear sentences, understand their objectives, fill out the brief with honest numbers against their size, show why a sport that wins with computing fits the people who build the machines, agree next steps.

**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you would say it out loud. Do not read it word for word. Glance at the bold anchors, then talk. Short sentences, slow pace, warm tone. The listeners are hardware people who have built systems for thirty years; they respect substance and dislike hype, so keep it plain, concrete and short. Let them talk more than you.

## Part 4: How Formula 1 partnerships work (8 to 10 minutes)

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Let me give you a simple picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains the options you have.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company. They own the commercial side: the calendar, the broadcast deals, the races.
>

>

>
> So a brand can partner at three levels: with the sport itself, with a team, or with a driver personally. For a company of Exxact's size, I want to be straight with you: the supplier door at a team, with hardware carrying part of the deal, is where the realistic value usually sits, and it can be excellent. I will explain as we go."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Think of it in five parts, and keep in mind that supplier entries buy slices of this, not all of it.
>
> **One, branding.** Your logo on the car, the race suits, the garage. Around 24 race weekends a year. Last season the total audience was 1.8 billion, and the average race reached 76 million people.
>
> **Two, the official title.** You become, for example, the Official AI Systems Partner of a team. You use the team's name, logo and imagery in your own marketing and in every buyer conversation.
>
> **Three, hospitality.** Paddock access, the Paddock Club, garage tours, time with the drivers. 66% of Paddock Club guests are senior executives, and for a technology brand that room includes the CTOs and research leaders you sell to.
>
> And one detail people miss. A Grand Prix runs over three days, and your passes work every day. Seven passes can host twenty different guests in one weekend.
>
> **Four, content and digital.** Access to the team's channels and drivers for content. Formula 1 is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **Five, people.** Driver appearances, filming days, hot laps for your top customers.
>
> And around all of this sits the business platform: every team carries 25 or more partners, more than 350 companies across the grid. That room is your pipeline."

### 4.3 The audience

*[Pure B2B framing for Exxact: the buyers first, the engineering halo second.]*

> "Now, who you reach. For Exxact I would be precise about this.
>
> **The buyers.** Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport: fans index at 160 for management and strategy roles, where the average person is 100. The people who buy and build high-performance computing watch this sport because it is their own discipline at 300 kilometers per hour. For a systems builder, that is the most natural audience in sport.
>
> **The world, when you want it.** 827 million fans, young and online. You do not need mass reach to sell clusters. But when the Exxact name needs to mean something beyond the NVIDIA world, the stage is already yours.
>

### 4.4 Why this could fit Exxact

> "Let me tell you why I find this opportunity interesting for Exxact. Three reasons.
>
> **First, the story writes itself.** This sport is a computing competition with a hard limit: capped budgets, capped aero computing. Winning here is not about having the most machines. It is about having exactly the right machines. That is, sentence for sentence, what Exxact builds. The specialist who configures the perfect system beats the giant who ships a standard box: that is your pitch in your market, and it is this sport's daily reality.
>
> **Second, the room is your market.** The technology wave on this grid, AWS, Atlassian, Oracle, HP, reportedly Microsoft, has filled the paddock with exactly the world you sell into, and the Paddock Club with the people who sign for machines. One good season of hosting the right research directors and CTOs can be worth more than a year of trade shows.
>
> **Third, a real working role, sized to you.** Teams run serious computing on both sides of the garage: simulation and AI at the factory, engineering stations at the track. Imagine the partnership where some of that visibly runs on machines Exxact built, with the hardware itself carrying much of the deal's value. We have built working-supplier shapes before: RUCKUS at Haas powers the team's operations with a live showcase in the garage. For a company your size, product-plus-cash is the door that makes the numbers work.

That is exactly why our recommendation for Exxact will focus on the entries where a specialist builder gets real value, not a logo it cannot afford."
