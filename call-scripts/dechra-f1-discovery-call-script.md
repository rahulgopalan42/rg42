# Dechra x SPORTFIVE: F1 Discovery Call Script

**Call type:** First call / discovery, listening-led. Goal: open warm and short, say why Dechra caught our eye, make the goal plain (find out if it makes sense, not sell), let them talk, fill the brief with sharp questions, and test openly whether this sport can do a real job for a veterinary pharmaceuticals company. Agree next steps only if the conversation earns them.

**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you'd say it out loud, contractions and all. Don't read it word for word; glance at the bold anchors and talk. This audience is scientific, commercial and, under private-equity owners, very numbers-minded. They'll be polite and skeptical, because Formula 1 isn't an obvious fit for animal medicine, and pretending otherwise would lose the room in a minute. Name the gap early, ask good questions, and let them talk more than you.

### Flags
- **Capacity note, normal on size, high scrutiny:** £830m revenue and 2,700 people put entry-level team positions within reach on size alone. But this is a private-equity-owned, leveraged business in the middle of a transformation, with a reported operating loss in FY2025 from deal accounting. Every investment needs a payback case the owners would accept. Surface budget and the decision process in the first half, lead with hospitality and content, size with honest numbers, and no headline-deal talk.
- **Fit check, be open about it:** the F1 fit is not obvious. No animals in the sport and no vet story to borrow. Go in to test four angles in this order of strength: the room (vet group owners and distributors), pride for their own people under new ownership, a care-and-precision story, and a pet-owner consumer story. The room is strongest; the consumer story is weakest and the most regulated. Be ready to recommend "not yet."
- **Advertising rules:** most of their products are prescription-only. Under EU Regulation 2019/6 (Article 120), prescription vet medicines can be advertised only to vets and people allowed to supply them, not the public; our understanding is the UK works the same way, while the US allows owner-facing pet medicine ads under FDA rules. So outside the US, any pet-owner idea must be corporate-brand or cause-led, never product-led. Their pet diets (Specific) are not medicines. Verify market by market with their regulatory team before anything goes on paper.
- **HQ ambiguity:** our brief called them UK-headquartered. Since June 2026 their global headquarters is in Boston; the registered UK company is still in Northwich per the FY2025 filing. On the call, say "UK roots, new home in Boston" and let them describe it.
- **No standing compliance or defense flags triggered:** roots are UK, headquarters now US, owners are EQT (Sweden) and ADIA's Luxinva. Brazil is an operating subsidiary, not roots. Name both owners correctly if ownership comes up.
- **Category check:** claim nothing open. Animal health, pharma, pet care and pet nutrition get mapped team by team, and the series too, at the recommendations stage. Also check whether any team or the series restricts pharma-linked partners.
- **Claims discipline:** never say or imply any F1 team uses Dechra products or has any link to animal health. Their numbers come from the Companies House filing and their own releases; attribute them ("your filing says", "as you announced"). We have no data on pet ownership among F1 fans; don't guess. The ideas in 4.4 and 4.5 are ideas to build, not facts. If you want the well-known story of a children's hospital learning patient handovers from a pit crew, verify it first; it is not in our checked facts.
- **Names check:** citations in this script (Technogym as McLaren's fitness partner since 2004, Cognizant, ServiceNow, NetApp and Tata Communications as B2B names around the sport, RUCKUS at Haas, Airwallex at McLaren, Liberty Media's ownership since 2017) were verified in September 2026 checks; Dechra facts were checked on 20 October 2026 against their filing and newsroom. Re-verify on call day.
- **Pipeline check:** confirm no live deal in animal health, pharma, pet care or pet nutrition, and check the register for earlier Dechra outreach.

## Part 3: The smart questions (12 to 15 minutes)

*[Conversation starters, not a form. Ask one, stay quiet, follow what they give you. Their answers become the brief, and they tell you whether there's a fit at all.]*

**Open the story**
- "Tell me where Dechra is right now, in your own words. What's going well, and what's the next hill to climb?"
- "What are the key focus areas for the next twelve months? What comes up in every leadership conversation?"

**The direction, and where this could play a role**
- "How do you see something like Formula 1, or sport at all, maybe playing a role in where Dechra is headed? Even a rough first instinct helps, and 'it doesn't' is a fine answer too."
- "And as a business, where could you imagine it adding value, if anywhere: the big customer relationships, your own people, or the brand with pet owners?"

**The new chapter**
- "From the outside, the last two years look like a lot of change: new owners, a new strategy, a move to Boston. How has that landed inside the company?"
- "What would make your people proud to work at Dechra next year? And is hiring and keeping good people a big topic right now?"

**The end goal**
- "Zooming out: if Dechra 2030 goes right, what does the company look like?"
- "And what would make a partnership like this a failure in your eyes? That answer usually tells us more than the first one."

**How investments get decided now**
- "Since EQT came in, how does a new investment like this get decided? Is there a set business case, a payback period, a committee it has to pass?"
- "What do your owners need to see to say yes to something outside the core plan? And how far ahead do they think: two years, five?"
- "Is anything off the table while the transformation is running? Better I know now."

**Evaluation and success**
- "If you ever did something like this, on what basis would it be judged internally, and by whom?"
- "What would it need to deliver for you to call it a success after year one?"

**Customers and buyers**
- "Who are the relationships that decide your year? I'm guessing it's a fairly small number of big vet groups and distributors. Is that fair?"
- "How do you look after those people today: conferences, education, dinners? What works best, and what's getting harder?"
- "And where do pet owners sit for you? Do you want them to know the name Dechra, or is it enough that their vet does?"

**Markets**
- "Where's the growth focus: North America, Europe, or both? The calendar runs on five continents, and the US alone has three races."

**Brand and marketing today**
- "How does Dechra market today? I'd guess mostly through your field teams, the Academy and the big vet conferences. What else?"
- "Is the bigger job right now top of the funnel or bottom of the funnel: being known by people who've never heard of Dechra, or going deeper with the vets and groups who already buy from you?"
- "And what are the rules you live by? I know there are limits on talking about prescription medicines to the public, and they change by country."

**Budget and timing**
- "The unromantic question: is there a budget range in mind for something like this? I ask early because it decides which doors we even look at, and it saves us both time."
- "And timing: is this a now conversation or a next-year conversation? Both are fine; they just change what we bring back."

**Decision process**
- "Apart from you, who'd be part of this decision? Jesper and the leadership team, finance, your owners?"

**Hospitality**
- "Last one, my favorite. If I gave you a paddock weekend tomorrow, who would you bring, and why those people? That answer usually tells me what a partnership is really for."

*[If they hesitate on budget:]*

> "No problem, you don't need a number today. If we get to recommendations, you'll see options at different levels, including the smallest sensible ones, with what each level buys and how you'd measure it."

## Part 4: How Formula 1 works, and where it could fit (8 to 10 minutes)

*[Calibrate to their answer from Part 2. Keep it plain; this room thinks in evidence. Invite questions: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Quick picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains a brand's options.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company: the calendar, the broadcasts, the races. The FIA writes the rules. And then eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac, each an independent business with its own partners.
>
> So a brand can enter at three levels: the sport, a team, or a driver personally. They come in very different sizes, and for Dechra I'd be straight from the start: the interesting doors are the small ones."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Five parts, quickly.
>
> **Branding:** your name on the car, the suits, the garage, around 24 weekends a year, 1.8 billion total audience last season.
>
> **The official title:** Official Something Partner of the team, usable in your own marketing and your customer conversations.
>
> **Hospitality:** paddock access and the Paddock Club, where 66% of guests are senior executives. One detail people miss: passes work all three days, so seven passes can host twenty different guests in a weekend.
>
> **Content:** access to the team's channels and drivers. This is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **People:** driver appearances, filming days, hot laps for your best customers.
>
> And around it all, the business platform: 25-plus partners per team, 350-plus across the grid. For Dechra I'd see that as a bonus, not the main event."

### 4.3 The audience

*[Three possible audiences for Dechra. Say which ones you're sure about and which you're not.]*

> "Who you reach. For Dechra it's three groups, and I'll tell you how sure I am about each.
>
> **The decision makers.** This one's solid. Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport, index 160 against 100 for the general population, and two thirds of Paddock Club guests are senior executives. If your key buyers are the people running big vet groups and distribution businesses, this is an invitation they'd say yes to.
>
> **Your own people.** About twenty-seven hundred of them, in twenty-seven countries. The sport runs around 24 weekends on five continents, three of them in the US. That's a lot of shared moments for a company that's been through a lot of change.
>
> **Pet owners, maybe.** 827 million fans, 42% women, 43% under 35, and they're number one in sport for buying premium and for researching online before they buy. But I don't have data on how many fans own pets, and I won't guess. That one's a test, not a promise."

### 4.4 Why this could fit Dechra

> "Why we thought this was worth a call. Three reasons, and you tell me if they hold. I'll start with the strongest.
>
> **One, the room.** Your own filing says it plainly: vet groups and buying groups are getting bigger, and those relationships matter more every year. When your buyers get fewer and bigger, three days with the right twenty people is worth more than any ad. Seven passes, twenty different guests, one weekend. That's a key account program, not a sponsorship.
>
> **Two, pride, in a year of change.** New owners, a new strategy, a new home in Boston. Changes like that can leave people unsure where they stand. A season gives your people something to follow and share together, week after week. It's not a reason on its own, but it adds up with the first one.
>
> **Three, precision and care.** A race team is a group of specialists looking after a machine that can't tell them what's wrong. They read the data, make small careful calls, and work under strict rules. That's not far from a vet team with a patient that can't talk. There may be a story there, for vets and maybe for pet owners in the US. This is the one I'm least sure about, and I'd want to test it with you.
>
> One honest note. A full team seat is a big number for a business that markets mainly through vets, and under your owners every investment needs a payback case. So we'd start with the smallest thing that could prove itself, most likely the room, and only build if it works. And if the numbers don't pass your owners' test, we'll be the first to say so."

### 4.5 Three early sketches (ideas, not proposals)

*[Offer as sketches; their reaction feeds the brief. Watch which one they lean into.]*

> "To make it concrete, three sketches, all sized for Dechra on purpose. Ideas, not proposals.
>
> **One, the buyers' weekend.** A hospitality program at two or three races, say one in Europe and one in the US: the owners and leaders of your biggest vet groups and distributors, hosted properly, with your leadership in the room. Seven passes, twenty guests a weekend, no logo needed. Pure hospitality sits below the price of a team deal.
>
> **Two, the team behind the patient.** A content and learning series with a team: how a race crew works under pressure, plans, checks and hands over, set next to how a clinic team does the same. Built for vets and vet nurses through your Academy, and for your own people. It speaks to vet teams' working lives, which you already care about through Not One More Vet.
>
> **Three, the owner story, US first.** Only if the first two prove out and the rules allow it: a small content or driver program for pet owners in the US, about the care it takes to look after something you love. Small, measured, and only where you're allowed to talk to owners."

### Q1. "What are comparable brands doing in Formula 1, and why are they using it?"

> "Good question, and I'll be straight: I can't point you to an animal health company in the sport, and I won't make one up. What I can show you is the pattern for businesses that sell to professionals, not the public.
>
> B2B names like Cognizant, ServiceNow, NetApp and Tata Communications sit around the grid. Technogym, a specialist fitness brand, has been McLaren's fitness partner since 2004. That's more than twenty years with one team.
>
> Why do they do it? Three jobs, usually. Trust: their name next to serious engineering. The room: hosting the people they sell to, race after race. And proof: CommScope's RUCKUS brand provides the enterprise wireless that powers the Haas team's operations, with a live showcase in the garage.
>
> And it can show up in revenue. Airwallex, a fintech at McLaren: during the partnership their revenue grew 50% year on year across EMEA and the Americas, and UK revenue grew 57% from new customers. A very different business from yours, but it shows what the platform can do when the brief is right."

### Q2. "How do F1 teams drive introductions in business?"

> "Very concretely. It's a managed process, not a cocktail party.
>
> Every team runs a family of 25-plus partners, and the team's partnership department actively connects them: compliant introductions, partner summits, business forums on race weekends, private dinners. Add the hospitality rights, and you choose who sits at your table.
>
> For Dechra I'd be straight: the partner network is a nice extra. The real value is your own guests. The vet group owners and distributors you'd bring are the point, and a race gives you three days with them instead of thirty minutes at a conference stand."

### Q3. "How is ROI measured in Formula 1?"

> "Four ways, and you agree the framework upfront. With your owners, I'd insist on that.
>
> **Relationship pipeline:** for Dechra this comes first. Which accounts you hosted, what happened with them over the next year, and what that's worth.
>
> **Your people:** you already run an engagement survey. We'd measure against it, before and after.
>
> **Brand tracking:** awareness and trust among vets, and among pet owners if we ever go there, measured before and after.
>
> **Media and content value:** independent firms like Nielsen track every second your brand appears and price it against your fee. For a hospitality-led program it matters less, so I wouldn't lead with it.
>
> The point is you'd judge it on numbers your owners already care about, not on impressions."

### Q4. "Which entry would fit us best?"

> "Today, instinct only: a hospitality program first, maybe with a content piece for vets and your own people, and a bigger step only if that proves itself. I wouldn't respect you by pretending that's a recommendation.
>
> The mapping after this call turns it into names, numbers and a sequence against your brief. And if the honest answer is 'not yet', that's what we'll bring back."

### Q5. "What does a partnership cost?"

> "The price follows the rights. Rule of thumb: entry-level team positions start in the low single-digit millions a year, official partner tiers sit above that, and driver, content and pure hospitality programs sit below, some well below.
>
> Why expensive? Eleven teams for the whole world, one seat per category, three-to-five-year deals. Scarcity.
>
> For Dechra, the shapes I'd explore first live at the small end: hospitality and content. And our advice is free either way: the rights holders pay our fee."

### Q6. "Why work with SPORTFIVE? Could we not go to a team directly?"

> "You could. Two reasons brands use us anyway.
>
> One, information. A team sells you its own story; we put all eleven next to each other: inventory, pricing, appetite, and what each would mean for Dechra specifically. For more than twelve years we worked exclusively with Mercedes and Ferrari; we gave that up deliberately to sit on the brand's side, across the whole grid. The neutral seat is the product.
>
> Two, process. We build the business case, benchmark the price and negotiate on your behalf, because we do it every week. With owners who'll test every number, that matters.
>
> And since the rights holders pay our fee, it costs you nothing to use us. There's no version where you pay more."

### Q7. "Are any of our competitors already in Formula 1?"

> "I won't wing that one from memory. This space moves, and you deserve the precise picture.
>
> Mapping animal health, pharma, pet care and pet nutrition across all eleven teams and the series is part of the analysis after this call. You'd see who's in, who's out, and where the open positions are."

### Q8. "We're a vet pharma company. What has Formula 1 got to do with animals?"

> "On the track? Nothing. There are no animals in Formula 1, and I'd lose your trust in a minute if I tried to tell you otherwise.
>
> But here's the thing. You don't sell to animals. You sell to the people who look after them: vets, the groups that own practices, the distributors, and behind all of them, people who love their pets. So the real question isn't what F1 has to do with animals. It's whether it can do a job for the people in your business. Hosting the twenty people who decide your year. Giving your own team something to be proud of. Maybe telling a story about care.
>
> And if you'll let me have a bit of fun with it: a race car can't tell its engineers where it hurts either. They read the data, make small careful calls, and work as one team. Your customers do that every day, with patients that can't talk.
>
> If none of that holds up against your numbers, we'll say so, and we'll part as friends. But I think it's worth an hour to find out."
