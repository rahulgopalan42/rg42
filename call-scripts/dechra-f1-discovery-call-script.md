# Dechra (Pet Nutrition) x SPORTFIVE: F1 Discovery Call Script

**Call type:** First call / discovery, listening-led, built around Dechra's pet nutrition business: SPECIFIC, its range of vet-recommended diets for cats and dogs. Goal: open warm and short, say why Dechra caught our eye, make the goal plain (find out if it makes sense, not sell), let them talk, fill the brief with sharp questions, and test openly whether this sport can do a real job for a pet food brand that sells through vets. Agree next steps only if the conversation earns them.

**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you'd say it out loud, contractions and all. Don't read it word for word; glance at the bold anchors and talk. This audience is scientific, commercial and, under private-equity owners, very numbers-minded. They'll be polite and a little skeptical, because Formula 1 isn't an obvious home for pet food. Name the gap early, ask good questions, and let them talk more than you.

## Part 3: The smart questions (12 to 15 minutes)

*[Conversation starters, not a form. Ask one, stay quiet, follow what they give you. Their answers become the brief, and they tell you whether there's a fit at all.]*

**Open the story**
- "Tell me where SPECIFIC is right now, in your own words. What's going well, and what's the next hill to climb?"
- "Where does nutrition sit in Dechra's plan for the next few years? Is it a growth bet, or a support act for the medicines?"

**The direction, and where this could play a role**
- "How do you see something like Formula 1, or sport at all, maybe playing a role in where the brand is headed? Even a rough first instinct helps, and 'it doesn't' is a fine answer too."
- "And as a business, where could you imagine it adding value, if anywhere: with vets, with pet owners, or with your own people?"

**How the sale really happens**
- "Walk me through how a pet ends up on SPECIFIC. Who starts that conversation in the clinic: the vet, the vet nurse, or the owner asking?"
- "What makes a clinic recommend you over the big names? And what's the hardest part of winning a clinic today?"

**Pet owners**
- "How much do you want pet owners to know the name SPECIFIC, given you sell through vets? Would owners walking in and asking for it help you, or would it cause friction with clinics?"
- "And what are the rules you live by on what you can say to owners about diets that support a condition? They change by country, and I'd rather know now."

**Markets**
- "Which markets matter most for SPECIFIC today? Is it mainly Europe, and is North America part of the plan?"

**The end goal**
- "Zooming out: if everything goes right for nutrition over the next three years, what does SPECIFIC look like?"
- "And what would make a partnership like this a failure in your eyes? That answer usually tells us more than the first one."

**How investments get decided now**
- "Since EQT came in, how does a new investment like this get decided? Is there a set business case, a payback period, a committee it has to pass?"
- "Would something like this come out of a nutrition budget, or would it need group backing?"

**Evaluation and success**
- "If you ever did something like this, on what basis would it be judged, and by whom?"
- "What would it need to deliver for you to call it a success after year one?"

**Brand and marketing today**
- "How do you market SPECIFIC today? I'd guess mostly through your field teams, the Academy, clinic materials and the big vet conferences. What else?"
- "Is the bigger job right now top of the funnel or bottom of the funnel: being known by clinics and owners who've never heard of SPECIFIC, or going deeper with the clinics who already recommend it?"

**Budget and timing**
- "The unromantic question: is there a budget range in mind for something like this? I ask early because it decides which doors we even look at, and it saves us both time."
- "And timing: is this a now conversation or a next-year conversation? Both are fine; they just change what we bring back."

**Decision process**
- "Apart from you, who'd be part of this decision? The leadership team, finance, your owners?"

**Hospitality**
- "Last one, my favorite. If I gave you a paddock weekend tomorrow, who would you bring, and why those people? That answer usually tells me what a partnership is really for."

## Part 4: How Formula 1 works, and where it could fit (8 to 10 minutes)

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Quick picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains a brand's options.

>

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Five parts, quickly.
>
> **Branding:** your name on the car, the suits, the garage, around 24 weekends a year, 1.8 billion total audience last season.
>
> **The official title:** Official Something Partner of the team, usable in your own marketing and your clinic conversations.
>
> **Hospitality:** paddock access and the Paddock Club, where 66% of guests are senior executives. One detail people miss: passes work all three days, so seven passes can host twenty different guests in a weekend.
>
> **Content:** access to the team's channels and drivers. This is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **People:** driver appearances, filming days, hot laps for your best customers.
>
> And around it all, the business platform: 25-plus partners per team, 350-plus across the grid. For SPECIFIC I'd see that as a bonus, not the main event."

### 4.3 The audience

*[Three possible audiences. Say which ones you're sure about and which you're not.]*

> "Who you reach. For SPECIFIC it's three groups, and I'll tell you how sure I am about each.
>
> **The people who recommend you.** This one's solid. If the clinics, the big vet groups and your distributors decide your year, a race weekend is an invitation they'd say yes to, and two thirds of Paddock Club guests are senior executives. Three days together beats thirty minutes at a conference stand.

> **Your own people.** About twenty-seven hundred across the group. The sport runs around 24 weekends on five continents. That's a lot of shared moments for a company that's been through a lot of change."

### 4.4 Why this could fit SPECIFIC

> "Why we thought this was worth a call. Three reasons, and you tell me if they hold. I'll start with the strongest.
>
> **One, the room.** Your own filing says it: vet groups and buying groups are getting bigger, and those relationships matter more every year. The 2026 cars run on fully sustainable fuel, and teams bring in specialist nutrition partners for their drivers. SPECIFIC is the same belief, for pets: the right nutrition, for a specific need, chosen with an expert. That's a story vets and vet nurses would get in one sentence.
>
> **Three, owners who research and stay loyal.** Premium pet food is bought by people who read labels and stick with what works. That's exactly how F1 fans shop. If owners walking into a clinic and asking about SPECIFIC helps you, there may be something here, in Europe first, within the rules. This is the one I'm least sure about.
>
> One honest note. Nutrition is a smaller part of Dechra, and under your owners every investment needs a payback case. So we'd start with the smallest thing that could prove itself, most likely the room or a content piece, and only build if it works. And if the numbers don't pass your owners' test, we'll be the first to say so."
