# Trint x SPORTFIVE: F1 Discovery Call Script

**Call type:** First call / discovery, brand-led. Goal: open with Trint's own story to show real homework, keep the SPORTFIVE intro to three clear sentences, understand their objectives, fill out the brief with honest numbers against their size, show why the world's biggest talking sport fits a speech-to-text company, agree next steps.

**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you would say it out loud. Do not read it word for word. Glance at the bold anchors, then talk. Short sentences, slow pace, warm tone. The listeners are a media-tech team founded by a journalist; they can smell a canned pitch instantly, so keep it plain, concrete and short. Let them talk more than you.

## Part 3: The brief. Key discovery questions (12 to 15 minutes)

*[These are conversation starters, not a form. Ask one, then stay quiet and follow what they give you. The bold labels are for you; the words are for them. Capture answers, they become the brief.]*

**Open the story**
- "Tell me where Trint is right now, in your own words. What is going well, and what is the next hill to climb?"
- "When you and your team talk about the next three years, what comes up again and again?"

**Objectives, the dream and the fear**
- "Imagine we did something in this sport together and it worked beautifully. Three years from now, what is different for Trint?"

**The growth story**
- "Newsrooms made your name. Where does growth come from next: more newsrooms, corporate comms teams, enterprises?"
- "Which is the harder fight right now: being known outside the newsroom world, or being chosen inside it?"

**Buyers and accounts**
- "Who exactly signs a Trint contract: the head of editorial operations, the comms director, IT? Those are the people we would want in a room."
- "Are there named accounts you would build a program around?"

**Markets**
- "Which markets matter most: the UK, the US, Europe? The calendar covers all of them."

**Product and integration**
- "Here is one I am curious about. A race weekend is a factory of spoken word: press conferences, interviews, briefings, in several languages. Would a real working role, Trint running inside a team's media operation, interest you? Or is this more about the story and the visibility?"

**Budget and timing**
- "Now the unromantic question, and for a company your size it is the most important one: is there a budget range in mind? I ask early because it decides which doors we even look at, and it saves us both time."
- "And timing: is this a now conversation, or a next-year conversation? Both are fine. They just change what we bring back."

**Decision process**
- "Apart from you, who is part of this decision? The founder, the board, your investors?"

**Marketing context**
- "What is working for you today? And is there a story you wish more of the market knew?"
- "Would your team activate a sport partnership, content, events, campaigns, or would you want support there too?"

**Hospitality**

## Part 4: How Formula 1 partnerships work (8 to 10 minutes)

*[Calibrate to their answer from Part 2. If they know the sport, compress. For Trint, give the driver door and supplier structures real airtime; that is where their budget lives. Invite questions as you go: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Let me give you a simple picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains the options you have.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company. They own the commercial side: the calendar, the broadcast deals, the races.
>
> The FIA is the regulator. They write the rules.
>
> And then there are eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac. Each team is an independent business with its own owners and its own partnerships.
>
> So a brand can partner at three levels: with the sport itself, with a team, or with a driver personally. For a company of Trint's size, I want to be straight with you: the driver door and the supplier door are where the realistic value usually sits, and they can be excellent. I will explain as we go."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Think of it in five parts, and keep in mind that smaller entries buy slices of this, not all of it.
>
> **One, branding.** Your logo on the car, the race suits, the garage. Around 24 race weekends a year. Last season the total audience was 1.8 billion, and the average race reached 76 million people.
>
> **Two, the official title.** You become, for example, the Official Transcription Partner of a team. You use the team's name, logo and imagery in your own marketing and sales conversations.
>
> **Three, hospitality.** Paddock access, the Paddock Club, garage tours, time with the drivers. 66% of Paddock Club guests are senior executives.
>
> And one detail people miss. A Grand Prix runs over three days, and your passes work every day. Seven passes can host twenty different guests in one weekend.
>
> **Four, content and digital.** Access to the team's channels and drivers for content. Formula 1 is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **Five, people.** Driver appearances, filming days, access for your own content.
>
> And around all of this sits the business platform: every team carries 25 or more partners, more than 350 companies across the grid. That room is your pipeline."

### 4.3 The audience

*[B2B framing for Trint: their buyers are media and comms leaders; the sport is also their raw material.]*

> "Now, who you reach. For Trint I see it in two layers.
>
> **The buyers.** Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport: fans index at 160 for management and strategy roles. In the Paddock Club, 66% of guests are senior executives. And the media centre at every race holds the world's press, the exact newsrooms you serve, working on deadline, every two weeks.
>
> **The content world.** This sport is a content machine: 61% of surveyed fans engage with F1 content daily, and it is the number one sport for social engagement. The teams, the broadcasters and the sport itself all produce spoken-word content at industrial scale. For a company whose product turns talk into text, this is not just an audience. It is a working environment full of your use case."

### 4.4 Why this could fit Trint

> "Let me tell you why I find this opportunity interesting for Trint. Three reasons.
>
> **First, the story writes itself.** The fastest sport on earth runs on the spoken word: the press conference quote that travels the world in minutes, the interview in Italian, the briefing in English, the podcast after the race. Trint in this sport has a one-sentence story: where the world's fastest sport turns talk into text.
>
> **Second, your customers are already here.** The BBC, the Associated Press, Reuters: the newsrooms that trust Trint cover every race. A visible role in this sport is marketing aimed exactly at the people who already know your name, and at the comms world watching them.
>
> **Third, a real working role, sized to you.** A team's media office handles interviews, press conferences and content in several languages, every two weeks. Imagine Trint running inside that operation as the official supplier, with the partnership partly paid in product. We have built working-supplier shapes before: RUCKUS at Haas powers the team's operations with a live showcase in the garage. For a company your size, product-plus-cash is the door that makes the numbers work.
>
> One last thing on value, and on honesty. These seats are scarce: eleven teams, one seat per category, multi-year deals, and that keeps prices high. That is exactly why our recommendation for Trint will focus on the entries where a company of 60 people gets real value, not a logo it cannot afford."

### 4.5 Three activation ideas (examples, not proposals)

*[Offer these as sketches to make it concrete. Let them react; their reaction feeds the brief.]*

> "To make it concrete, three sketches. Early thoughts, nothing more, and all of them sized small on purpose.
>
> **One, the voice of the race.** A content series built on what the sport says: the best radio moments, press-conference quotes and interviews of each race weekend, turned into text and translated, published fast. Made with a team's media office or a driver, powered visibly by Trint. Cheap to run, loved by fans, and it demonstrates the product every single race.
>
> **Two, the supplier seat.** Trint as the working transcription tool inside one team's media operation, with an official supplier title and the fee largely offset by product. Your sales line becomes: the team that faces the world's press every two weeks runs its words through Trint.
>
> **Three, the driver door.** A personal partnership with one driver: your logo on their personal channels, filming days, and a content angle that fits you perfectly, their words, in their languages, to their fans. Driver deals sit below team pricing and can be the smartest first step into the sport."
