**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you'd say it out loud, contractions and all. Don't read it word for word; glance at the bold anchors and talk. ## Part 4: How Formula 1 works, and where it could fit (8 to 10 minutes)

*[Calibrate to their answer from Part 2. Keep it precise; this room trusts data, not adjectives. Invite questions: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Quick picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains a brand's options.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company: the calendar, the broadcasts, the races. The FIA writes the rules. And then eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac, each an independent business with its own partners.
>
> So a brand can enter at three levels: the sport, a team, or a driver personally. They come in very different sizes, and for Advanced Navigation I'd be straight from the start: the interesting doors are the right-sized ones."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Five parts, quickly.
>
> **Branding:** your name on the car, the suits, the garage, around 24 weekends a year, 1.8 billion total audience last season.
>
> **The official title:** Official Something Partner of the team, usable in all your own marketing and every customer conversation.
>
> **Hospitality:** paddock access and the Paddock Club, where 66% of guests are senior executives. One detail people miss: passes work all three days, so seven passes can host twenty different guests in a weekend.
>
> **Content:** access to the team's channels and drivers. This is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **People:** driver appearances, filming days, hot laps for your top customers.
>
> And around it all, the business platform: 25-plus partners per team, 350-plus across the grid. That room is a market in itself."

### 4.3 The audience

*[Two audiences for Advanced Navigation: the engineers who choose and build with the technology, and the executives who sign.]*

> "Who you reach. For you it's really two groups, and this sport is unusual because it holds both.
>
> **The engineers.** This is the most engineering-obsessed sport on earth, and its audience skews young: 43% under 35. The people who design autonomous trucks, survey drones and spacecraft watch this sport because it's their own problem at full speed. That's also who you're trying to hire.
>
> **The signers.** Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport, index 160 against 100 for the general population. In the Paddock Club, two thirds are senior executives, and for an engineering brand that means CTOs and heads of engineering. And F1 fans are number one in sport for researching online before they buy. That suits a company that wins on test data.
>
> And the stage is already full of serious technology: AWS, Oracle, HP, Microsoft, Atlassian. Your buyers already read this sport as a place where engineering is taken seriously."

### 4.4 Why this could fit Advanced Navigation

> "Why we thought this was worth a call. Three reasons, and you tell me if they hold.
>
> **One, precision at speed is your whole business, and this sport's whole story.** You make sure a machine knows exactly where it is, which way it's pointing and how fast it's moving, to the centimetre, at speed, and keeps knowing when the signal drops. Formula 1 is the most watched test of precision at speed there is, built under hard limits: capped budgets, capped computing. You wouldn't be borrowing a story; you'd be explaining one people already love to watch.
>
> **Two, the room, where your revenue is.** Most of your revenue comes from the US and Europe, and this sport reaches both: three US races, two American teams, Apple broadcasting it in the US, and race weekends across Europe. Plus a home race in Melbourne where you can host Australian customers and partners with real pride.
>
> **Three, people and pride.** You're hiring engineers fast, and this is the sport engineers grow up watching. An Australian company standing on this stage says something to every graduate you want, and to every customer abroad who's still learning your name.
>
> One honest note on size. You're growing fast, but the money you raised in March is for factories, people and acquisitions, not marketing, and a standard team seat is a big number next to that. So whatever we bring back starts small: content, hosting, and a supplier role only where your product does real work. And if the honest answer is 'not yet', we'll say exactly that."
