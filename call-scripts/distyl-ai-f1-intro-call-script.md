In March 2026 it signed a multi-year extension (it remains Official Partner of the Las Vegas Grand Prix) and put an Agentforce AI agent on F1.com to answer fans' questions on the 2026 rules. In June 2026 Racing Bulls (VCARB) took Agentforce 360 into the team for fan engagement, garage screens and Paddock Club hospitality logistics. Why they're in: to show its AI agents doing real work in public, for a demanding customer, and to host buyers at the race. For Distyl: the same story Distyl tells, AI doing real work with people in charge, is already being told in the sport by a much bigger company. That shows there's appetite, and that the AI agent space isn't empty.
- **Airwallex at McLaren (stat bank).** Who and what: a B2B payments scale-up. Why: pipeline and trust with business buyers in new markets. Result: revenue up 50% year on year across EMEA and the Americas during the partnership; UK revenue up 57% from new customer acquisition. For Distyl: a growth-stage B2B company, not a household name, tied F1 to measurable new business.
- **RUCKUS (CommScope) at Haas (stat bank).** Who and what: enterprise wireless powering the team's operations, shown live in the garage; SPORTFIVE ran the grid-wide process. Why: a working role makes the product the story, for IT buyers. For Distyl: the model for a working role, if a team has a real problem Distyl's approach could help with (an idea to test, never a claim).

*[Background if they ask who else is around: enterprise tech is well represented. Oracle (Red Bull title partner), Atlassian (Williams title partner), Google Cloud (McLaren's official cloud partner, and Distyl's partner since April), AWS (F1's official cloud and machine learning provider since 2018), Microsoft with Mercedes (reported January 2026), and Cognizant and ServiceNow around the sport.]*

> "I think what's interesting is that most of the tech companies in Formula 1 aren't selling to fans. They're selling to companies, a lot like you. Salesforce is probably the closest to what you do. It's put its AI agents to work for the sport and for one of the teams, in public, and it hosts its customers at the races. Then there's a smaller one like Airwallex with McLaren, a payments business, and they tied it to new business, revenue up 50% across EMEA and the Americas during the partnership. And there's the working version, like RUCKUS with Haas, where the product runs part of the team and that becomes the story. ## 5. If they ask: "How are similar brands activating?"

> "There are four or five ways, and most brands pick two.
>
> The first is a working role. RUCKUS with Haas is a good one: their wireless runs the team's operations, and they show it live in the garage. Salesforce has gone the same way with Racing Bulls, its AI is on the garage screens and helps run the hospitality.
>
> Second is the room. F1 fans index at 160 for business decision makers, the highest in sport, and 66% of Paddock Club guests are senior executives, a lot of them CIOs and CTOs. Passes work all three days, so seven passes can host twenty different guests in one weekend. That's how Airwallex used McLaren, and they tied it to revenue.
>
> Third is content. F1 is number one in sport on social, and what works best is how the team works behind the scenes, which suits a brand with something real to show.
>
> Fourth is drivers. A driver program is smaller and more personal, good for hosting and content, less so for a technical story.
>
> And fifth is picking your races. For a US business there are three, Miami, Austin and Las Vegas, and you can build the whole year around those."

**Ideas for Distyl (ideas only, not proposals):**

- **A "person in charge" weekend at a US race:** host the operating leaders Distyl sells to (claims, care, supply chain) across one weekend, show them how a team makes decisions under pressure, then a Distyl session in the style of their demo nights. It builds on what they already do.
- **A content series on the human in the loop:** the strategy wall and race engineers making calls with software, next to a Distyl engineer doing the same inside a big company. Needs a team willing to open the doors.
- **Engineer hiring:** a campus or engineering challenge with a team's name on it, if hiring forward deployed engineers is a priority.

## 7. If they ask: "Where could our product fit in the team?"

*[Ideas to test with a team's technical department, never claims. Don't say or imply any team uses or needs Distyl.]*

- **Factory operations and supply chain:** a team designs, makes and changes thousands of parts through a season under a cost cap. Distyl's best-known result is faster root-cause analysis on supply chain problems for a hardware maker (their claim). Plausible spot: helping the operations team trace why a part is late or failed, with a person signing off.
- **Engineering knowledge and the rulebook:** Distillery's core, the Context Mesh, is built to hold an organization's knowledge, policies and workflows (their description). Plausible spot: helping engineers find past decisions, reports and the relevant regulations fast. Salesforce built a fan-facing version for the 2026 rules on F1.com.
- **Commercial operations and guest service:** Distyl does contact centre and customer care work (their claim). Plausible spot: partner and guest servicing, hospitality logistics, ticketing and merchandise support. Salesforce now does hospitality logistics with Racing Bulls, so check what each team already has.
- **What the role could look like:** an "Official Enterprise AI Partner" (the label in the email) or a technical supplier seat, with a small Distyl team embedded on one concrete process, measured, then told as a story. Product plus cash, sized to Distyl.

> "I'd be careful not to guess. But if you look at how a team runs, there are a few places where what you do could fit. The factory's one: thousands of parts, changing all season, and a lot of time spent working out why something's late or broke. The engineers' knowledge is another, years of reports and decisions, and a rulebook that keeps changing. And then the commercial side, the guests, the partners, the fans. Whether any of that's real is a question for a team's technical people, and that's the kind of thing we'd go and test for you."

*[The objection to expect: most of Distyl's work is anonymous.]*
