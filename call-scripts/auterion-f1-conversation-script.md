## 1. Brand intelligence

- **What they do (verified).** Software for drones and robots. The core is AuterionOS, which they call "the operating system for autonomous robots". It's one software layer that runs drones and uncrewed vehicles in the air, on land and at sea, from lots of different manufacturers, so one operator can run many machines at once. They also sell Skynode flight computers, mission and fleet software, and Nemyx, their engine for getting many drones from different makers to work together (launched September 2025).
- **Origin (verified).** Spun out of ETH Zurich. Most sources say it was founded in 2017, though their own boilerplate has said 2018. The founders were Lorenz Meier (founder and CEO) and Kevin Sartori (left in 2021). As a student and researcher, Meier created PX4, the open-source autopilot, as well as MAVLink and the Pixhawk hardware standard. Those open standards now sit with the Dronecode Foundation.
- **Where (verified, with one ambiguity).** HQ has been in Arlington, Virginia since 2024. Their site lists Munich as European HQ. Engineering teams are in Zurich and Munich, with people on the ground in Kyiv. In 2024 the company told swissinfo that its Zurich staff work only on the civilian business.
- **Scale (reported; attribute it).** Reuters, November 2025: about 100 million euros (around $115M) in annual revenue, about 160 employees, profitable in 2025. Resilience Media, 27 February 2026: on track to double revenue to around $200M on signed contracts, and weighing a raise of about $200M at a valuation above $1.2bn. The company said it was "not fundraising at the moment". Trackers put headcount near 180, with engineering hiring rising.
- **Funding (verified).** $10M seed in 2018 (Lakestar, Mosaic, Costanoa, Tectonic). $130M Series B in September 2025, led by Bessemer, with $25M of it from the US Office of Strategic Capital and a valuation reported "north of $600 million". Rheinmetall took a significant minority stake in November 2025 (Reuters).
- **Who buys (their claims plus reporting).** Mostly governments. Meier has said most revenue comes from the US government, and they also name Germany, Ukraine, Taiwan, the Netherlands and the UK. The commercial side is still on their site: mapping, inspection, and manufacturers who build on AuterionOS. Resilience Media (February 2026) says they still have commercial customers but no longer treat them as the first priority.
- **Recent news (for awareness only; all defense, none of it for the call).** They announced a joint venture with Ukraine's Airlogix in February 2026. Resilience Media (3 September 2026) reported a roughly $300M government contract for more than 5,000 systems built in Germany. A vertical launch system for Nemyx was announced on 5 October 2026, and an integration milestone with Project Q followed on 19 October 2026.
- **Sports and marketing history (our checks, 20 October 2026).** We found no sports sponsorships and no consumer campaigns. Their marketing looks like trade and industry events plus Meier's own writing (he has author pages at TIME and CEPA). This would be their first step into sport.
- **Likely priorities (our read, not their words).** Scaling production with partners, hiring engineers fast, and growing in Europe around Munich.

## 2. The strongest F1 connection

Auterion has spent its whole life on one problem: software that lets a machine make fast decisions on its own while a person stays in charge, and that gets lots of different machines working as one. A Formula 1 car is the most public example of that problem anywhere: full of sensors, run by software at the limit in real time, with a driver in control, a pit wall reading every signal live, and from 2026 an energy budget that is a software problem every lap. The realistic prize for Auterion isn't mass reach but the engineers they're competing to hire, plus a civilian, engineering-first story told where that kind of work gets taken seriously.

## 3. The conversation

>
> So, quickly, I look after brand partnerships in SPORTFIVE's Formula 1 business. We don't work for one team. We work right across motorsport, so really our job is helping a brand work out whether there's a sensible way in at all, and if so, where.

*[Bjorn and George: one line each, no pitching.]*

> "And today's just an open conversation. I'd love to hear about Auterion, I'll tell you why you came to mind, and we'll see if there's anything in it. If there isn't, that's completely fine."

### B. Why I reached out (45 to 60 seconds)

> "So, why you. I was reading about how Auterion started. Lorenz wrote an open-source autopilot as a student at ETH, and a big part of the drone world ended up building on it. That's quite a rare path.
>
> But what stuck with me was the problem you've been working on the whole time. Software that lets a machine make quick decisions on its own, with a person still in charge. And then getting lots of different machines, from different makers, to work as one.
>
> And I spend my days around Formula 1, and that's pretty much what an F1 car is. It's covered in sensors, it's run by software right at the limit, and it's all happening in real time. There's a driver in control and a whole team reading the data live on the pit wall. From this year there's a much bigger electric share too, so how the energy gets used every lap is a software problem.
>
> Different machine, same kind of problem. So I thought there was probably a more interesting conversation here than the usual sponsorship one."

### C. Why Formula 1 might be relevant (1 to 2 minutes)

> "I think there are maybe three reasons it could make sense for you. Tell me if any of them land.
>
> The first one's people. From what I can see, you're hiring a lot of engineers in Zurich, Munich and the US. This is the most engineering-obsessed sport there is, and the audience is young, too: 43% of fans are under 35. A lot of the robotics and software people you're trying to hire grew up watching it. So being in front of them there is a hiring tool as much as a brand one.
>
> The second is the story. What's interesting about a lot of the tech companies that have come into F1 lately is that it isn't really about the logo. It's about showing what their technology does, somewhere people understand performance. For you, I'd guess that's the civilian side: autonomy, the open-source roots, the engineering. Told by engineers, for engineers.
>
> And the third is the room. There are more than 350 partner companies around the grid, and race weekends are where a lot of that world meets. I'm thinking of the manufacturers who build on your software, industrial partners, people like that.
>
> But I don't want to get ahead of myself. There are a few ways in, through a team, a driver, content or hospitality, and which one makes sense depends on what you're trying to do. So I'd much rather hear from you first."

And if it's closed, you'll hear that from us straight away."

## 5. Discovery questions

**1. Priorities**

> "If I sat in on your leadership meeting this year, what would keep coming up?"

- Follow-up: "And where does the commercial side sit in all that? The mapping, the inspection, the manufacturers building on your software?"
- If they say scaling production or big contracts: *[Don't follow them into it. Bridge to people:]* "And does hiring keep up with all that growth?"

**2. Talent**

> "How hard is it to find the engineers you need right now? Where do you lose them, and who to?"

- Follow-up: "Is it more about getting known by the right people, or keeping the ones you've got?"
- If hiring is the pressure: "That's interesting, because it's probably the most natural fit we've got. Let me park it and come back to it."

**3. Who you need to win over (customers, decision makers, B2B or B2C)**

> "Who are the people you most need to win over? I'm guessing the ones who buy from you and the ones who build on your software are pretty different groups."

- Follow-up: "Put another way, if I gave you a paddock weekend tomorrow, who'd you bring?"
- *[They're B2B with no consumer business. If they say government buyers, note it and move on. See the hospitality caution in the prep notes.]*

**4. Markets**

> "Which markets matter most to you over the next few years? The US, Germany, Switzerland, somewhere else?"

- Follow-up: "Is Europe getting bigger for you now, with Munich?"
- If the US: "There are three US races now, and two American teams, Haas and Cadillac."
- If Europe: "A good chunk of the calendar is within easy reach of Munich and Zurich."

**5. Marketing and partnerships today**

> "What does marketing look like at Auterion at the moment? Trade shows, Lorenz's writing, something else? And is there anything you'd never do?"

- Follow-up: "Have you ever looked at sport, or anything like it, before?"

**6. Formula 1 familiarity**

> "And how much do you follow Formula 1, if at all? There's no wrong answer."

- Follow-up: "Anyone on the engineering team who's a big fan?"
- Light aside, if the mood's right: "One small thing you might enjoy: drones are part of how F1 gets filmed these days." *[Never link this to Auterion. We don't know whose hardware or software those drones run. If asked: "No idea whose kit it is, and I wouldn't guess."]*
- If they barely follow it: "Quick picture, then. The series is owned by Liberty Media, the FIA writes the rules, and there are eleven teams, each its own business with its own partners. So you can come in through the sport, a team or a driver, and there's content and hospitality around all of it."

**7. How you'd measure it**

> "If you did something like this, how would you judge it after a year? What would need to happen for you to say it was worth it?"

- Follow-up: "And who'd be doing the judging?"
- If they say hiring: "Then we'd measure hires and applications, not impressions."

**8. Decision process, budget and timing**

> "How would something like this get decided at Auterion? Who'd be involved, and is there a rough sense of budget or timing? Even a range helps, because it tells us which doors are worth looking at."

- Follow-up: "Is this a this-year thing or a next-year thing? Both are fine."
- Follow-up: "And are there any internal rules on sponsorship or hospitality we should design around from day one?"
- If there's no number: "No problem, you don't need one today. If we come back with ideas, you'll see a few sizes, including small ones."

## 6. Potential commercial opportunities

*[Ideas to explore, not proposals. All three depend on the defense category check, and all stay civilian.]*

- **"Machines at the limit" engineering content (driver or content program).** A short series with a team: how a race team runs a car in real time, next to how Auterion's engineers get many machines working as one. It would be built for hiring and told by engineers. It's the smallest and most flexible way in, and it serves the talent job directly. Zurich, where staff work only on the civilian side, is the natural base.
- **The engineers' weekend (hospitality-led program).** A small program at one or two European races for their best engineers, people they want to hire, and the industry partners who build on their software. Passes work all three days, so seven passes can host twenty different guests. It needs no logo and no public announcement. Aim it at engineers and industry, never at government officials.
- **An engineering-scoped team seat, later (team partnership).** An entry-level partnership with one team, scoped to engineering and talent, with the official title used in hiring and partner conversations. It only comes into play if the checks clear and the first two show it's worth it. We'd avoid a technical supplier angle, because we'd never assume a team needs their software.

Then in [two to three weeks] we come back with a few ideas at a few sizes, and if the answer's 'not yet', we'll say so.

> "Thanks again, [Name]. And if you're ever curious to see it up close, we'd love to have you and a couple of your engineers at a race. Speak soon."
