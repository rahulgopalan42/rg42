## Part 4: How Formula 1 works, and where it could fit (8 to 10 minutes)

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Let me give you a simple picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains the options a brand has.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company. They own the commercial side: the calendar, the broadcast deals, the races.
>
> The FIA is the regulator. They write the rules.
>
> And then there are eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac. Each team is an independent business with its own owners and its own partnerships.
>
> So a brand can partner at three levels: with the sport itself, with a team, or with a driver personally. They come in very different sizes, which matters for a company at your stage, and I will be straight about that as we go."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Think of it in five parts.
>
> **One, branding.** Your logo on the car, the race suits, the garage. Around 24 race weekends a year. Last season the total audience was 1.8 billion, and the average race reached 76 million people.
>
> **Two, the official title.** You become, for example, the Official Energy Intelligence Partner of a team. You use the team's name, logo and imagery in your own marketing and in every customer conversation.
>
> **Three, hospitality.** Paddock access, the Paddock Club, garage tours, time with the drivers. 66% of Paddock Club guests are senior executives.
>
> And one detail people miss. A Grand Prix runs over three days, and your passes work every day. Seven passes can host twenty different guests in one weekend.
>
> **Four, content and digital.** Access to the team's channels and drivers for content. Formula 1 is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **Five, people.** Driver appearances, filming days, hot laps for your top customers.
>
> And around all of this sits the business platform: every team carries 25 or more partners, more than 350 companies across the grid. That room is a market in itself."

### 4.3 The audience

*[B2B framing first for Emerald: their buyers are utilities, data center operators and AI companies. The wider audience is the bonus, not the point.]*

> "Now, who you reach. For Emerald I would be precise about this.
>
> **The buyers.** Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport: fans index at 160 for management and strategy roles, where the average person is 100. In the Paddock Club, 66% of guests are senior executives. For you that room includes the energy, infrastructure and technology leadership you sell to, and the technology wave on this grid, AWS, Atlassian, Oracle, HP, reportedly Microsoft, has pulled exactly your world into the paddock.
>
> **The engineering world.** This is the most engineering-obsessed sport on earth, won by simulation, data and efficiency under hard limits. The people who build and run serious infrastructure follow it because it is their own discipline at full speed.
>
> **The world, when you want it.** 827 million fans, young, global and online. A two-year-old company does not need mass reach to sell software to utilities. But when the Emerald name needs to mean something to policymakers, partners and future hires at the same time, this stage reaches all three at once.
>
> And your home market is the sport's fastest growing one: three US races, two American teams, Apple broadcasting."

### 4.4 Why this could fit Emerald

> "Let me share why we thought this conversation was worth having, from our side. Three reasons, and then you tell me if they hold.
>
> **First, this sport lives your thesis.** Formula 1 wins by getting more performance out of less energy, under hard caps: the money is capped, the computing for car design is capped, and in 2026 the cars run on 100% sustainable fuel with a much bigger share of electric power, with the whole sport committed to Net Zero by 2030. Nobody here can win by using more. They win by using better. That is, sentence for sentence, Emerald's pitch for the grid: more AI out of the same power. You would not be borrowing a story. You would be telling your own on a bigger stage.
>
> **Second, the room is your market.** Your customers are AI companies, data center operators and utilities, and your investor list reads like a Paddock Club guest list already: NVIDIA, Siemens, RWE, Samsung, Aramco Ventures. The energy world and the technology world both sit inside this sport, and the people who sign infrastructure deals sit in that room every race weekend.
>
> **Third, trust, at speed.** You are two years old and selling to the most conservative buyers on earth: utilities and grid operators. For a company like that, credibility is the product. A serious engineering stage transfers trust fast, the way it has for other young technology brands here. And it compounds: the same platform that impresses a utility also impresses a regulator, a partner and your next hundred engineers.
>
> One honest note on size. These are scarce seats: eleven teams, one seat per category, multi-year deals, and that keeps prices high. Emerald is young. So if we take this anywhere, it will be to the entries that make sense for where you are today, and we will say plainly if the honest answer is: not yet."
