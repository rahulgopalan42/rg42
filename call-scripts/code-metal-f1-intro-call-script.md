> "So there are a few main ways, and most brands mix two or three.

*[Ideas for Code Metal, clearly ideas, not proposals. All three depend on the defense category check and stay civilian.]*

- **Idea: "Same hardware, proven faster."** With a team's engineers, take a piece of their own non-car engineering code, move it to new hardware or a safer language, and show it running faster with proof it behaves the same. A short content series built on a real job, only if the team's technical department agrees one exists.
- **Idea: a small US race program.** A few passes at Austin or Las Vegas to host the car, chip and comms engineering leaders they're trying to win. Small door, tight guest list, pipeline measured.
- **Idea: an engineering-talent angle.** A motorsport edition of their research awards or hackathon, with a team's engineers as judges, aimed at the engineers they're hiring. Kept separate from their defense-linked events. Verify appetite with a team.

## 7. If they ask: "Where could our product fit in the team?"

*[Ideas to test with a team's technical department, never claims. Never say or imply any team uses or needs this.]*

- **Design and simulation (strongest).** Where a team has its own simulation and analysis code, Code Metal could move it onto newer hardware and speed it up, with proof the results don't change. That matters more under a cost cap and limits on aero computing.
- **Trackside data and comms.** Where a team runs its own analysis tools on garage and trackside hardware, or moves data between circuit and factory, faster verified code helps. The Advanced RF Group (radio and signal processing) may add a comms angle. Car-side electronics are tightly regulated (verify), so start with the team's own tools, not the car.
- **Factory test rigs and IT.** Rigs and test benches often run on specialised hardware. Moving older rig code onto new hardware, or older code into a memory-safe language like Rust, is the kind of job they describe.
- **What a role could look like.** An "Official AI Engineering Partner" (the email's wording) or a technical supplier seat: product plus cash, one real project, content built on it, and a few hospitality passes.

*[Prep note: any in-kind technical supply may count towards the team's cost cap, so check how the team would treat it before an offer takes shape (verify).]*

> "Straight answer, I don't know yet, and I wouldn't want to guess on a team's behalf. What I can tell you is where I'd look. Teams are engineering companies with a lot of their own code, they work under a spending cap, and there are limits on the computing they can use for aero. So the first place I'd test is simulation and analysis, getting more out of the same hardware. Then maybe the trackside and data side. That's a conversation with a team's technical people, and we'd only bring it to you if there's a real job there."
