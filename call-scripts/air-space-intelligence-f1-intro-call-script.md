## Boilerplate: what ASI does

- **About ASI:** ASI, formerly Air Space Intelligence, is a Boston-based AI company whose software predicts how complex physical operations will play out and helps people decide what to do, starting with flight. Its Flyways AI platform reads weather, winds, traffic and airspace limits and suggests flight routes that use less fuel and avoid congestion. Alaska Airlines, its first airline customer, says Flyways saved about 1.2 million gallons of fuel in 2023, and ASI also names Delta Air Lines and United Airlines as customers. In June 2026 the FAA picked ASI for a 12-year program worth up to $875 million to help modernise how US air traffic is managed, and the company says the same platform supports the US Air Force and other defense customers. ASI has about 165 employees (Boston Globe, July 2026). On 20 October 2026 it announced a $325 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation, led by Spark Capital with Andreessen Horowitz among its backers, and it says the money will take its AI into energy, logistics and defense.
- **In one line:** If someone asks on the call: "They make AI that helps airlines plan better flights. It looks at the weather, the wind and the traffic ahead and suggests routes that save time and fuel. And the FAA's just picked them to help modernise US air traffic control."

## 4. Questions to ask them

*[A conversation, not a form. Ask one, then stop and listen.]*

**1. Priorities**

> "You've just raised, and you've said the next step's taking this beyond aviation, into energy and logistics. How much of the next year is still about airlines and the FAA work?"

- *Follow-up:* "And what does a good year look like for the airline side? More carriers, or going deeper with the ones you've got?"
- *If they say the FAA rollout comes first:* "Makes sense. That probably decides how much bandwidth there is for anything like this."

**2. Markets**

> "Is it a US story for now? I saw you've got people in Europe. Are airlines outside the US part of the plan?"

- *Follow-up:* "Is there anywhere you feel less known than you'd like to be?"
- *If they say international airlines:* "That's interesting, because the sport's partner list already has some big international carriers in it. I'll come back to that."

**3. Buyers and decision makers**

> "Who signs for Flyways at an airline? Is it the head of operations, the network operations centre, the COO?"

- *Follow-up:* "If I gave you a paddock weekend tomorrow, who would you bring?"
- *If they name airline operations leaders:* "That's exactly the kind of list a hosting program gets built around."

**4. Marketing today**

> "From the outside it looks like you've let the customers and the work do the talking, and kept the brand quite quiet. Now there's a new name and a big raise. Is that changing?"

- *Follow-up:* "Have you done anything in sport before? And is there anything you'd never do?"
- *If they say they've just started on brand:* "Good timing, then. F1 works best as part of a plan, not as the whole plan."

**5. B2B, and the other audiences**

> "I'm assuming it's B2B all the way. But are there other people you need to reach? The engineers you're hiring in Boston, or the wider aviation world?"

- *Follow-up:* "How hard is it to get the people you want right now?"

**6. Measuring success**

> "If you did something like this, how would it get judged internally, and by whom? Pipeline, being known, hiring?"

**8. Decision, budget and timing**

## 8. If they ask: "Where could our product fit in the team?"

*[Ideas to test with each team's technical and operations people, never claims. Never say or imply any team, or F1, uses or needs Flyways, or say what any team uses to plan travel or freight.]*
