# Mariana Minerals: Follow-up Call After the Mercedes Meeting

## Call at a glance

- **Who:** Brandon Towers at Mariana Minerals (plus [co-founder], if they join). From SPORTFIVE: Rahul and George [add Bjorn if he joins].
- **Where we are:** we set up and ran the first direct meeting between Mariana and the Mercedes F1 commercial team (Stuart and colleagues). This is the debrief.
- **What this call is for:**
  - how Brandon felt the Mercedes call went, and his key takeaways
  - how we can support from here
  - filling the gaps in the brief, so it's sharp before anything goes back to Mercedes
  - George's questions
- **Three things to come away with:**
  1. Brandon's honest read of the Mercedes call, including any doubts.
  2. The value Mariana wants from this, ranked, in their own words.
  3. The missing brief items (success measures, budget range, timing, who decides) and an agreed next step with a date.
- **Tone:** casual, confident, curious. We're the agency running the process, and you're there to make it easy for them. Listen more than you talk.
- **Don't:** guess what Mercedes thought, promise terms or prices, or push for a decision on this call.

So I'd like to understand how we can support you from here. And I'd also like to fill in a few gaps in the brief, just so we've got something really sharp before anything goes back to Mercedes.

When he's finished his first reaction, ask the bigger question:

## 3. Sharp questions for Brandon

**Q1.**

*[Gets the objection out now, while you can still do something about it.]*

### B. The value to Mariana

**Q5.**

> "If this works, what's different for Mariana in a year? Is it new customers, new partners, hiring, or just being taken more seriously?"

*[Turns "value" into something you can measure.]*
- *Follow-up:* "If you had to pick one, which matters most?"

**Q6.**

> "On the partner side, are there two or three companies you'd most want to meet through Mercedes? Even just the type of company is fine."

*[Named accounts make the network story real, and give Mercedes something concrete to respond to.]*

**Q7.**

> "On the technical side, what would a good first project look like? Something small you could point to in six months."

*[Keeps "technical collaboration" practical, without getting into the engineering.]*

**Q8.**

> "And who else do you want this to reach? Your investors, the engineers you're hiring, people in Washington?"

*[Mariana is an American industrial story; check whether policy and talent are part of the aim.]*

### C. The brief gaps

**Q9.**

> "How will you and Turner judge whether this was worth it? What would you want to be able to show the board after year one?"

*[Success measures, in their words. This becomes the ROI framework.]*

**Q10.**

*[Budget. If he hesitates: "No problem. We'll show you options at a few levels, and you can see what each one gets you."]*

**Q11.**

> "On timing, is there a moment you'd want this lined up with? Lithium One coming on, a big customer, the next raise?"

*[Lithium One's commercial production is expected in 2027, per reports.]*

**Q12.**

> "Who else needs to be part of the decision, and what would they need to see from us?"

*[The decision process. It tells you what to build next: a one-pager, a business case, numbers.]*

### D. What happens next

**Q13.**

> "What would you want from Mercedes in the next conversation?"

*[Tells you what to go back and ask Mercedes for.]*

**Q14.**

> "Would it help to see one or two other teams side by side, so you know the Mercedes option is the right one? Or is Mercedes the clear lead for you?"

*[Shows we're on their side, not the team's. Don't push it if Mercedes is clearly the one.]*
