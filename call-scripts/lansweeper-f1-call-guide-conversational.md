## Questions they might ask, and how to answer

### "How relevant is Formula 1 to our target audience?"

> "I think there's actually quite a strong alignment, particularly from a B2B perspective.
>
> Formula 1 fans index at 160 for business decision-makers, with 100 being the general population. So essentially, they're 60% more likely than the general population to be business decision-makers.
>
> They also index at 126 when it comes to researching products online before making a purchase, which I think is quite relevant given the nature of your product and the fact that people can start using Lansweeper before necessarily engaging with your sales team.
>
> But beyond the audience demographics, there's also the international reach. You're looking at around 827 million fans globally, more than 20 race weekends across five continents, three races in the US and a significant presence across Europe.
>
> So, from a geographical perspective, there's quite a natural alignment with the markets you're operating in."

### "We sell to IT decision-makers, not Formula 1 fans. How is that relevant?"

> "Absolutely, and I think that's where you need to look beyond the traditional sponsorship model.
>
> Around two-thirds of Paddock Club guests are senior executives. You've also got more than 350 partner companies across the grid, many of which are bringing their own customers, prospects and leadership teams to races. When you look at those companies, there's quite a bit of overlap with the industries you're targeting.
>
> You've also got some of your publicly referenced customers already involved in the sport, whether that's Nestlé through KitKat's Formula 1 partnership or Sephora through F1 Academy.
>
> But what I think is particularly valuable is the quality of engagement. Normally, getting 30 minutes with a CIO or CISO can be quite challenging. Through Formula 1, you potentially have the opportunity to spend considerably more time with that person in a very different environment.
>
> And that's where I think the commercial value lies for a business like Lansweeper."

### "How are other technology companies leveraging Formula 1?"

> "There are actually some quite relevant examples for Lansweeper, particularly because many of these partnerships go beyond traditional branding."

>

**TeamViewer at Mercedes**

> "TeamViewer is probably one of the more established examples. They've been working with Mercedes since 2021, and what's interesting is that Mercedes was already a customer before the sponsorship came together.
>
> According to TeamViewer, the team is using the technology across thousands of IT and operational devices, both at the factory and trackside.
>

For an enterprise IT audience, having access to the person responsible for the technology infrastructure of a Formula 1 team is arguably more valuable than meeting a driver.

**ServiceNow at Aston Martin**

> "ServiceNow is another interesting example, particularly given your existing integration. They've been with Aston Martin since 2023, initially supporting the team's IT and facilities operations.
>

What's interesting is that the content wasn't necessarily about cybersecurity software. It was about taking a concept associated with their brand and bringing it to life through Formula 1.

**Keeper Security at Williams**

>

>

>
> For Lansweeper specifically, I think there's also an opportunity to simplify the way you communicate what you do. The message that you can't manage or protect what you can't see is something that can be brought to life quite naturally through Formula 1."
