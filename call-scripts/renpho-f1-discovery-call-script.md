## Part 4: How partnerships work, and the McLaren picture (8 minutes)

*[Calibrate to what they already know. George carries the McLaren detail; you carry the structure. Invite questions as you go: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Quickly, how the sport is set up, because it explains your options.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company. The FIA is the regulator. And then there are eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac. Each team is an independent business with its own owners and its own partnerships.
>
> So a brand can partner at three levels: with the sport itself, with a team, or with a driver personally. Most brands enter through a team, which is also where the McLaren conversation lives."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a team partner? Think of it in five parts.
>
> **One, branding.** Your logo on the car, the race suits, the garage. Around 24 race weekends a year. Last season the total audience was 1.8 billion, and the average race reached 76 million people.
>
> **Two, the official title.** You become, for example, the Official Recovery Partner of a team. You use the team's name, logo and imagery in your own marketing, packaging and campaigns.
>
> **Three, hospitality.** Paddock access, the Paddock Club, garage tours, time with the drivers. And one detail people miss: a Grand Prix runs over three days, and your passes work every day. Seven passes can host twenty different guests in one weekend.
>
> **Four, content and digital.** Access to the team's channels and drivers for content. Formula 1 is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **Five, people.** Driver appearances, filming days, your products in the drivers' hands, hot laps for your top partners and creators.
>
> And around all of this sits a business platform: every team carries 25 or more partners, more than 350 companies across the grid."

### 4.3 The McLaren picture, and the honest note

*[George can carry this. Keep it factual and warm. The honest note at the end builds trust.]*

> "On McLaren specifically, since that is the interest.
>
> This is the reigning champion team, twice over. McLaren won the Constructors' Championship in 2024 and again in 2025, their tenth, and Lando Norris is the reigning Drivers' Champion, with Oscar Piastri right behind him. It is one of the most loved stories in the sport right now.
>
> And it is a serious commercial platform. Mastercard became the team's title partner this season, and partners like Google Cloud sit alongside. We know the environment well: we built the Airwallex partnership at McLaren, a fast-growing tech brand, and during the partnership their revenue grew 50% year on year across EMEA and the Americas.
>
> One honest note, because you should hear it from us first. Success has a price. A double champion team commands the top of the market in every tier, and demand for it is the highest on the grid. That is not a reason to look away from McLaren. It is a reason to enter the conversation well prepared, with the full picture. That is exactly what we are for."

### 4.4 Why RENPHO fits this sport

> "Let me tell you why I find this opportunity interesting for RENPHO. Three reasons.
>
> **First, recovery is part of performance here.** Formula 1 drivers are elite athletes. They race 24 weekends a year, across five continents, through heat, time zones and enormous physical strain. How they sleep, recover and reset between sessions is a real performance topic in this sport. RENPHO is not borrowing a story here. Your products live exactly in that story.
>
> **Second, you are already going this way.** You partnered with Cooper DeJean around sleep and recovery. Formula 1 is that same idea at global scale: not one athlete in one market, but a team of athletes in front of 827 million fans across the whole world, all season.
>
> **Third, the audience is your customer.** F1 fans are young, 43% under 35, they live online, and they are the strongest shoppers in sport: number one for buying premium products, number one for researching products online, number one for brand loyalty. In the US, a key market for you, fans are 32% more likely to consider a sponsor's product, and the sport there is booming: three races, two American teams, Apple broadcasting.
>
> One last thing on value. Every team sells one seat per category, eleven teams for the entire world, deals of three to five years. Small supply, growing demand. That is why these partnerships hold their value."

### 4.5 Three activation ideas (examples, not proposals)

*[Offer these as sketches to make it concrete. Let them react; their reaction feeds the brief.]*

> "To make it concrete, three sketches. Early thoughts, nothing more.
>
> **One, the recovery room.** Your products inside the team's actual routine: the drivers' room, the trainers' kit, the factory gym. A supplier-style partnership where the team really uses RENPHO, and a content series around it: how an F1 driver recovers between 24 race weekends. Marketing you can demo is the most believable marketing there is.
>
> **Two, the travel story.** Follow the team through a triple-header, three races in three countries in three weeks, and tell the recovery story fans never see: the flights, the jet lag, the reset. Your products are the heroes of that story, and it is content the sport's audience truly wants.
>
> **Three, the fan side.** Team-branded product editions, race-weekend campaigns in your key markets, and app content built with the team: train and recover like a driver. The sport's fans are the best product-buyers in sport; give them something to buy."

### Q2. "What is the difference between partnering with F1 itself, a team, or a driver?"

> "Three doors into the sport.
>
> **The series, Formula 1 itself.** You partner with the sport: branding at every race, global reach, neutrality. Bigger deals, premium prices.
>
> **A team.** The story lives here: the car, the drivers, the garage, the content, the hospitality. Many more entry points and price levels. This is where the McLaren conversation lives, and for most brands it is the right door.
>
> **A driver personally.** The smallest door: personal channels, appearance days, campaign rights. For a recovery brand a driver deal can be a strong add-on, like your Cooper DeJean partnership, but it carries no team rights and no paddock hospitality.
>
> The brief will tell us which door, and at what level. They can also combine."

### Q3. "Teams have thirty partners. How does one brand stand out?"

> "Fair question. Three answers.
>
> First, structure. Partners sit in clear tiers, and your tier defines exactly what you own: positions, days, content, hospitality. All contractual.
>
> Second, exclusivity. Within your category you are alone. The team cannot sign another recovery brand next to you.
>
> Third, attention is won through activation, not logo size. Drivers really using your products, a recovery story fans have never seen, a product edition people can buy: these beat bigger logos every weekend.
>
> And the rights that matter most to you, the drivers, the content days, the access: those are private. They never get diluted by other partners."

### Q4. "Can a partnership really sell products, or is it just branding?"

> "It can sell, and this audience is unusually good at buying.
>
> F1 fans are number one in sport for buying premium products and number one for researching products online. In the US, fans are 32% more likely to consider a sponsor's product; among women it is 41%, and among Gen Z 39%.
>
> And the mechanics are concrete: team-branded product editions, campaigns using the team's name and imagery in your stores and channels, content with the drivers that links straight to product, and race-weekend retail moments in key markets.
>
> You track it like any campaign: sales lift around activations, traffic, conversion. We build that measurement into the plan from day one."

### Q5. "Which team would fit us best? Is McLaren the right answer?"

> "McLaren is a serious answer, and your instinct has good reasons behind it: the reigning double champions, the reigning drivers' champion, a strong commercial platform.
>
> What I will not do today is confirm it by guessing. The brief decides: your budget, your markets, your products, what the category picture looks like team by team, and what each team would offer you. Sometimes that work confirms the first instinct, and then you proceed with evidence. Sometimes it surfaces a route with better value for the same goals, and then you get to choose.
>

### Q6. "How is ROI measured in Formula 1?"

> "Four ways, usually.
>
> **Media value.** Independent firms like Nielsen track every second your branding appears, across TV and digital, and give it a money value against your fee.
>
> **Brand tracking.** Awareness and image shift, measured before and after: does the market see RENPHO as a serious recovery brand?
>
> **Sales.** Product editions, campaign lift, e-commerce traffic and conversion around activations. For a brand built on e-commerce, this is the most direct line.
>
> **Relationships.** Which retailers, distributors and partners you hosted, and what came of it.
>
> You agree the framework upfront and review it with the team every quarter."

### Q7. "Who owns Formula 1? How does the money work?"

> "Three layers, very simple.
>
> Liberty Media, an American media company, owns Formula 1 itself. They control the commercial rights: races, broadcast deals, the calendar.
>
> The FIA is the independent regulator.
>
> The eleven teams are independent businesses. Each team earns a share of the sport's revenues plus its own sponsorships.
>
> Since 2021 the sport has a cost cap, so teams became profitable, stable businesses. Team sponsorship has doubled since 2020, to 2.2 billion dollars a year. You would be entering a healthy, growing market."
