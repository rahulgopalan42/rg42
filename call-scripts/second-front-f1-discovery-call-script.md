# Second Front x SPORTFIVE: F1 Discovery Call Script

**Call type:** First call / discovery, listening-led. Goal: open warm and short, say why Second Front caught our eye, make the goal plain (understand if it makes sense, not sell), let them talk, fill the brief with sharp questions, and show how a sport built on strict rules and fast upgrades could fit a company whose whole job is getting software through the strictest approvals there are. Agree next steps only if the conversation earns them.

**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you'd say it out loud, contractions and all. Don't read it word for word; glance at the bold anchors and talk. The listeners are veterans and software operators who sell into government. They're direct, precise and allergic to hype, so keep it plain and short. Keep every spoken line about software, rules, speed and safety; this is not a defense pitch, and you never need to say a word about weapons or warfare. Let them talk more than you.

## Part 3: The smart questions (12 to 15 minutes)

*[Conversation starters, not a form. Ask one, stay quiet, follow what they give you. Their answers become the brief.]*

**The end goal**

**Evaluation and success**
- "If you ever did something like this, on what basis would it be judged internally, and by whom?"
- "What would a partnership of this kind need to deliver for you to call it a success after year one?"

**Buyers and customers**
- "On the software side, who signs: the founder, the CEO, the head of government sales? And on the government side, who's the person who has to say yes?"
- "Are there named companies you'd love to bring onto your platform next year? That list would shape anything we build."

**Markets**
- "Is the next chapter US-first, or is the UK and Europe push the big one? The calendar runs five continents, and the US alone has three races."

**People**
- "How hard is it to get great engineers to look at you? This is one of the few sports where the engineers are the heroes."

**Hospitality**

## Part 4: How Formula 1 works, and where it could fit (8 to 10 minutes)

*[Calibrate to their answer from Part 2. Keep it precise; this room hates fluff. Invite questions: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Quick picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains a brand's options.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company: the calendar, the broadcasts, the races. The FIA writes the rules and checks the cars against them. And then eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac, each an independent business with its own partners.
>
> So a brand can enter at three levels: the sport, a team, or a driver personally. They come in very different sizes, and for Second Front I'd be straight from the start: the interesting doors are the smaller ones."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Five parts, quickly.
>
> **Branding:** your name on the car, the suits, the garage, around 24 weekends a year, 1.8 billion total audience last season.
>
> **The official title:** Official Something Partner of the team, usable in all your own marketing and every sales conversation.
>
> **Hospitality:** paddock access and the Paddock Club, where 66% of guests are senior executives. One detail people miss: passes work all three days, so seven passes can host twenty different guests in a weekend.
>
> **Content:** access to the team's channels and drivers. This is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **People:** driver appearances, filming days, hot laps for your top customers.
>
> And around it all, the business platform: 25-plus partners per team, 350-plus across the grid. That room is a market in itself.
>
> You don't need all five. For a company your size, one or two of them, done well, is the smart way in."

### 4.3 The audience

*[Two audiences for Second Front: the founders and engineers who need to know them, and the senior leaders who sign.]*

> "Who you reach. For Second Front it's really two groups, and this sport holds both.
>
> **The builders.** This is the most engineering-obsessed sport on earth, and its audience skews young: 43% under 35. Software founders and engineers watch it because it's their own world at full speed: data, rules, deadlines. That's where you get known, and where you hire.
>
> **The decision makers.** Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport, index 160 against 100 for the general population. In the Paddock Club, two thirds of guests are senior executives, and for a software brand that means the CEOs, CIOs and CTOs of technology companies. The leaders of the software companies you want on your platform are in that room.
>
> And the stage already knows your partners. AWS is the sport's official cloud provider, Google Cloud partners McLaren, and Microsoft reportedly signed with Mercedes in January. The cloud companies you work with are already here."

### 4.4 Why this could fit Second Front

> "Why we thought this was worth a call. Three reasons, and you tell me if they hold.
>
> **One, speed inside the rules.** Your whole business says you can move fast and stay safe at the same time. Formula 1 is the most public proof of that idea anywhere. Budgets are capped, computing is capped, every new part has to be legal and pass the checks, and the team that gets through the rules fastest wins. You wouldn't be borrowing a story; you'd be pointing at the loudest example of your own.
>
> **Two, the right room.** Your growth depends on software leaders knowing you exist and trusting you with their most important deals. This sport gathers exactly those people, race after race, and the US has three races and two American teams.
>
> **Three, the moment.** You're growing into the UK and Europe, you've got serious investors behind you, and you're asking some of the most careful buyers in the world to trust a young company. Standing confidently next to serious engineering helps with that, if it's done in the right shape.
>
> One honest note. A full team partnership costs millions a year, and for a company your size that's a big bet. We won't pretend otherwise. Whatever we recommend will start small and sharp, and if the honest answer is 'not yet', we'll say exactly that."

### 4.5 Three early sketches (ideas, not proposals)

*[Offer as sketches; their reaction feeds the brief. All three are small doors on purpose.]*

> "To make it concrete, three sketches, all sized for Second Front on purpose.
>
> **One, how a team gets through the rules.** A content series on how a team designs a new part, proves it's legal, gets it through the checks and onto the car, race after race, without slowing down. The best 'speed inside the rules' story in the world, told in your voice, on your channels and your podcast. Built with a team or a driver, at content prices, not team prices.
