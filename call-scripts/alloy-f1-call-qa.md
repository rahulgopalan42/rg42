# Alloy x SPORTFIVE: F1 Call, Questions and Answers

**Call:** intro call with [Name, Job title] at Alloy. From SPORTFIVE: Rahul, Bjorn and George.

---

## The intro

> "Hi [Name], thanks for making the time. I'm Rahul, I look after brand partnerships in SPORTFIVE's Formula 1 business, and I've got Bjorn and George with me.
>
> Quickly on how we work: we don't sell for one team. We work across all eleven, we start from what the brand wants, and the teams pay our fee, so none of this costs you anything. Today's just a conversation."

### "So why did you reach out to us?"

> "Two reasons, really. First, your customers. The banks and payment companies you sell to are already all over the F1 paddock as partners. Mastercard's on the McLaren, Visa's on the Red Bull, Barclays is with Williams.
>
> Second, what you do. You decide in a split second whether someone is who they say they are. And that's pretty much how an F1 team works too. All weekend, they're deciding who and what to trust, and fast.
>
> And there's a bit of a trend here too. Security and identity brands are going quite big in F1 right now. Okta came in with McLaren last year, 1Password with Red Bull, Zscaler joined Aston Martin this summer, and ThreatLocker signed with Racing Bulls just last month. CrowdStrike's been with Mercedes since 2019. And they all say the same thing: a team runs on trusted data, so if it works there, it'll work for their customers.
>
> So it felt like a conversation worth having. But I'd much rather hear it from you. Where's Alloy at right now?"

## Questions they might ask, and how to answer

### "What are companies like us doing in F1?"

> "The closest to you are the identity and security brands. And what's interesting is they've all got a real job inside the team, not just a logo. Let me give you a few examples."

**Okta at McLaren.** When a McLaren fan logs in to the team's app, website or online shop, that login runs on Okta. McLaren needed it to hold up when millions of fans pile in on a race weekend, and Okta says it also protects those fans from identity fraud. McLaren's brand partners use the same login to book their Paddock Club tickets.

**1Password at Red Bull.** When a Red Bull engineer signs in to live car data, tyre data or weather models, at the track or at the factory, that access goes through 1Password. Its logo is on the drivers' steering wheel screens, and it runs a podcast with a former Red Bull mechanic.

**Keeper Security at Williams.** When someone at Williams needs admin rights on their laptop, Keeper gives them there and then and takes them away afterwards. Keeper had a fan zone stand at the Austin race, and this year it ran a global campaign fronted by Alex Albon.

**CrowdStrike at Mercedes.** CrowdStrike protects thousands of the team's computers at the factory and the track, and it's been there since 2019. What made it stand out was a 13-part video series where Mercedes specialists explained the car's safety kit, the halo, the helmet, the fire extinguisher, and linked each one to keeping a business safe.

**Bitdefender at Ferrari.** Bitdefender's threat data feeds straight into Ferrari's security team. It hosts its own customers at private events with Ferrari drivers and engineers. And in May it published research on scams aimed at F1 fans, from fake tickets to fake merchandise shops.

**Airwallex at McLaren.** Airwallex's payments run inside McLaren's finance team, and Airwallex says it saves them half a day a month on international payments. It also runs the payments on McLaren's merchandise platform for partners. Last month McLaren gave it a bigger role, as its AI finance partner from 2027.

**Mastercard at McLaren.** Mastercard picks a handful of cardholders for each of five races and gives them travel, a spot inside the McLaren garage and time with Lando and Oscar. Only Mastercard cardholders can enter.

**Visa at Red Bull and Racing Bulls.** Visa takes the cars to US cities on a show run tour, with offers for its cardholders and VIP hospitality. Its CMO says it uses F1 to "connect with fans, clients and cardholders", and those clients are largely the banks that issue its cards.

> "So for you, would it be more about the proof point, or about time with the banks you sell to?"

### "Why are these brands in F1?"

> "The security brands all say pretty much the same thing: a team runs on trusted data, so if it works there, it'll work for you. CrowdStrike's CEO called F1 'a highly data-intensive sport'. Zscaler's CMO said every car is a mobile data centre.
>
> The banks and payment companies are there for something else. Barclays, Visa and Standard Chartered all use the same word: clients. It's about time with their customers.
>
> For you, I'd guess it's a bit of both. The proof point, and the room."

### "Who are our competitors in F1, and which teams are available?"

> "From what's public, the identity and security brands are spread across most of the grid. Okta's with McLaren. 1Password's with Red Bull, Keeper's with Williams, CrowdStrike's with Mercedes, Bitdefender's with Ferrari, and Zscaler's with Aston Martin. Alpine, Racing Bulls and Haas have security partners too.
>
> For you, the ones that look most taken are McLaren, because of Okta, and the two Red Bull teams, because of Visa. Where we don't see an identity or fraud partner today is Cadillac, Audi and Haas. At Audi, Revolut's the title partner, so we'd check what that covers.
>
> One thing to bear in mind: fraud prevention is narrower than cybersecurity. So a team with a security partner might still have room for you. It depends on how their contracts define the category, and checking that is exactly what we do next.
>
> Which teams are you most interested in?"

### "It's very crowded. Won't we get lost? How do we stand out?"

> "It's a fair worry. Airwallex's marketing lead has said the same thing about McLaren. They sit next to Mastercard and Goldman Sachs, so they were never going to be the biggest logo.
>
> So instead, they put their product to work, inside McLaren's finance team. That gave them something real to talk about. And it worked: last month McLaren gave them a bigger role.
>
> For you, I'd take three things from that. Own one job, fraud and identity, not 'security' in general. Make it visible, with a real role people can see. And use the room for your own buyers, by hosting the fraud leaders from the banks and fintechs you sell to.
>
> Which of those feels most like Alloy?"

### "Why would a team want a partner like us?"

> "Tech's got pretty crowded in F1. It's now the biggest sponsorship category for the teams, and Reuters counted eight new AI deals in six months earlier this year.
>
> So what teams look for now is a partner with one clear job, and a story the paddock hasn't heard a hundred times. The security names mostly protect the team's own systems. Fraud's a different story, because it's about who a business lets in. And teams run real businesses with fans now: online shops, fan clubs, ticket ballots.
>
> Whether that seat's free at a given team is what we check properly. I'd rather do that than guess today."

### "What would a first deal look like for a company our size?"

> "Usually it's an official partner title in one category, at one team. Your product does a real job there, you get some branding on the car or the kit, a few race weekends of hospitality, and some content. Normally over a few years.
>
> That's how Keeper started at Williams and 1Password at Red Bull. And some brands start even smaller, with a couple of hospitality weekends, and grow from there."

### "What does it cost?"

> "It's a wide range. Entry-level and supplier positions start in the low single-digit millions a year. Official partner tiers sit above that, and hospitality or content programmes sit below. For you, I'd start with hospitality and content, and look at a partner role once we know which team fits."

### "Why go through SPORTFIVE rather than a team directly?"

> "You can go direct. The difference is a team will sell you what that team has. We worked exclusively with Mercedes and Ferrari for over twelve years, and we gave that up so we could work for the brand across all eleven teams. And the teams pay our fee, so you get the whole picture at no cost."

### "Which team would fit us?"

> "I don't know yet, and I'd be making it up if I named one now. That's what we do next. We look at where the category could work, who the other partners are, and the price, and come back with a short list."

### "How would we measure whether it worked?"

> "We'd agree that upfront, before anything's signed. For you, I'd guess it's pipeline: meetings with the right banks and credit unions, and what happens after a hospitality weekend. We can add brand tracking and media value if they matter to you."

### "Mastercard's our partner, and it's at McLaren. Does that help?"

> "It might. It could open a door, or Mastercard's deal might already cover your area, since they sell fraud tools too. We'd check what their rights include before we say anything to anyone."

### "We sell to banks, not fans. Isn't F1 a consumer thing?"

> "Fair question. The fans are consumers, but the paddock isn't. Two thirds of Paddock Club guests are senior executives, and the banks and payment companies on the grid are there for their clients. And if it doesn't add up for a business your size, I'll tell you."
