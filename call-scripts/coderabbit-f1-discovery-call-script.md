## Part 4: How Formula 1 partnerships work (8 to 10 minutes)

*[Calibrate to their answer from Part 2. If they know the sport, compress. Invite questions as you go: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Let me give you a simple picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains the options you have.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company. They own the commercial side: the calendar, the broadcast deals, the races.
>
> The FIA is the regulator. They write the rules.
>
> And then there are eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac. Each team is an independent business with its own owners and its own partnerships. Some belong to car makers, like Ferrari, Mercedes and Audi. Some to consumer giants, like Red Bull. Some to investors.
>
> So a brand can partner at three levels: with the sport itself, with a team, or with a driver personally. Most brands enter through a team, and I can explain why when we get there."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Think of it in five parts.
>
> **One, branding.** Your logo on the car, the race suits, the garage. Around 24 race weekends a year. Last season the total audience was 1.8 billion, and the average race reached 76 million people.
>
> **Two, the official title.** You become, for example, the Official AI Code Review Partner of a team. You use the team's name, logo and imagery in your own marketing and sales conversations.
>
> **Three, hospitality.** Paddock access, the Paddock Club, garage tours, time with the drivers. 66% of Paddock Club guests are senior executives, and for a technology brand that room includes the CIOs and CTOs you sell to.
>
> And one detail people miss. A Grand Prix runs over three days, and your passes work every day. Seven passes can host twenty different guests in one weekend, and hundreds across a season.
>
> **Four, content and digital.** Access to the team's channels and drivers for content. Formula 1 is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA. That is where the developer audience lives.
>
> **Five, people.** Driver appearances, team bosses at your events, filming days, hot laps for your top customers.
>
> And around all of this sits the business platform: every team carries 25 or more partners, more than 350 companies across the grid, meeting each other all season. That room is your pipeline."

### 4.3 The audience

*[Two-level B2B framing for CodeRabbit: the developers who adopt, and the executives who sign.]*

> "Now, who you reach. For CodeRabbit I see two audiences, and this sport covers both.
>
> **The developers who adopt you.** F1 fans are young and live online: 43% are under 35, 61% of surveyed fans engage with F1 content every day, and the sport is number one in the world for social engagement. And this is the most engineering-flavored sport on earth. It is won by simulation, data and split-second software. Developers do not just watch it. They respect it.
>
> **The executives who sign the contract.** Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport: fans index at 160 for management and strategy roles, where the average person is 100. In the Paddock Club, 66% of guests are senior executives, and for a technology partner that means CIOs, CTOs and CISOs. The person who approves a company-wide CodeRabbit contract is, quite literally, in that room.
>
> And your home market is the sport's fastest growing one. The US has three races, two American teams, Apple broadcasting the sport, and purchase consideration up 32%."

### 4.4 Why this could fit CodeRabbit

> "Let me tell you why I find this opportunity interesting for CodeRabbit. Three reasons.
>
> **First, the grid became a software showcase.** Atlassian, Oracle, HP, and reportedly Microsoft did not buy small logos. They put their names on the teams. When the biggest software companies in the world choose the same stage, they move their whole market's attention there. A partnership puts CodeRabbit on that stage as a peer, at a fraction of what the giants pay.
>
> **Second, one platform reaches both of your audiences.** Most marketing makes you choose: developer channels or executive channels. This sport is the rare place with both. The developers are in the audience. The executives are in the Paddock Club. One partnership, both jobs.
>
> **Third, a real working role.** These teams ship software under race-day deadlines, where a bad change costs positions or worse. Imagine the partnership where a team's own engineers use CodeRabbit and say so. We have built exactly that shape before: RUCKUS at Haas is a partner whose product runs the team's operations, with a live showcase in the garage. Marketing you can demo is the most believable marketing there is.
>
> One last thing on value. All of this sits on very small supply: eleven teams for the entire world, one seat per category, three to five year deals. Small supply, growing demand. That is why these partnerships hold their value."

### 4.5 Three activation ideas (examples, not proposals)

Seven passes host twenty guests in a weekend. For the people you most want to win, this is the invitation of the year."
