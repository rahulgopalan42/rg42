## Boilerplate: what Rillet does

**About Rillet:** Rillet is an AI-native ERP and accounting platform for finance teams: the general ledger, the month-end close, revenue recognition, consolidation and reporting in one system, with AI agents doing the routine work inside the ledger and people approving it. It was founded in 2021 by CEO Nicolas Kopp, an LSE-trained accountant who worked at Morgan Stanley and led N26's US business, and CTO Stelios Modes, who built N26's payments infrastructure, and it launched publicly in 2024. It sells to CFOs and controllers at fast-growing companies, from venture-backed start-ups up to public companies, and says more than 600 businesses run on it, including Mercor, Neuralink and Skild AI, most of them moving off NetSuite, Oracle, SAP, Workday or Sage. In August 2026 it raised a $100M Series C led by ICONIQ at a $1B valuation, taking total funding past $200M from backers including Sequoia and Andreessen Horowitz, and it has an alliance with EY and partnerships with more than half of the Accounting Today top 20 CPA firms. Its stated goal is to "take the month out of month-end", and it's now pushing into bigger companies and new sectors such as healthcare, biotech, fintech and logistics.

**In one line, if someone asks on the call:** "They make the accounting system finance teams run their books on, built so the books close as you go, with AI doing the routine work and people signing it off."

## 8. If they ask: "Where could our product fit in the team?"

*[Ideas to test with a team's finance and technical people, never claims. Never say or imply any team uses or needs Rillet. The plain picture: it's a back-office fit, real as a story, and some teams already have big finance software names in place.]*

- **The finance team and the close (most plausible):** every team has a finance function that closes its books, and since 2021 those books also have to stand up to the FIA's cost cap review. A faster close, a clean audit trail and agents doing the routine entries map onto that world. As a story it fits; as a need, it's untested.
- **Cost cap evidence (second, harder):** the cap means sorting every cost into in or out, with evidence. An auditable ledger could help, but the FIA's Financial Regulations are specialist and Rillet would have to show it can handle them. Also check Rillet's fit beyond US GAAP, since many teams are based in the UK and Europe.
- **Partner revenue (third):** teams run 25 or more partner contracts, many over several years, and revenue recognition on contracts like that is the kind of thing Rillet automates for software companies. An idea to test, nothing more.
- **Not a fit:** design and simulation, the factory floor, trackside operations, logistics, comms and fan platforms. Those aren't Rillet's world.
- **Who's there already:** Oracle has said Red Bull runs finance planning on Oracle Fusion; SAP said in September 2025 that Mercedes is moving to SAP Cloud ERP ahead of the 2026 financial regulations; Epicor was named AlphaTauri's Official ERP Partner in 2021 (current status at Racing Bulls not confirmed). So replacing a big team's whole ERP is unlikely. A defined job, or a team without a finance software partner, is more realistic.
- **What a role could look like:** "Official Accounting Partner" (the email's term), with the product doing a defined job in the finance function (the close, one entity, the partner revenue), plus the cost cap content story. Product plus cash, sized at supplier level.

> "I'll be straight with you, this isn't a car part. But every team's got a finance department, and since the cost cap came in, those numbers get checked by the FIA every year, and getting them wrong can cost you development time. So a faster close and a clean audit trail mean something to a team. Some of the big teams already have Oracle or SAP in place, so I wouldn't go in talking about replacing their whole system. I'd look for one defined job, like the close or a part of the business, and test it with a team's finance people before anyone promises anything."
