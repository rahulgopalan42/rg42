### "Could we make and sell team or F1 eyewear? How does licensing work?"

> "In principle you could, and for you it's the most interesting part. But it's a separate deal from a sponsorship, so let me explain the two.
>
> A sponsorship buys the right to be associated with the team and to market that: a title, your name in agreed places, days with the drivers, and guests at races. A licence is different. It's the right to make and sell products that carry the team's name and marks. For a licence you usually pay a royalty on what you sell, often with a guaranteed minimum. So the team earns something even if the product sells slowly.
>
> In eyewear the two often come together. Ferrari's announcement of its Ray-Ban renewal says the licence and the sponsorship were both expanded. Oakley did it the other way round at Aston Martin, with four years as a supplier before its first licensed product. And SunGod started at McLaren purely as a licensee.
>
> A licence usually spells out five things: the products, the countries, the number of years, the royalty and minimum, and approvals. A licence can be limited to certain countries, so you could ask for Asia only. Approvals matter, because the team signs off each design before it's made, and usually the packaging and the adverts too. So I'd plan in months, not weeks.
>
> On who signs: a team licence is agreed with the team, and teams have their own licensing people. Aston Martin has a head of licensing and merchandise, for example. If you wanted the F1 name itself, that's a separate licence from Formula 1's own licensing programme. A driver's name, number or face is separate again. It needs the driver's own agreement, on top of the team's. Formula 1's own guidelines send those requests to the team or the driver's management.
>
> The good news is that you know this world. Your November letter says your licensed collections are mostly exclusive to your markets, and designed and made by you. So this would be a new licensor, not a new skill.
>
> What I can't tell you yet is which team could grant one. Where a team already has an eyewear licensee, I'd expect that to be exclusive. So we'd check that before anything else."

### "Are there rules on an Indian company doing a deal like this?"

>
> A licence is a different kind of payment. Royalties and licence fees paid abroad have their own paperwork, and tax usually has to be withheld, depending on the treaty with the other country. ### "What's the biggest risk?"

> "Signing a logo with no job. Most teams have 25 or more partners, and the ones that get lost put a name on the car and stop there. For an eyewear brand, the job is usually a product fans can buy. A deal needs a real job, a plan for your stores, and someone on your side who owns it.
