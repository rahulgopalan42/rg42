# Kastle Systems x SPORTFIVE: F1 Discovery Call Script

**Call type:** First call / discovery, listening-led. Goal: open warm and short, say why Kastle caught our eye, make the goal plain (understand if it makes sense, not sell), let them talk, fill the brief with sharp questions, and show how this sport could fit a company whose whole business is deciding who gets through which door. Agree next steps only if the conversation earns them.

**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you'd say it out loud, contractions and all. Don't read it word for word; glance at the bold anchors and talk. This is a practical, numbers-first services company that sells to building owners and property managers. They'll smell a reach pitch a mile off, and their chairman knows sport from the owner's side, so keep it plain, concrete and short. Let them talk more than you.

## Part 3: The smart questions (12 to 15 minutes)

*[Conversation starters, not a form. Ask one, stay quiet, follow what they give you. Their answers become the brief.]*

**Open the story**
- "Tell me where Kastle is right now, in your own words. What's going well, and what's the next hill to climb?"
- "What are the key focus areas for the next twelve months? What comes up in every leadership meeting?"

**The direction, and where this could play a role**
- "How do you see something like Formula 1, or sport at all, maybe playing a role in where the brand is headed? A rough first instinct is fine; there's no wrong answer."
- "And as a business, where could you imagine it adding value: bigger portfolio deals, the brand story, your customers' tenants, hiring?"

**The enterprise push**
- "Haniel said customers want one phone call to take care of all of it. When a big owner decides to consolidate, who makes that call: the property manager, the asset manager, the CFO? And what tips it your way?"
- "Is the hardest part of that sale being known by the right person, or being trusted by them?"

**The end goal**
- "Zooming out: if everything goes right over the next three years, what does Kastle look like?"
- "And the other side: what would make a partnership like this a failure in your eyes? That answer usually tells us more than the first one."

**Evaluation and success**
- "If you ever did something like this, on what basis would it be judged internally, and by whom?"
- "What would a partnership of this size need to deliver for you to call it a success after year one?"

**Customers and buyers**
- "Where's the growth coming from: Class A office, apartments, enterprise campuses, data centers through i2G?"
- "Are there named owners or portfolios you'd build a program around? If there's a list of twenty you'd love to sit next to, that steers everything we do."

**Markets**
- "Is this a US story, or does Australia come into it? And which metros matter most? The three US races sit in very different markets."

**Brand and marketing today**
- "The Barometer gave Kastle a voice most security companies don't have. What does marketing focus on beyond that, and what's working?"
- "Is the bigger job right now top of the funnel or bottom of the funnel: being known by owners who've never heard of you, or winning and expanding the portfolios already in play?"

**Budget and timing**
- "The unromantic question: is there a budget range in mind for something like this? I ask early because it decides which doors we even look at, and it saves us both time."
- "And timing: is this a now conversation or a next-year conversation? Both are fine; they just change what we bring back."

**Decision process**
- "Apart from you, who'd be part of this decision? Haniel, Mark as chairman, the new leadership team?"

**Hospitality**
- "Last one, my favorite. If I gave you a paddock weekend tomorrow, who would you bring, and why those people? That answer usually tells me what a partnership is really for."

## Part 4: How Formula 1 works, and where it could fit (8 to 10 minutes)

*[Calibrate to their answer from Part 2. Practical room: keep it concrete, keep the global numbers light, lean on the US and the room. Invite questions: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Quick picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains a brand's options.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company: the calendar, the broadcasts, the races. The FIA writes the rules. And then eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac, each an independent business with its own partners.
>
> So a brand can enter at three levels: the sport, a team, or a driver personally. They come in very different sizes, and for Kastle I'd be straight from the start: the interesting doors are the right-sized ones."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Five parts, quickly.
>
> **Branding:** your name on the car, the suits, the garage, around 24 weekends a year, 1.8 billion total audience last season.
>
> **The official title:** Official Something Partner of the team, usable in all your own marketing and every sales conversation.
>
> **Hospitality:** paddock access and the Paddock Club, where 66% of guests are senior executives. One detail people miss: passes work all three days, so seven passes can host twenty different guests in a weekend.
>
> **Content:** access to the team's channels and drivers. This is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA.
>
> **People:** driver appearances, filming days, hot laps for your top customers.
>
> And around it all, the business platform: 25-plus partners per team, 350-plus across the grid. That room is a market in itself."

### 4.3 The audience

*[Two groups for Kastle: the people who sign, and the people who carry the badge. Keep the global numbers short; the US is the point.]*

> "Who you reach. For Kastle I'd be precise, because you don't need the whole world.
>
> **The people who sign.** Formula 1 has the highest concentration of business decision makers in sport, index 160 against 100 for the general population. In the Paddock Club, two thirds are senior executives. That's the profile of the people who sign for you: owners, asset managers, CFOs. And with hospitality rights you don't hope they show up; you invite the ones you want.
>
> **The people who carry the badge.** The fans skew young and well-off: 43% under 35, number one in sport for buying premium, and joint number one for high income. That's the tenant your customers are fighting for, the one they want back in the office and signing the lease on the nicer apartment.
>
> **The US, right now.** Three US races, two American teams, Apple broadcasting, and US purchase consideration up 32%. Your home market is where this sport is moving fastest."

### 4.4 Why this could fit Kastle

> "Why we thought this was worth a call. Three reasons, and you tell me if they hold.
>
> **One, the paddock is your product at full speed.** Every person there wears a pass. Every pass opens some doors and not others. Guests, crews, media, drivers, all moving through the same tight space, and it has to just work. That's the job you do for an office tower every day. You wouldn't be borrowing a story; you'd be pointing at the most famous example of your own.
>
> **Two, the room is your buyer list.** Your buyers are a small, specific group, and this sport puts people like them in one place three times a year in the US. Seven passes can host twenty of them in a weekend. A season of hosting the right owners can do more than a year of trade shows.
>
> **Three, the trust moment.** Haniel put it well: the question your buyers ask now is 'who do I trust to run this for me?' When a big owner hands one company the keys to the whole portfolio, they pick the name they trust. Standing confidently on a serious stage, next to some of the biggest B2B names in the world, builds that trust quickly.
>
> One honest note on size. Your buyers are mostly American and fairly few, so most of this sport's global reach is waste for you, and team seats are priced for that reach. Whatever we recommend will start with the room and the story, sized for Kastle. And if the honest answer is 'not yet', we'll say exactly that."

> "Four ways, and you agree the framework upfront.

>
> **Business pipeline:** introductions, meetings and portfolios signed through the platform. That's how Airwallex could point at 50% revenue growth during their McLaren years.
>
> **Content performance:** reach and engagement on everything you build.
>
> For Kastle I'd weight it hard to pipeline. If the job is bigger portfolio deals, we measure portfolio deals, not impressions."

### Q4. "Which entry would fit us best?"

### Q8. "Our buyers are a few thousand landlords and property managers, almost all in the US. Why would we pay for 827 million fans around the world?"
