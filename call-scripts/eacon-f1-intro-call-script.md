## 1. Brand snapshot (not spoken)

- **What they do:** autonomous driving for open-pit mines. Their ORCASTRA system makes haul trucks driverless, on new trucks or retrofitted onto existing ones from any maker, and runs dispatching and charging for the whole fleet. (verified: IM, Global Mining Review)
- **Who they are:** EACON Group (易控智驾), listed in Hong Kong as HKEX 7687 on 8 July 2026. Founded in Beijing in May 2018. Headquarters moved to Shanghang, Fujian in December 2024, with R&D bases in Beijing, Shanghai and Zhengzhou, a Perth office, and subsidiaries in Australia and Singapore. Wason Lan is founder and CEO. (verified)
- **Scale:** revenue of RMB 1.44bn in 2025 (about $211M), up 45.5%. Still loss-making, with a net loss of about RMB 516M in 2025. The IPO raised about HK$2.3bn, for a market value of about HK$13bn at listing. (verified: CnEVPost, IM)
- **Fleet:** 2,580 active autonomous trucks at the end of 2025, ranked first in China (prospectus, via press). Since then they say they've automated more than 3,000 trucks across about 40 projects (August 2026), and that more than 1,500 of them are now battery-electric (September 2026). (their claim)
- **Customers:** CHN Energy, Zijin Mining, Shougang, Baowu and SPIC in China; Thiess, Norton Gold Fields and MACA in Australia. Backers include CATL, which led the Series D in 2025, and Zijin Mining. IPO cornerstones included Fidelity, JPMorgan Asset Management and Barings. (verified)
- **Recent news:** in July 2026, six retrofitted Komatsu HD1500 trucks started running day shifts with no safety drivers at a gold mine near Kalgoorlie, WA. In August 2026 their autonomy went onto the AT150, a cabless electric truck that drives in either direction, built with NHL and SPIC. In September 2026 they signed an MoU with Kazakhstan's mining and metallurgy association. (their announcements)
- **Priorities:** going international. About 23% of the IPO proceeds are earmarked for overseas expansion and winning customers (reported). Target markets named in reports are Australia, Canada, Chile, Mongolia, the Middle East and Africa, and one analysis adds Indonesia and Peru. (per reports)
- **Sports and marketing history:** none found. Their marketing today is mining trade shows (MINExpo, Electric Mining, the China International Mining Conference) and international press releases.
- **In plain English:** "They make mining trucks drive themselves. These are big haul trucks in real mines, in China and now Australia, and more and more of them are electric."

## 4. If they ask: "Why are brands like us in Formula 1?"

**Komatsu at Williams: mining equipment, and the best-known name in autonomous haulage**

- **Who and what:** Komatsu signed a multi-year deal to become Williams' Principal Partner from the 2024 season, as its Official STEM and Early Careers, Esports and Construction Machinery Partner. It still appears on the Williams partner page (checked 25 October 2026). It also launched a Komatsu-Williams Engineering Academy for ten STEM students (announced September 2024).
- **Why they're in:** Komatsu says it's about technology, reliability and developing the next generation of engineers. There's history as well: Komatsu backed Williams in the 1990s title years.
- **What it means for EACON:** the biggest name in EACON's category already uses F1 to talk about technology and talent. It also means Williams is probably not EACON's door (see prep notes).

**Ma'aden at Aston Martin: a mining company**

- **Who and what:** Saudi mining group Ma'aden became Aston Martin's first ever Principal Partner in February 2025, after first coming on board in late 2024. It runs an "Unearth Your Greatness" campaign covering content, university and internship programmes, and recruitment.
- **Why they're in:** as the team announced it, to attract talent for a technology-led generation of mining, build international awareness and reach younger audiences.
- **What it means for EACON:** a miner is already telling the story that mining is becoming a technology business, and telling it in F1. EACON would be telling the same story from the technology side.

**Airwallex at McLaren: a B2B company growing outside its home region**

- **Who and what:** B2B payments. During the partnership, revenue grew 50% year on year across EMEA and the Americas, and UK revenue grew 57% from new customers.
- **Why they're in:** B2B pipeline, and credibility in markets where people didn't know the name yet.
- **What it means for EACON:** it's the closest commercial model for EACON's next step: a newer name, going international, that needs buyers in new countries to take it seriously.

**Spoken answer:**

> "I think it's less about the logo on the car and more about who you get in front of. If you look at your own world, Komatsu's been Williams' principal partner for a few seasons now, and a lot of what they talk about is technology and bringing young engineers into the industry. Ma'aden, the Saudi mining group, did something similar with Aston Martin, very much about showing mining as a technology business. Then on the B2B side, Airwallex used McLaren to grow outside its home region, and its revenue across Europe and the Americas grew about 50% year on year during the partnership. For a company that's just listed and is going international, I think that mix of credibility, customers and talent is the interesting part."

## 7. If they ask: "Where could our product fit in the team?"

*[Be plain here. Driverless haul trucks have no believable place in an F1 team's operations, because teams don't run mines or haul fleets. Steer towards marketing-led options. The spots below are ideas to test with a team's technical department, never claims.]*

- **Energy management and simulation (the most plausible, and still a stretch):** EACON schedules charging for more than 1,500 electric trucks based on battery level, predicted energy use and production needs, and the 2026 cars run a much bigger electric share. This could support an engineering-exchange or content role, but not a supply contract.
- **Data, perception and real-time decisions:** their 360-degree perception and live dispatching against a team's data and strategy work. At most this means guest engineering talks or joint content, and it's weak as an operational fit.
- **Factory logistics:** autonomous movement of materials around a team's campus. Probably not, since a factory isn't a mine. It's listed here only so you can rule it out if they raise it.
- **What a title could look like:** an "Official Autonomous Mining Technology Partner" designation would be a marketing label that carries the story, supported by content, hospitality and a talent programme. It wouldn't be a technical role.

> "I'll be straight with you on this one. I don't think there's a natural place for driverless haul trucks inside a race team, because they don't run mines. Where it gets interesting is the overlap in how you think. You're managing energy and charging across a big electric fleet in real time, and the cars now run a much bigger electric share under the new rules. So a technical story could be about how both sides manage energy under hard limits, which is more of an engineering exchange than a supply deal. I'd want to test that with a team's technical people before promising anything, and I'd build the partnership around customers, talent and markets first."

## 8. Other questions they may ask

**"What does it cost?"**

> "It depends a lot on what you want to do. Entry-level and supplier positions start in the low single-digit millions a year, official partner tiers sit above that, and driver, content and pure hospitality programmes sit below. I'd rather understand your goals first and then show you realistic numbers than throw a figure at you now."

**"Why SPORTFIVE, rather than going straight to a team?"**

> "A team can only sell you its own inventory. We worked exclusively with Mercedes and Ferrari for more than twelve years, and we gave that up on purpose so we could work across all eleven teams on the brand's side. The rights holders pay our fee, so the advice doesn't cost you anything. And if the right answer is 'not now', we'll tell you that."

**"Which team would fit us?"**

> "Straight answer: I don't know yet, and I'd be wary of naming one on a first call. That's what the grid mapping is for. We look at every team: who has room in your category, where they're strong in the markets you care about, and who's already on board. Then we come back with a short list."

*[Keep this to yourself, but Williams is likely ruled out because of Komatsu (see prep notes).]*

**"How do you measure ROI?"**

> "We agree it upfront, before anything's signed. Usually it's a mix of media value, brand tracking with the audiences you care about, pipeline, meaning the meetings and deals that come out of hospitality, and the hospitality outcomes themselves. For you, I'd guess pipeline and hiring matter a lot more than TV numbers."

**"We sell to a few hundred mining companies. Why would we need a sport with hundreds of millions of fans?"**

> "That's fair, and I wouldn't try to sell it to you on the fans. Your buyers are a small group of very senior people, and F1 is one of the few places where people like that turn up willingly for three days and stay to talk. The bigger audience matters more for hiring, and for investors now that you're listed. If neither of those is a priority right now, maybe it's not the right time, and that's fine."

*[If they say "we're loss-making and just listed, the timing's wrong": agree that it has to pay for itself. Point them to the smaller doors (hospitality-only, a regional deal, content) and a timeline that fits their budget cycle.]*

>
> So here's what I'd suggest. We'll write up a short brief from today, about a page, and you correct anything we got wrong. Then we'll map the realistic options across the grid, with honest numbers, and come back to you in [two to three weeks]. If the answer is 'not yet', we'll say that plainly. None of this costs you anything.
>
> Does that work as a plan? And before we wrap up, any questions for me, Bjorn or George?
>
> Thanks again, [Name]. It's been great to hear more about what you're building, and congratulations on the listing."
