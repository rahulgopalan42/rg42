**Call:** intro call with [Name, Job title] at Quess Corp. From SPORTFIVE: Rahul, Bjorn and George.

## The intro

> "Hi [Name], thanks for making the time. I'm Rahul from SPORTFIVE, and I've got Bjorn and George joining me as well.
>
> Just to give you a bit of background on us, SPORTFIVE is a global sports marketing agency. We work across pretty much every major sport, helping brands find the right partnerships, negotiate the deals and then manage them.
>
> We're a big organization, but we're actually quite flat in how we work. We work closely with colleagues across different sports and departments, so depending on what a brand needs, we can bring in the right people.
>
> And I think that's quite important because we don't want to overwhelm brands with everything we can offer. We'd much rather understand what you're trying to achieve first and then work out what makes sense. We've also got a business intelligence team that helps us with that.
>
> Now, just to give you a bit of background on our Formula 1 business. Historically, we worked exclusively with Mercedes and Ferrari. But we decided to move away from that model, and today we have non-exclusive agreements across the grid.
>
> And the reason is quite simple. It gives us the flexibility to look at the entire market rather than being tied to one or two teams. So when we're speaking to a brand like Quess, we can look at the different opportunities, understand what each team can offer, how they're priced, and ultimately find the best fit for what you're looking to achieve. And because we know the market quite well, we can also make sure you're getting a fair deal.
>
> Just to give you an example, we worked with CFI, who were initially looking at a partnership with Williams. But after understanding their objectives, we identified an opportunity with Lewis Hamilton that we felt would deliver better value, and we ended up brokering that deal. We've also worked on deals with McLaren, Ferrari and individual athletes. And outside Formula 1, we work with VGW in NASCAR.
>
> So really, the point is that we're not tied to any particular solution. And that's what we'd like to do today. Understand a bit more about Quess, what you're trying to achieve, and whether Formula 1 could make sense. But equally, let's keep the conversation open."

> "Yeah, so there were really two things that stood out to me. And then there's a third that makes it practical.

## Questions they might ask, and how to answer

### "Who watches F1? Is it our audience?"

> "Yeah, I think more of it than you'd expect. There's research showing that F1 fans index at 160 for business decision makers, compared to 100 for the general population. That's the highest of any sport. So the HR head or chief executive who decides how a company staffs up is much more likely than average to follow Formula 1.
>
> The second thing is how young the audience is. F1 has around 827 million fans, and 43% of them are under 35. That's the age of the people you place. Your own hiring data puts the average new associate in their mid-twenties.
>
> And it reaches India even without a race. Formula 1 says it has 79 million fans in India, on Nielsen's numbers. Every session is shown live there on FanCode, in a deal that runs to 2028."

### "We sell to HR heads and CEOs, not fans. Who's in the paddock?"

> "Yeah, that's a fair point, and I think it's actually one of the strongest reasons for a B2B company to look at Formula 1. Around two-thirds of Paddock Club guests are senior executives. And across the grid you've got more than 350 partner companies, each bringing their own guests.
>
> When you look at those partners, a lot of them are the kinds of employer you staff. M&S is with Williams, and Siemens and AT&T are with Red Bull. PepsiCo is with Mercedes, and Standard Chartered is a partner of Formula 1 itself. So that's retail, manufacturing, telecoms, consumer goods and banking, which are the sectors where you place most of your people.
>
> A lot of them are also the multinationals building in India. Goldman Sachs is a McLaren partner, and Bloomberg has called its Bengaluru campus its biggest office outside New York. UBS is with Mercedes, and it opened a new centre in Hyderabad in February. And Mercedes-Benz says its Bengaluru centre is its largest research site outside Germany.
>
> There's a link to your new Japan corridor as well. Toyota is Haas's title partner, Honda supplies Aston Martin's engines, and Komatsu is with Williams. Now, I'm not saying any of these are your customers. You'd know that better than I do.
>
> The other thing is that teams introduce their partners to each other. So the people your guests meet are often people you'd want to meet anyway. And instead of half an hour with an HR head, you get a full race weekend with them."

### "How are similar brands using F1, and what are they getting out of it?"

> "Yeah, so staffing is one of the few areas where your direct competitors are already on the grid. That means we can look at exactly what they're doing. I've picked seven examples. Three are staffing and recruitment names, then an engineering talent firm, an HR software company, and two companies with Indian roots."

**ManpowerGroup at Ferrari**

> "ManpowerGroup is probably the closest example, because they're a direct competitor. They've been with Ferrari since 2017, when their Experis brand became the team's official HR partner. Before that, Experis had spent three years supplying engineers and skilled technicians to Ferrari's racing side. So they were a supplier first and a sponsor second.
>
> Their chief executive, Jonas Prising, said at the time: 'At ManpowerGroup we share Ferrari's vision that access to talent and speed to market are critical in this fast-changing world of work.' I think that sums up why they went in.
>
> Manpower's Italian business describes the work quite clearly. They recruit for the team across the whole of Italy, because specialists are hard to find in the Motor Valley. They help new hires and their families relocate. And they keep their own people on site to look after the staff they supply, plus a bench of candidates ready to go.
>
> When they renewed in 2022, their logo went on the car. They haven't published any business return. But Manpower's Italian business says the work has now run nine years, and they're still listed as a Ferrari partner this season."

**Randstad at Racing Bulls**

> "Randstad has been in Formula 1 for about twenty years. They were with Williams from 2006 to 2017. Then in January 2019, their Italian business joined the team that's now Racing Bulls, as official HR partner. They renewed in 2022, and they're on the team's partner page now.
>
> Their reason was quite simple. Fabio Costantini, of Randstad HR Solutions, said: 'This sector has a constant requirement for technical experts and specialists.'
>
> What they do is run a programme with the team called Formula Future. They find students and graduates, select them, and train them at the factory in Faenza. For the 2021 intake, more than 3,000 people applied and 41 were chosen.
>
> The renewal announcement said 90% of those who finished the programme joined the team permanently. And the team's partner page now says 250 people work in motorsport because of it. Franz Tost, who ran the team then, called Randstad's expertise 'very beneficial to us.'
>
> They haven't published a sales return. But I think the signal is two teams, about two decades, and a programme the team itself puts numbers on."

**Indeed at Red Bull**

> "Indeed is the newest one. They joined Red Bull this March as the team's recruitment partner, on a multi-year deal. And reports at the time called it Indeed's first sponsorship in Formula 1.
>
> Their marketing chief, James Whitemore, gave the reason in one line. He said the deal 'reinforces a universal truth: success depends on the talent you hire.' And the team principal, Laurent Mekies, said: 'People have always been the foundation of our success at Oracle Red Bull Racing.'
>
> In terms of what they get, Indeed's name is on the car and the helmets. They're also the presenting partner of Red Bull's talent academy, which covers work experience, student projects and apprenticeships for engineers and technicians. And the deal covers the team's F1 Academy entry too.
>
> It's too early for results, and nothing has been published. But what it tells you is that one of the world's biggest job sites looked at a team's hiring story this year, and decided it was worth paying for."

**Akkodis at Mercedes**

> "Akkodis is the engineering and technology arm of the Adecco Group, the Swiss staffing company. They've been with Mercedes since 2022. Before that, under their old name Modis, they worked with Mercedes' Formula E team from 2020.
>
> Their president at the time, Jan Gupta, said the aim was 'to make an impact in helping the Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One team towards carbon neutrality.' And Toto Wolff said the team wanted their 'best-in-class IT and engineering R&D expertise.'
>
> Part of what they do is quite close to your world. They run a talent programme with the team called Lead the Charge. Mercedes says the first round drew more than 4,000 applicants from 99 countries, for paid placements at the factory in Brackley. Three people have completed it, and one now works for the team full time.
>
> And then in October, Akkodis announced a translation system for the team's race guests. It turns team radio and commentary into more than 50 languages, in under 50 milliseconds. It was first used at Monza.
>
> They haven't published a business return. The signals are a fifth season and a role that keeps getting wider. Mercedes does publish one small result, which is that a car-sharing scheme run by people from the programme cut commuting emissions by more than 10%."

**Workday at McLaren**

> "Workday makes HR and finance software, and they've been a McLaren partner since 2023. The two expanded the deal a year later. Their marketing chief, Emma Chalwin, called McLaren 'a globally recognized racing team and a valued Workday customer.' So again, customer first.
>
> McLaren plans its finances on Workday, which matters under the cost cap. And this year Deloitte published a case study on how McLaren moved its HR onto Workday in 30 weeks. Every employee had logged in within four weeks of launch.
>
> McLaren's Sarah Wilson said: 'The new human capital management system gives us faster real-time data for us to be able to make decisions.' And Zak Brown is on the record saying: 'Workday has transformed the way we operate as a business, so we're more agile and efficient.' There's no sales return published, but the expansion is a good sign.
>
> UKG, another HR software company, did something similar in November last year. They became Aston Martin's official HR, pay and workforce management partner, on a multi-year deal. The team's people chief, Sophie Ackland, said the team understands 'that great businesses are powered by great people.' Nothing has been published there yet."

**Mphasis at Haas**

> "This one is interesting because they're a neighbour of yours. Mphasis is an IT company based in Bengaluru and listed in India. They've been with Haas since the Las Vegas race two years ago, as the team's official digital partner.
>
> Their chief executive, Nitin Rakesh, called it an exciting opportunity to show what technology can do. Mphasis works with the team on data, analytics, AI and security, and their logo went on the car from that race. Forbes reported it as a five-year technical partnership.
>
> No results have been published. But they're now in their second season, and they stayed when Haas changed its title partner. And I think it shows that a listed company from Bengaluru can do this."

**Tata Communications with Formula 1**

> "The longest-running Indian name in the sport isn't actually with a team. Tata Communications first worked with Formula 1 from 2012. They stepped away around 2020, and came back in 2022 as the official broadcast connectivity provider. They're on Formula 1's partner list now.
>
> Stefano Domenicali, Formula 1's chief executive, said at the time: 'We are delighted to welcome Tata Communications back as a partner following previous successes in our partnerships.' So it was a return, not a first deal.
>
> And they do a real job. Tata says it carries more than 100 video feeds and over 250 audio channels from each race to Formula 1's centre in the UK, in under 200 milliseconds.
>
> There's no commercial return published. But the signal is that they came back, and that they do a job the broadcast relies on."

**What that adds up to**

> "So I think the main takeaway is that your field is already in Formula 1. Two direct competitors, ManpowerGroup and Randstad, have each stayed for years, and a big job site joined this year. None of them publishes a sales return.
>
> What they publish is the work: how many applied, how many were trained, how many were hired. And the ones that last all started with a real job for the team. You've also got two companies from India in the sport, one of them from your own city.
>
> For Quess, I'd take three things from that. First, if you do this, build it around people rather than a logo. That could be a programme, a story, or the customers you host.
>
> Second, I wouldn't copy the leaders. Manpower and Randstad both built on Italian teams and Italian engineers. Your story is scale, and India.
>
> And third, start with what you can measure and afford. On your margins, that may well be hosting or content before it's a team."

### "Content is really important for us. How would a team help us make it?"

> "Yeah, absolutely. And I think for you, content could be the main part of this. Your subject is people, and Formula 1 is full of stories about people that most fans never see.
>
> Most team partnerships include a set number of filming days each year with the drivers and the team's people. The team's content people help you make it, and the team's own channels matter too. F1 is the number one sport on social media, ahead of the NBA, with 2.2 billion engagements. And 61% of fans engage with F1 content every day.
>
> The one thing to be aware of is that Formula 1 controls the actual race footage, not the teams. So partner content is filmed around the race: at the factory, in the garage, or on filming days.
>
> There are a few good examples already. CrowdStrike made a series with Mercedes where the team's specialists explain the safety kit on the car, and Mercedes says it got around 6.2 million views. And closer to you, Mercedes and Akkodis publish the stories of the people who came through their talent programme, by name, with what each of them now does.
>
> For Quess, I'd start with the pit stop. In 2023 McLaren changed four tyres in 1.80 seconds, which was a world record. About twenty people did that, and they're the team's own mechanics, so each of them has a day job on the car as well.
>
> So you ask the questions you ask every day. How does a team pick those people, and how does it train them? What happens when one of them is ill on a Sunday morning?
>
> You'd film that with the crew and the people who manage them. Then you put it next to a store opening or a factory line, where your customer needs the right people in place on day one. We wouldn't make any claim about who the team hires through, and there's no stunt. It's just how a team staffs the most visible two seconds in sport.
>
> Another idea is the people nobody sees. A top team is about a thousand people, and the fans see two drivers. So a short series on the jobs behind the car, and how someone gets one, speaks straight to the young people you place.
>
> And we'd make sure the content rights are clearly written into the partnership. That means how many filming days, which drivers and team people, what the team posts on its own channels, and how long and where you can use it."

### "Why are these brands in F1?"

> "I think it really comes down to two things. The first is the story. Every company says its people are its advantage, and an F1 team is one of the few places where you can watch that happen.
>
> You've got about a thousand people, a new race every week or two, and no second chance on Sunday. For a staffing or HR brand, that's a ready-made way to talk about finding and keeping the right people. That's essentially why Indeed, Randstad and ManpowerGroup are there.
>
> The second is access. That means the employers they want to host, and the young people they want to reach. Randstad and Akkodis both use their teams to attract engineers, and all of them bring clients to races.
>
> For Quess, I'd guess it's the second one. You don't need Formula 1 to show you can hire at scale, because half a million people on your books does that. What you may want is a place to meet the heads of multinationals, outside India."

### "Who are our competitors in F1, and which teams are available?"

> "Yeah, so we've done some initial work on that. I don't want to promise anything on availability yet, because right now it would be a guess. What I can tell you is who's where.
>
> Staffing and recruitment names sit at three teams. ManpowerGroup is at Ferrari, Randstad is at Racing Bulls, and Indeed is at Red Bull.

>
> The second thing is understanding what you want out of it. If it's hosting multinational clients, that points one way. If it's a people programme with your name on it, that points another. And if it's being seen in India, a driver may do more than a team.
>
> One thing worth knowing is the title. We had Official Staffing Partner in mind for you. But the titles already out there are quite wide, like 'HR partner' at Racing Bulls, and 'HR, pay and workforce management' at Aston Martin. So we'd read each one carefully before saying any more.

The point is Formula Future, where thousands apply, a few dozen are trained at the factory, and most of those who finish are hired. That gives Randstad a story about finding talent that no advert could, and that's the kind of role I'd look for, in your own way."

>

> And there's one more thing. The decision to build a capability centre in India is taken at a head office in Europe, America or Japan. F1 races in all three, and those are the people in the Paddock Club. So if more of your growth is meant to come in dollars, that's where the audience is."

### "We tried sport with East Bengal and it didn't work. Why would this be different?"

> "Yeah, I think that's a fair question. The short answer is that it's a different job. I've read about it, so let me say what I understand, and please correct me if I'm wrong.
>
> In 2018 you took 70% of the company that ran East Bengal's football team. So you were the owner. You paid for players and a coach, the team carried your name, and you answered to the members and fans of a hundred-year-old club.
>
> The team finished second in the league in the first season. But by August 2019, Ajit Isaac was saying you would 'most probably not be there' by the end of that season. And by July 2020 the sporting rights were back with the club.
>
> A partnership isn't that. You don't own the team, pay the players, or carry the results. You buy a set of rights for a set number of years, so your name in agreed places, days with the drivers and the team's people, and guests at races.
>
> It's written down, it has a price, and it has an end date. And if it doesn't work, you don't renew.
>
> So what I'd take from East Bengal is the lesson rather than the fear. Be clear what the job is before you sign, keep control of what you're paying for, and have a way out. Those are the three things we'd build into anything here."

### "Are there rules on an Indian company doing a deal like this?"

> "Yeah, there are, and I think your finance and legal team should look at them before anything else. I'm not a lawyer, so please take this as a pointer.
>
> India's foreign exchange rules, as the Reserve Bank publishes them, list sponsorship of a sports activity abroad among the payments that need government approval first. That applies when the amount is above 100,000 US dollars, and the approval comes from the sports ministry.
>
> Payments abroad also come with tax paperwork. And tax may have to be withheld, depending on the treaty with the team's country.
>
> You also have a holding company in Singapore, where your biggest overseas business is. Whether a deal should sit there or in India is a tax and governance question. That's yours to answer, not ours.
>
> Indian companies are in the sport, though. Mphasis, which is listed in India, is a partner of Haas, and Tata Communications is a partner of Formula 1. So I'd check with your finance and legal team first, and we'll fit the timetable around their answer."

### "What does SPORTFIVE do outside Formula 1?"

> "Quite a lot, actually. We work across football, tennis, hockey and several other sports, with more than 60 offices across over 20 countries.
>
> In football, for example, we've worked with Borussia Dortmund since 1999, and that relationship has recently been extended through 2034. We help them find sponsors, manage partnerships and sell hospitality. In tennis, we're the Australian Open's lead agency for partnerships in Europe.
>
> And we've also got our business intelligence team supporting us with research, strategy and partnership evaluation. So although we're talking about Formula 1 today, we can explore opportunities across pretty much the entire sports landscape."

### "What's the biggest risk?"

> "I think the biggest risk is signing a logo with no job behind it. Most teams have 25 or more partners, and the ones that get lost put a name on the car and stop there. On your margins, that's money you can't get back.
>
> So a deal needs a real job, a plan for the people you'll host, and someone on your side who owns it. That's the first thing.
>
> The second is a conflict you didn't see. Three teams have a staffing or recruitment partner, and four more have HR names close to you. Contracts often cover more than the title says, which is why we check every team before your name goes anywhere.
>
> The third is specific to you, and it's spending at a level where your shareholders can't see the return. You're listed, and you report every quarter. So I'd size the first step so it can be judged within a year, and stopped if it doesn't pay.
>
> And finally, it's about choosing the right option for the job. If the job is being known across India, cricket reaches more people there than F1 does, and you know that from working with Jasprit Bumrah. If it's meeting the heads of multinationals outside India, Formula 1 is hard to beat. And that's really where we'd come in, helping you make that call."
