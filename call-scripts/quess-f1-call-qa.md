### "We tried sport with East Bengal and it didn't work. Why would this be different?"

> "Because it's a different job. I've read about it, so let me say what I understand, and correct me if I'm wrong.
>
> In 2018 you took 70% of the company that ran East Bengal's football team. You were the owner. You paid for players and a coach, the team carried your name, and you answered to the members and fans of a hundred-year-old club. The team finished second in the league in the first season. But by August 2019 Ajit Isaac was saying you would 'most probably not be there' by the end of that season. By July 2020 the sporting rights were back with the club.
>
> A partnership is not that. You don't own the team, pay the players, or carry the results. You buy a set of rights for a set number of years: your name in agreed places, days with the drivers and the team's people, guests at races. It's written down, it has a price, and it has an end date. If it doesn't work, you don't renew.
>
> What I'd take from East Bengal is the lesson, not the fear. Be clear what the job is before you sign. Keep control of what you're paying for. And have a way out. Those are the three things we'd build into anything here."

>
> You also have a holding company in Singapore, where your biggest overseas business is. Whether a deal should sit there or in India is a tax and governance question, and it's yours to answer, not ours.
