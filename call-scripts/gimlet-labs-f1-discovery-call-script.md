# Gimlet Labs x SPORTFIVE: F1 Discovery Call Script

**Call type:** First call / discovery. Goal: introduce yourself and SPORTFIVE, understand Gimlet Labs' objectives, fill out the brief, show why Formula 1 is where AI infrastructure companies build their name with the world's biggest buyers, agree next steps.

**How to use this script:** Every spoken block is written the way you would say it out loud. Do not read it word for word. Glance at the bold anchors, then talk. Short sentences, slow pace, warm tone. The listeners are deep technologists from a US company; they respect precision and dislike marketing talk, so keep it plain, concrete and short. Let them talk more than you.

## Part 4: How Formula 1 partnerships work (8 to 10 minutes)

*[Calibrate to their answer from Part 2. If they know the sport, compress. Invite questions as you go: "Stop me anytime."]*

### 4.1 The sport in thirty seconds

> "Let me give you a simple picture of how the sport is set up, because it explains the options you have.
>
> Formula 1 itself, the series, is owned by Liberty Media, an American media company. They own the commercial side: the calendar, the broadcast deals, the races.
>
> The FIA is the regulator. They write the rules.
>
> And then there are eleven teams, including two American ones, Haas and Cadillac. Each team is an independent business with its own owners and its own partnerships. Some belong to car makers, like Ferrari, Mercedes and Audi. Some to consumer giants, like Red Bull. Some to investors.
>
> So a brand can partner at three levels: with the sport itself, with a team, or with a driver personally. Most brands enter through a team, and I can explain why when we get there."

### 4.2 What a team partnership includes

> "What do you get as a partner? Think of it in five parts.
>
> **One, branding.** Your logo on the car, the race suits, the garage. Around 24 race weekends a year. Last season the total audience was 1.8 billion, and the average race reached 76 million people.
>
> **Two, the official title.** You become, for example, the Official AI Infrastructure Partner of a team. You use the team's name, logo and imagery in your own marketing and sales conversations.
>
> **Three, hospitality.** Paddock access, the Paddock Club, garage tours, time with the drivers. 66% of Paddock Club guests are senior executives, and for a technology brand that room includes the CIOs and CTOs you sell to.
>
> And one detail people miss. A Grand Prix runs over three days, and your passes work every day. Seven passes can host twenty different guests in one weekend, and hundreds across a season.
>
> **Four, content and digital.** Access to the team's channels and drivers for content. Formula 1 is the number one sport in the world for social engagement, 2.2 billion engagements, ahead of the NBA. A strong stage for explaining what you do.
>
> **Five, people.** Driver appearances, team bosses at your events, filming days, hot laps for your top customers.
>
> And around all of this sits the business platform: every team carries 25 or more partners, more than 350 companies across the grid, meeting each other all season. That room is your pipeline."

### 4.3 The audience

> "Now, who you reach. Your market is unusual: a small number of very large decisions. So let me be precise about what this sport offers you.

In the Paddock Club, 66% of guests are senior executives, and for a technology partner that means CIOs, CTOs and CISOs. And the partner room around the grid, 350 companies, includes the biggest technology names on earth. For a company that needs twenty relationships, not twenty million clicks, that room is the product.
>
> **The engineers.** You are competing for the best systems and chip people in the world. This sport is an engineering magnet: 43% of fans are under 35, and the racing itself is won by simulation, data and hardware pushed to its limits. A team partnership tells every engineer you want to hire: we play at that level.
>
> **The world, when you want it.** 827 million fans and the number one sport for social engagement. You do not need mass reach today. But when the Gimlet name needs to mean something to markets, boards and future customers, the stage is already yours."

### 4.4 Why this could fit Gimlet

> "Let me tell you why I find this opportunity interesting for Gimlet. Three reasons.
>
> **First, the story writes itself.** Formula 1 is an efficiency competition. Capped budgets, limited fuel, even capped computing for car design. Winning here means getting more from the same machines than the other ten teams. That is, sentence for sentence, what Gimlet sells. You would not be borrowing this sport's story. You would be telling your own.
>
> **Second, your market is already on the grid.** AWS is the sport's official cloud provider. Google Cloud partners McLaren. Oracle's name is on Red Bull. Microsoft reportedly signed with Mercedes in January at around sixty million dollars a year. When the biggest buyers of AI infrastructure spend at that level here, they are telling you where their attention lives. A partnership puts Gimlet in that room as a peer, at a fraction of those numbers.
>
> **Third, trust is your bottleneck, and this buys trust.** You are a young company asking the largest companies on earth to run their most important new workloads on you. They will ask: will Gimlet be here in ten years? A multi-year team partnership is a public answer. It says permanence, in a language executives read fluently.
>
> One last thing on value. All of this sits on very small supply: eleven teams for the entire world, one seat per category, three to five year deals. Small supply, growing demand. That is why these partnerships hold their value."
