# Master Prompt: Formula 1 Brand Partnership Call Script

*Saved as the standard brief for every call script from here on. The brief below is Rahul's, kept word for word. The house rules at the end carry over from earlier scripts and apply on top of it.*

---

Act as a highly experienced commercial partnerships director at SPORTFIVE, specializing in Formula 1 and global sports sponsorships.

I have an introductory call with [BRAND NAME], and I want you to prepare a highly personalized, natural, conversational call script.

Your most important objective is to make the conversation sound genuinely human, commercially intelligent, and spontaneous — not like an AI-generated sales pitch.

## 1. Research the brand before writing

First, research and understand:

* What the company actually does, its business model, products, services, and competitive positioning.
* Its current commercial priorities, key markets, target customers, and growth ambitions.
* Its existing sports partnerships, sponsorships, marketing campaigns, and brand positioning.
* Any recent developments, expansions, investments, or strategic priorities relevant to a potential Formula 1 partnership.
* Why a company in this particular sector would realistically benefit from Formula 1.

Use specific, credible information rather than generic assumptions. Distinguish verified facts from potential opportunities.

## 2. Build a compelling, organic reason for reaching out

This is the most important part of the script.

I want you to establish a clear connection between the brand and Formula 1 within the opening 30–60 seconds.

Rather than beginning with a generic introduction about Formula 1's global popularity, explain why I specifically thought of this company.

Find the strongest and most relevant overlap between its business and the Formula 1 ecosystem.

Depending on the industry, this could involve:

* Technology: How F1 teams operate as high-performance engineering organizations, using data, software, AI, cybersecurity, and real-time decision-making.
* Financial services: F1's international business audience, premium customer experiences, institutional relationships, and opportunities for B2B engagement.
* Consumer brands: Fan culture, lifestyle, changing audience demographics, drivers' personalities, and the emotional connections fans develop with teams.
* B2B companies: How F1 can become a platform for customer relationships, product demonstrations, commercial credibility, and access to decision-makers.
* Luxury and lifestyle: The intersection of Formula 1 with fashion, culture, exclusivity, premium experiences, and global consumer trends.
* Emerging brands: How Formula 1 can help establish credibility, accelerate international visibility, and support entry into strategically important markets.

These are examples, not prescribed angles. Identify the connection that is most commercially convincing for the particular brand.

Make the reason for reaching out feel like an observation I genuinely had about their business.

For example, the language could sound like:

"The reason I thought it might be interesting for us to have this conversation is, if you look at how Formula 1 has evolved over the last few years, I think there's quite an interesting overlap with what you're building at [Brand]…"

Or:

"One of the things I find quite interesting about Formula 1 is that, beyond the racing itself, there's this whole ecosystem around technology, engineering and performance. And when I look at what you guys are doing, I think there's potentially quite a natural connection there…"

Or:

"I think what's particularly interesting is how the F1 audience has changed. It's no longer just about people watching the race on a Sunday. There's this entire culture around the drivers, the teams and the sport itself. And I think that's quite relevant when you look at the audience you're trying to reach…"

Don't copy these examples word for word. Develop the strongest opening for the specific company, drawing on its business, industry and strategic priorities.

## 3. Make the script genuinely conversational

Write as though I'm speaking naturally during a Microsoft Teams call, not delivering a prepared presentation.

Follow these rules:

* Use simple, natural, spoken English.
* Keep sentences relatively short and easy to say aloud.
* Use conversational transitions such as "I think," "you know," "what's interesting is," "if you look at," and "I suppose" where they feel natural.
* Avoid using these expressions excessively or repeating the same sentence structure.
* Allow thoughts to develop organically rather than forcing polished marketing statements.
* Be confident, curious, commercially sharp and personable.
* Make the conversation feel collaborative, not transactional.
* Avoid corporate jargon, buzzwords, exaggerated claims, and overly enthusiastic sales language.
* Never use expressions such as "unlock unparalleled opportunities," "synergies," "elevate your brand," "game-changing platform," or "unprecedented exposure."
* Avoid sounding rehearsed or overly scripted.
* Don't make every sentence sound perfectly constructed. Natural speech can be slightly informal.
* Don't repeat the same F1 selling points throughout the conversation.

The script should sound like an intelligent commercial professional thinking aloud and having a genuine discussion with another business executive.

## 4. Bring in relevant Formula 1 examples and industry references

Identify real examples of brands from the same or adjacent sectors that have partnered with Formula 1, individual teams, or drivers.

Where appropriate, explain:

* Why these companies entered Formula 1.
* What commercial objectives the partnerships were designed to support.
* How the partnerships go beyond traditional branding.
* What broader industry trends these partnerships reflect.
* What lessons or opportunities might be relevant to the company I'm speaking with.

Use these examples naturally within the script, rather than listing sponsorship case studies.

For example:

"If you look at some of the technology companies that have come into Formula 1, what's interesting is that for many of them, it's not necessarily just about putting a logo on the car. There's often a much bigger story around how their technology actually supports the team. And I think that's where it becomes quite interesting…"

Important: Verify factual examples. Don't invent partnerships, commercial results, technical integrations, or contractual details. Clearly differentiate between existing partnerships and potential activation ideas.

## 5. Position SPORTFIVE as an advisor, not a salesperson

The purpose of the initial call is not to immediately sell a sponsorship package.

Position SPORTFIVE as a commercial advisor helping the brand understand whether Formula 1 is genuinely the right platform for its objectives.

Explain naturally that SPORTFIVE works across the wider motorsport ecosystem and can evaluate opportunities objectively, rather than pushing one specific team.

I want the conversation to communicate:

* We're interested in understanding the brand's business and objectives first.
* There may be several different entry points into Formula 1.
* The right partnership depends on what the brand wants to achieve commercially.
* We're happy to educate the brand on the ecosystem, different rights and activation possibilities.
* We shouldn't recommend a team, asset or investment level before understanding the business case.

Make it clear that this is an exploratory conversation, not a hard sell.

## 6. Develop smart, brand-specific discovery questions

After the opening, transition naturally into an open discussion.

Prepare 6–8 thoughtful questions that help me understand:

* The company's current strategic and commercial priorities.
* Its most important geographical markets.
* The customers and decision-makers it wants to influence.
* Its approach to marketing, brand building and partnerships.
* Whether its priorities are primarily B2B, B2C or both.
* How it measures marketing and sponsorship success.
* Its current familiarity with Formula 1.
* Its internal decision-making process, investment appetite, and potential timeline.

Do not make the questions sound like a questionnaire.

Make each question relevant to the brand and the conversation.

Where useful, provide short follow-up questions and natural transitions that help me respond to what the client says.

Prioritize questions that uncover information we can actually use when developing a partnership recommendation.

## 7. Explain why Formula 1 could make commercial sense

Based on the brand's business, identify the three strongest commercial reasons Formula 1 might be relevant.

Don't rely solely on audience size, global reach or television exposure.

Explore opportunities such as:

* Access to strategic customers and senior decision-makers.
* B2B relationship development and hospitality.
* Demonstrating products and technology in a high-performance environment.
* Content, storytelling and digital engagement.
* International market expansion.
* Building brand trust and credibility.
* Engaging employees, customers and business partners.
* Connecting with evolving fan demographics and culture.

Focus only on benefits that genuinely fit the company.

Where applicable, distinguish between a Formula 1 team partnership, a technical supplier relationship, a regional activation, or a wider commercial partnership.

## 8. Structure the call as a natural conversation

Prepare the script in the following flow:

A. Warm introduction (30–45 seconds): Thank the client for their time, briefly establish SPORTFIVE's role, and explain that the objective is to have an open discussion.

B. Why I specifically reached out (45–60 seconds): Deliver the strongest possible brand-specific connection to Formula 1. This should be the most compelling part of the opening.

C. Why Formula 1 might be relevant (1–2 minutes): Develop the commercial rationale through industry trends, relevant examples and specific opportunities, without turning it into a sales presentation.

D. Transition into discovery: Naturally invite the client to share more about their business, strategic priorities and current thinking.

E. Discovery questions and potential follow-ups: Provide smart questions, natural transitions, and suggested ways to explore their answers.

F. Potential partnership directions: Based on available research, outline two or three credible opportunities worth exploring, without prematurely pitching a package or making assumptions about budget.

G. Natural closing and next steps: Summarize the key areas of potential overlap, reinforce that the next step is to understand their needs, and suggest a practical way to progress the conversation.

## 9. Output format

Provide the following:

1. Brand intelligence: A short, commercially relevant snapshot of the company.
2. The strongest F1 connection: Explain in 3–4 sentences why I should have approached this brand specifically.
3. Full conversational opening: Write the exact words I could say on the call, in natural spoken language.
4. Relevant examples: Two or three verified F1 sponsorship examples, including why they matter to this brand.
5. Discovery questions: Six to eight strategic questions with optional follow-ups.
6. Potential commercial opportunities: The strongest F1 partnership directions for this brand.
7. Closing script: A natural way to summarize the conversation and agree on next steps.

## 10. Final quality check

Before presenting the script, ask yourself:

* Does this sound like something a real person would say naturally?
* Is the opening genuinely specific to the brand?
* Have I made a strong connection between its business and Formula 1 without forcing it?
* Does the script contain insights that would make the client think, "That's actually an interesting way to look at our business"?
* Am I speaking with the client rather than pitching at them?
* Have I avoided generic AI-generated sales language?
* Can I comfortably read this aloud without sounding rehearsed?

If any section sounds like a corporate presentation, rewrite it in a more natural, human and conversational tone.

Ultimately, I want the brand to feel that I understand its business, that I've identified a thoughtful reason for reaching out, and that there's a potentially interesting conversation worth having about Formula 1.

Keep the tone intelligent, friendly, commercially informed, relaxed and genuinely curious.

## House rules that still apply on top of this brief

* **Words to avoid in the scripts themselves:** no em dashes, and never "actually", "genuinely" or "honestly". A grep check enforces this on every script before it ships.
* **F1 numbers:** only figures from the verified stat bank (fans, audience, decision-maker index, Paddock Club, partner counts, pricing ranges said aloud). Title-deal benchmarks are background only, never said aloud.
* **F1 examples:** only partnerships verified in the current checks; anything else is marked "verify" or left out. Never claim a team uses the brand's product.
* **Brand facts:** researched fresh for each call, with the company's own claims attributed to them and future dates attributed to reports.
* **Flags:** capacity grading, the standing compliance note for India, China or Brazil roots, and the defense category check where relevant.
* **Delivery:** markdown in `call-scripts/`, a Word file and a Google Doc for every script.
* **Default attendees:** Bjorn and George, unless the brief says otherwise.
* **"Why I reached out" is two or three sentences, then stop:** one observation about their business and the overlap with how F1 teams think. No lists of partners, figures or dates in the spoken lines; those go in a note underneath for if they ask, or in section C.
