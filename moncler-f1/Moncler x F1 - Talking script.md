# Moncler × Formula 1: talking script

## 1 · Opening

Thank you for your time today. It is good to see you again.

In our first conversation, you told us about Moncler: where the brand is today, and where you want to take it. We promised to come back with recommendations. That is what we have today.

The meeting has three parts.

Part one: why Formula 1. What the sport is today, who the fans are, and why it fits Moncler.

Part two: our recommendations. Why we suggest a driver, then four drivers and three teams.

Part three: next steps.

It takes about thirty minutes. Please stop us at any time.

This is not a final offer. It is a direction. If something does not feel right for Moncler, please tell us.

**Ask Moncler:** Before we start: has anything changed on your side since we last spoke?

## Part 1 · Why Formula 1 (slides 2–11, about 10 minutes)

## 2 · The opportunity

Let me start with the idea in one sentence. Formula 1 gives Moncler a credible way into one of the most visible worlds of sport and culture.

The key word is "credible". Many fashion brands now appear around F1. Some look like guests. Others look like they belong. The difference is not the budget. It is the fit.

## 3 · The idea

The simple option is to put product in the paddock. We think that is not enough.

Our idea is a driver. One person who carries Moncler Collection through the whole year: travel, cities, race weekends, and the private moments around the race.

The reason is practical. A driver's year means more than twenty races, in many countries. Cold mornings at the circuit. Long flights. Evenings in big cities. That is the life Moncler designs for.

## 4 · The objective

This is the objective we took from our first conversation. Place Moncler Collection in global cultural and sporting moments. And introduce the brand to new communities, beyond your existing clients.

Everything after this slide answers this objective.

**Ask Moncler:** Did we understand this correctly? Would you change anything?

## 5 · Why Formula One?

So, part one. Why Formula 1?

## 6 · What F1 has become

Formula 1 has changed. Today it is sport, fashion, travel, technology and entertainment together. And much of the story is told on social media first.

One moment shows this clearly: the paddock walk. The drivers arrive at the circuit, and the photographers are waiting, like at a fashion show. It happens at every race weekend.

## 7 · Scale

Four numbers.

827 million fans around the world. That is 63% more than in 2018.

43% of them are under 35.

42% are women. In 2018, it was 37%.

And 6.7 million people went to the races in 2025. That is a record.

For Moncler, the two numbers in the middle matter most: young people, and women. We see them as the luxury clients of the next ten years.

The last number matters too. These are real people who travel to a city for a race weekend. And many race cities are also important luxury cities: Miami, Las Vegas, Shanghai, Singapore, Monaco.

So F1 gives you two things together: global reach on screens, and physical moments in key cities.

## 8 · The audience

This slide compares F1 fans with football fans and basketball fans. 100 is the average person.

On all four measures, F1 fans are ahead of both: interest in fashion, buying premium, paying more for quality, and high income.

The biggest gap is in buying the premium version of a product. F1 fans are at 132. Football fans are at 112. That is about twenty points.

Why does this matter? Income tells you what people can spend. This number tells you what they choose to buy. For a premium brand, the choice matters more.

To be fair, on paying more for quality the difference is small: 108 against 104. The real case is fashion interest, premium buying and income.

**Ask Moncler:** Does this match your own client data? Where are you strong today, and where do you want to grow?

## 9–11 · Five reasons

Five reasons why this fits Moncler.

One: culture. A race weekend is now a cultural event: fashion, music, hospitality and nightlife.

Two: a new luxury audience. Younger, and more balanced between men and women. Moncler can reach them without changing its premium position.

Three: stories all year. Every race weekend is a moment. Between the races, the driver's travel and training keep the story going.

Four: global and local. One idea for the whole world, activated only where you choose: your markets, your stores. There are three races in the United States, and races in China, Japan and Singapore.

Five: fans accept partners. In the 2025 Global F1 Fan Survey, by Formula 1 and Motorsport Network, 76% of fans said sponsors make the F1 experience better. Three out of four. The condition is that the partnership feels authentic. That is why we focus on fit, not only on visibility.

**Ask Moncler:** Which markets or stores would you start with?

That was part one. Now part two: our recommendations.

## Part 2 · Our recommendations (slides 12–26, about 15 minutes)

## 12 · Why partner with an F1 driver?

There are three ways into F1: the sport itself, a team, or a driver.

Our recommendation is to start with a driver. A team can be added. Here is why, in five short points.

## 13 · A human point of view

A logo can be seen. But it cannot wear a jacket, travel, or tell a story. A driver can.

There is also a practical reason. Inside the circuit, drivers mostly wear team clothing. The arrival is different. What a driver wears on arrival is usually a personal choice, and it is photographed at every race. For a fashion brand, that is the moment that matters.

Moncler already knows this way of thinking. With Project Mondo G, a Mercedes-Benz G-Class became a Moncler piece. A driver is the same idea, with a person.

## 14 · Authentic integration

With a driver, Moncler does not need to create moments. They already exist: the arrival at the circuit, the flight to the next race, a client event, a fashion week.

Moncler is there, worn by the same person each time. Nothing looks staged. That is what makes it credible.

## 15 · Human storytelling

Drivers live with ambition, resilience and excellence. Moncler is built on the same values.

A driver's career has years of work, risk, wins and losses. People follow that story. Moncler's heritage, craft and performance can live inside it.

## 16 · A 365-day platform

A sponsor is visible on race weekends. A driver is visible all year: races, training, travel, interviews, fashion weeks and store openings. Also in the winter months, when there are no races.

## 17 · People connect with people

People build relationships with people, not with logos. A driver gives Moncler a face that many people already follow. That brings new audiences to the brand.

**Ask Moncler:** How do you work with ambassadors today? And how do you measure whether it works?

## 18 · Driver recommendations

We chose four drivers. Two are established. Two are young.

The real choice is about time. With an established driver, you pay for recognition today. With a young driver, you pay less today and share the growth. The risk is that a young career may not grow as expected. Both are valid. It depends on your objective.

Availability and existing contracts are not checked yet. We check first, then we confirm.

## 19 · Carlos Sainz

Carlos Sainz. You know Carlos from four seasons at Ferrari.

A race winner, and one of the most respected drivers in the paddock. Precise, consistent and calm. The style is elegant but understated: quality that does not follow trends.

Carlos now drives for Williams, with a multi-year contract. Williams is also one of the teams we recommend. I will come back to that.

## 20 · Charles Leclerc

Charles Leclerc. Ferrari driver, born in Monaco. In Italy, no introduction is needed.

Charles is one of the most recognised faces in F1, with an elegant image and real credibility in fashion. Charles moves easily between the circuit, luxury events and fashion editorials.

For Moncler: very strong recognition from the first day. Recognition at this level comes at a higher price.

## 21 · Arvid Lindblad

Arvid Lindblad. This is Arvid's first season in F1, with Racing Bulls. One of the most promising young drivers in the sport.

Ambitious, and not afraid to do things differently. Arvid's background is multicultural, and the outlook is modern.

For Moncler: a long-term partnership. Moncler could be there from the beginning.

## 22 · Oliver Bearman

Oliver Bearman drives for Haas. A fast rise through motorsport, which shows determination.

Oliver connects well with younger audiences, is growing in fashion, and is natural in front of the camera.

For Moncler: a younger, modern face for the brand's sporting heritage.

So: Carlos and Charles for recognition now. Arvid and Oliver for a story that grows over time. Some brands choose one of each.

**Ask Moncler:** Which of the four feels closest to Moncler? One face, or two?

## 23 · Team recommendations

Now the teams. A simple way to see the difference: a driver gives you a face. A team gives you a place: hospitality, the paddock, the garage, and the team's name and images for your campaigns and stores.

We recommend three teams. They are three different deals. In general, the more successful the team, the higher the price, and the less room you have to shape the partnership.

## 24 · McLaren

McLaren. One of the oldest teams in F1, based in England. Team champions in 2024 and 2025, and Lando Norris won the 2025 drivers' title. The drivers are Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri.

This season, with the new rules, Mercedes has been the strongest team. But McLaren is still one of the most commercially active brands in the sport, with young, fashion-aware fans and experience with lifestyle and apparel partners.

For Moncler: a link with a recent champion. The trade-off: many partners already, and a premium price.

## 25 · Audi

Audi. A new team, in its first season. Moncler would not be one name among many. Moncler could be a founding luxury partner.

The team is based in Hinwil, in Switzerland. European design, precise engineering, and the Alps: a natural fit with Moncler Grenoble.

The trade-off: a new team needs time to win.

## 26 · Williams

Williams. A historic British team, founded in 1977. The team principal is James Vowles. The drivers are Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon.

2026 has been a difficult season for Williams. That is part of a long rebuild, with strong investment. It is also one reason the entry price is lower than at a top team. For Moncler, that means more visibility for the money, and more say in how the partnership looks.

And Carlos drives for Williams. So the team and the driver could be one story.

So, three options: join a recent champion, be a founder, or shape a comeback.

**Ask Moncler:** Driver, team, or both? And which option is closest to how Moncler likes to work?

That was part two. Now part three: next steps.

## 27 · The close

To summarise. F1 is a global platform where performance, style and modern culture meet.

With the right driver, Moncler is not only a sponsor. Moncler becomes part of how the sport looks, all year.
