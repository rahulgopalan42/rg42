# Moncler × Formula 1: meeting script

**What this is.** A script for presenting SPORTFIVE's "Moncler × Formula One" deck in person. It's written to be said, as a conversation; say it your way. **Remember** is the line to hold on to for each slide. **Ask Moncler** marks the points where you stop and let them talk. Lines in *italics* are cues.

**The thread.** This isn't about a logo on a car or product in the paddock. It's about using F1, through the right driver, as a year-round cultural platform for Moncler Collection: travel, city life, race-week style and the personal moments around elite performance.

**The goal of the meeting.** Not a decision. Leave with three things: their reaction to the idea, a sense of which drivers and teams feel like Moncler, and their priorities (markets, audiences, timing) so we can come back with something tailored.

**Timing.** About 30 minutes, with Moncler doing a third of the talking. Slides 9 to 11 are one build; treat them as one slide.

---

## 1 · Opening ★

Thank you for having us.

Let me say up front what today is, and what it isn't. It isn't a sponsorship pitch, and we're not here to put a logo on a car. Formula 1 has become something much bigger than a race, and we think there's a natural, credible place for Moncler in it. Our job is to show you where, and then listen, because the right shape for this depends on what Moncler wants to achieve.

So we'll walk you through the idea, a few drivers and teams we think fit, and we'd love your honest reaction as we go.

**Ask Moncler:** Before we start: if this worked, what would success look like for Moncler twelve months from now?

## 2 · The opportunity

**Remember:** A credible route into one of the world's most visible cultural ecosystems.

Here's the idea in one line. Formula 1 offers Moncler a credible route into one of the most visible cultural and sporting ecosystems in the world.

The word that matters is credible. Plenty of fashion brands now turn up around F1. The ones that work don't look like they've bought their way in; they look like they belong.

## 3 · The idea

**Remember:** Not product in the paddock: a driver as a year-round cultural connector.

So the opportunity isn't simply to put product in the paddock. It's to use a driver as a year-round cultural connector, someone who brings Moncler Collection into travel, city life, race-week style and the personal moments around elite performance.

Think about what a driver's year actually looks like: twenty-four cities, constant travel, cold mornings at the track, evenings in the most photographed places in the world. That's a natural home for Moncler.

## 4 · The objective

**Remember:** Moncler Collection in global cultural moments, and in front of new communities.

This is the objective we've worked to: position Moncler Collection within globally relevant cultural and sporting moments, while introducing the brand to communities beyond its established client base.

The second half is the important part. F1 isn't just a stage for existing clients; it's a way to meet the next ones.

**Ask Moncler:** Is that the right objective? And which communities matter most to you right now: younger clients, particular markets, more women, more men?

## 5 · Why Formula One?

So, why F1, and why now?

## 6 · What F1 has become

**Remember:** Performance, fashion, travel, technology, entertainment, told social-first.

Formula 1 today sits across elite performance, fashion, travel, technology and entertainment, and it's told social-first.

That last point is the shift. The sport's growth has come from storytelling off the track as much as racing on it. The paddock arrival on a Thursday or a Sunday morning is now photographed and shared like a front row at a show. That's the moment a fashion house can own.

## 7 · Scale

**Remember:** 982 million fans, young and increasingly female, and 6.7 million people at the races.

The scale first. 982 million fans worldwide. 43% of them are under 35, and 42% are women. And 6.7 million people attended races across the season.

So F1 gives Moncler two things at once: global media scale, and physical premium moments in key cities. Few platforms combine both.

*Cue: the deck gives no source for the figures on slides 7 and 8. Get them from the deck's author before the meeting. If asked and you don't have them yet, say we'll send the sources afterwards; don't name one you haven't checked.*

## 8 · The audience

**Remember:** F1 fans over-index on everything luxury cares about, ahead of basketball and football.

This is the slide that matters most for a luxury house. It compares F1 fans with basketball and football fans. An index of 100 is the average person.

On personal interest in fashion, F1 scores 115.5, ahead of basketball at 112.8 and football at 106.5. On buying the premium version of products, F1 is at 132.6, a third above average, against 126.3 and 111.6. On paying more for quality, 107.7. And on being in the highest income group, 123.4, against 116.9 and 107.8.

Here's the insight. Basketball is usually seen as the fashion sport. On every one of these measures, F1 is ahead of it. That makes F1 the strongest sports platform for a luxury brand, not just a big one.

**Ask Moncler:** Does that match what you see in your own client data? Where are you strongest today, and where are you trying to grow?

## 9–11 · Five reasons it works for Moncler ★

**Remember:** Culture, a new luxury audience, always-on stories, global and local, and fans who welcome sponsors.

Five reasons, briefly.

Cultural relevance. Race weekends now work as international culture moments, well beyond the track: fashion, music, hospitality and nightlife.

A new luxury audience. A younger, increasingly gender-balanced fanbase lets Moncler reach the luxury clients of the future without diluting its premium positioning.

Always-on storytelling. Twenty-four races give you twenty-four content peaks, and a driver's travel, preparation and life off the track fill the gaps in between.

Global and local. One consistent brand idea worldwide, activated selectively around your priority markets, your stores and your cultural moments.

And commercial receptivity. In the 2025 Global F1 Fan Survey, 76% of fans said sponsors enhance the sport. That's unusual. F1 fans welcome partners, as long as the partnership feels authentic and adds something.

**Ask Moncler:** Which of your markets and stores would you want to activate around first?

## 12 · Why partner with an F1 driver?

Now, why a driver rather than just a team or a logo?

## 13 · A human point of view

**Remember:** A driver wears, interprets and narrates the brand; a logo can't.

A driver gives Moncler a recognisable human point of view inside Formula 1. Unlike a logo-led sponsorship, the right talent can wear, interpret and narrate the brand across racing, travel and culture. It turns a global platform into a personal, credible story.

## 14 · Authentic integration

**Remember:** Paddock arrivals, travel, client events, Fashion Weeks: moments that already happen.

A driver lets Moncler move beyond sponsorship and become part of F1's culture. Paddock arrivals, global travel, client events, Fashion Weeks: the product is seen in moments that already happen, and that already reflect Moncler's lifestyle and heritage. Nothing feels staged.

## 15 · Human storytelling

**Remember:** Ambition, resilience, excellence: the driver's values are Moncler's values.

Drivers embody ambition, resilience and excellence. Those are values at the heart of Moncler. A driver's personal journey turns Moncler's heritage, craftsmanship and performance DNA into stories people connect with emotionally.

## 16 · A 365-day platform

**Remember:** F1 is no longer just race weekends; a driver is visible all year.

F1 isn't confined to race weekends anymore. A driver gives you year-round moments: Grands Prix, training, travel, media appearances, Fashion Weeks, cultural events and retail activations. Continuous visibility, not twenty-four spikes.

## 17 · People connect with people

**Remember:** Consumers build relationships with people, not brands.

And the simplest point. People build relationships with people, not with brands. A driver gives Moncler a relatable, aspirational face that builds affinity and introduces the brand to audiences beyond its current clients.

**Ask Moncler:** How do you work with ambassadors today, and how do you judge whether one is working?

## 18 · Driver recommendations ★

**Remember:** Four profiles, from proven stars to future stars.

We've shortlisted four drivers. Think of them on a spectrum: two established names who bring recognition now, and two of the sport's young talents who bring a longer story and a chance to grow together.

*Cue: availability and any existing personal fashion partnerships still need to be checked for each driver. Say so if asked.*

## 19 · Carlos Sainz

**Remember:** Timeless excellence: proven, precise, understated.

Carlos Sainz represents timeless excellence. He's a proven race winner and one of the most respected professionals in F1, with precision, resilience and consistency, and an understated sophistication that feels naturally Moncler. His personal style, global appeal and real connection to travel and performance fit a brand whose quality transcends trends.

## 20 · Charles Leclerc

**Remember:** Where elite performance meets contemporary luxury culture.

Charles Leclerc sits where elite sporting performance meets contemporary luxury culture. He's one of F1's most recognisable figures, with a naturally elegant image and real fashion credibility. He moves easily between the racetrack, luxury events and editorial fashion, which reflects Moncler's blend of performance, innovation and elevated style.

## 21 · Arvid Lindblad

**Remember:** The future: young, multicultural, built for a long-term partnership.

Arvid Lindblad is about the future. He's one of the sport's most exciting young talents, and he represents ambition, innovation and the courage to challenge convention. His multicultural background and next-generation appeal give Moncler the chance to build a long-term partnership, connecting the brand's heritage with where sport and culture are heading.

## 22 · Oliver Bearman

**Remember:** Young, modern, natural on camera, with growing fashion relevance.

Oliver Bearman combines elite potential with an authentic, modern presence that resonates with younger audiences. His fast rise through motorsport reflects the determination and pursuit of excellence that define Moncler, and he's growing in fashion relevance and natural in front of the camera.

Here's the strategic choice across the four. Leclerc and Sainz bring recognition today. Lindblad and Bearman bring a story you build together, and the earlier a brand commits to rising talent, the more of that rise it owns. Some brands do both: one established face and one future one.

**Ask Moncler:** Which of these feels most like Moncler? And would you lean towards one face, or a pairing of established and emerging talent?

## 23 · Team recommendations

**Remember:** Three teams, three different deals: a winner, a newcomer, a comeback.

A driver can work on its own, but some brands want a team relationship as well, or instead. Here are three, each a very different kind of deal.

## 24 · McLaren

**Remember:** Buy into a winner with real cultural pull.

McLaren is at the top of the sport right now. It won the Constructors' Championship in 2024 and 2025, and Lando Norris won the 2025 Drivers' title. It has one of the most commercially active brands on the grid, a young, fashion-literate fanbase and a record of lifestyle and apparel tie-ins. Moncler would be linked to a winning team with real cultural pull.

## 25 · Audi

**Remember:** Founding luxury partner of a brand-new team.

Audi is a new team building its brand from scratch, so Moncler could come in as a founding luxury partner, not one name among many. European premium design and engineering, with an Alpine and technical heritage, fits Moncler Grenoble's performance outerwear very naturally.

## 26 · Williams

**Remember:** Heritage, a comeback story, and more say for Moncler.

Williams is a historic British team, founded in 1977, in a well-funded rebuild under team principal James Vowles, with Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon driving. It brings heritage and an underdog comeback story. The entry cost is lower than at a front-running team, so Moncler would get more visible placement and more say in how the partnership looks.

And note the link: Sainz drives for Williams. A Williams partnership and a Sainz ambassadorship could work as one story.

So the three choices are really three strategies: buy into a winner, build with a newcomer, or shape a comeback with more control.

**Ask Moncler:** Team, driver, or both? And which of those three strategies sounds most like how Moncler likes to partner?

## 27 · The close ★

**Remember:** A global platform at the intersection of performance, style and modern culture.

To bring it together. Formula 1 is a global platform to place Moncler Collection where performance, style and modern culture meet. Through the right driver, Moncler doesn't sponsor F1; it becomes part of how F1 looks and feels, all year.

Here's what we'd suggest from here.

First, your feedback on today: the idea, the drivers and the teams. Second, we check availability, existing commitments and indicative investment for your shortlist. Third, we come back with a tailored proposal built around your markets, your calendar and your objectives, ahead of the 2027 season.

**Ask Moncler:** Who else at Moncler should be part of the next conversation? And is there a moment in your calendar, a show or a store opening, that we should be building towards?

## Questions Moncler may ask, and how to answer

**"Isn't F1 too loud, too masculine, for Moncler?"**
That's the F1 of ten years ago. Today 42% of fans are women and 43% are under 35, and race weekends are fashion and culture moments in their own right. And because Moncler works through a driver, Moncler controls the tone: what he wears, where, and how it's shot.

**"Won't we just be one of many fashion brands in the paddock?"**
That's why the choice of driver matters. We'd pick talent without a competing luxury outerwear relationship, and we'd agree category exclusivity in the deal. At Audi, Moncler could be the founding luxury partner, not one name among many.

**"Why not just gift product and get the paddock photo?"**
Gifting gets you a photo. A partnership gets you a story: the driver's travel and preparation between races, the right to use the imagery in your campaigns and stores, and a calendar of moments planned together.

**"What does it cost?"**
It depends on the driver or team and the rights. Williams, for example, is a lower entry point than a front-running team. Once we know your shortlist and priorities, we'll come back with indicative ranges; we won't guess today.

**"How would we measure it?"**
Against your objective: reach and earned media from the paddock and travel moments, social engagement, traffic to stores in race cities, new clients, and sell-through of featured pieces. We'd agree the measures up front.

**"Which driver would you choose?"**
It depends on the objective. For recognition now, Leclerc or Sainz. For a long-term story that Moncler helps shape, Lindblad or Bearman. If you want both, pair one of each.

**"Where does the data come from?"**
The 76% is from the 2025 Global F1 Fan Survey. For the fanbase, audience and index figures, give the sources from the deck's author if you have them by then; if not, say we'll send them straight after the meeting.
